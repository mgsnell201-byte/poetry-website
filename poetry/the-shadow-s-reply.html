The Shadow's Reply

I was walking down Elm Street talking to myself as usual, you know how it is, asking whether it's better to be the thing or the shadow of the thing, when from somewhere behind me a voice answered, clear as a church bell, "Both are equally temporary." I stopped and turned around but there was only the usual cast of characters: a woman wrestling with grocery bags, a golden retriever who looked like he had opinions, and a row of mailboxes standing at attention. No one seemed to have noticed my private conversation with the afternoon air. Half a block later, still thinking about this as I passed the bakery where the air always smells like possibility and cinnamon, I asked myself why we spend so much time trying to be solid when we're mostly made of light and memory anyway, and again that voice, closer this time, whispered, "Because even shadows need somewhere to stand." That's when I noticed it— stretched out before me on the pavement, a perfect replica of my confusion, following my every step like a loyal dog who had finally learned English. It had been listening all along, my quiet companion, my silent twin, my own darkness given a voice. "Next time," it seemed to say, as I watched it dance across a cracked sidewalk, "just walk toward the sun. I'll handle the rest."
