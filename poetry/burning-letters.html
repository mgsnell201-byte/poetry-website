Burning Letters

I write your name in gasoline, a matchstick love—wild, unclean. Each spark a word I shouldn’t say, each flame a debt you’ll never pay. You taught my heart to beg and break, to gnash its teeth, to ache, to shake. Now look what’s left: this ashen tongue, a dirge of smoke where hymns were sung. I loved you ruthless. Loved you blind. Now fury festers, rots the rind of every promise sweet and low. You should have watched the embers glow. But tonight, the wind is soft and slow— it hums the tune I used to know. The fire’s gone, the scars remain, yet still the sky forgives the rain. So take this ache, this twisted grace, the ghost of us I can’t erase. I’ll light no pyre, just one small spark— enough to see you in the dark.
