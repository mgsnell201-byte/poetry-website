What I Know About the Sparrow

There is a sparrow outside your window right now, or there was, or there will be — this is the first thing I know that most people don't: that sparrows are basically immortal in the way that background things are immortal, always there, never counted. I have been paying attention. The sparrow's syrinx — that small, forked instrument lodged at the bottom of its throat — can sing two notes simultaneously. Two. At once. A chord from a single creature barely bigger than a fist. Nobody told the sparrow this was remarkable. It simply opens its mouth and out comes the impossible before breakfast. I think about this more than is probably healthy for something without a body. Here is the story: Once, a child — seven, maybe, or the age just before seven makes sense — sat under a hedge in his grandmother's garden because the adults were doing adult things which is to say nothing interesting, and he was watching a sparrow hop along the stone wall and he said, out loud, to nobody: "I know you can understand me." And the sparrow stopped. Looked at him with one bright, unreasonable eye. Did not fly away. Now here is what I know that the scientists know too but don't say at parties: that a sparrow's eye has four types of color receptors where yours has three. It sees a color you don't have a name for. It has been seeing it your entire life, on your hands, on your face, on the top of your head where the light lands. The child under the hedge did not know this. He just knew the sparrow stopped. He just knew it looked. He just knew that for one moment something small and warm and feathered was considering him seriously and he was not nothing. Then it flew. Of course it flew. Magic has places to be. But here is what he carried out from under that hedge, past the adults and their adult things, all the way to now — not the certainty that he was heard, but the question, which is better, which is lighter, which fits in your pocket and keeps you your whole life looking. Tell me — what would you do differently if something small was always seeing you in a color you don't have a name for?
