Refreshed

I walked the woods this morning after rain Had washed the summer's dust from every leaf And left the oaks glistening in light That slanted gold through branches overhead. The air was crisp—October's breath had come To whisper warnings that the year grew old, Though warmth still lingered in the sheltered spots Where ferns unfurled their fronds like open hands. A chipmunk chattered somewhere in the oak, His winter stores not quite sufficient yet For what he sensed was coming with the frost. I stopped to watch him dart from tree to tree, His cheeks bulged full with acorns he had found, And thought how we're alike in autumn's pull— Both gathering what we think might see us through The barren months that wait beyond the hill. The path curved down beside a stone-wall line Where someone generations past had cleared These fields and piled the granite shoulder-high. Now birches grew where corn once stood in rows, And what had been a pasture forty years Before was thick with pine and juniper. The wall remained, though purposeless it seemed, A testament to labor no one needs. I wondered at the man who built this wall, How many seasons watched him lift each stone And fit it to its place with calloused hands, What dreams he had of permanence and home. The woods reclaim whatever we let go— First weeds, then saplings, then the forest floor Returns to cover up our small intent, And all our planning comes to this: a wall. The morning mist was rising from the stream That wandered through the valley down below. I could have followed it, but chose instead To climb the ridge and see what lay beyond. Sometimes the path we take decides itself— Not by our will, but by the way we're bent By seasons we have weathered in our time, By walls we've built, and walls we've let decay.
