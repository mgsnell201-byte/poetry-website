Poem · No. 296

Take Me Instead

I am the hinge between hunger and hope, my body a comma in the forest's long sentence of survival. Behind me, folded like a prayer in ferns, my fawn breathes in whispered beats— each exhale a secret I would die to keep, a promise buried deep. But you, golden shadow, muscle wrapped in silence, you have found us with your amber mathematics, calculating the distance between want and taking, your patience breaking like dawn. So I step forward into the cathedral of your gaze, each hoof-fall a bell ringing: here, here, here— not my child, but me, not the hidden, but the free. Look at me. I make myself a constellation of motion, tail flagging white against the dark devotion of your hungry stare— shadow-patterns dancing on my spine like warnings, like prayers offered to the mourning air. I am the decoy moon pulling your tide toward me, away from the small sun sleeping in speckled fur, away from innocence that must not stir. See how I stumble—see how my leg trembles with imagined weakness, how I beg without words for your attention's weight, how I create this theater of fate. Your focus becomes a chain I wear, heavier than winter, sharper than despair. It presses against my ribs until my heart speaks in a language older than words: Take me instead. I am the sacrifice that walks on delicate legs, the offering that breathes with deliberate raggedness, drawing your predator-prayer away from innocence— my love a fierce defense. In this moment, I am not doe— I am pure attention made flesh and bone, a living target painted with love's geometry, a mother's desperate artistry. Behind me, my fawn stays hidden, a small god dreaming of grass and safety while I dance with death's golden eyes, while I weave these necessary lies. Knowing that sometimes the greatest gift we give is to be seen when someone else must live unseen, untouched by predator's desire— I draw you toward my willing fire. Watch me. Follow me. Let that be enough. Let love be enough.
