The Unsinkable Pugs

Upon the prow where once a girl Spread arms against the salt-cold air, And a boy behind her, steady, held The whole wide ocean in his care— Two pugs now stand, and she throws wide Her stubby paws to greet the gale, While he, pressed close against her side, Keeping her safe against the rail. "I'm flying!" barks the girl, no doubt, Though no one hears above the foam— The boy grunts back, a wheezy shout, "I've got you, Rose. I'll bring you home." But hush—remember what this steel Once carried through the April dark: The chandeliers, the waltz, the meal, The band that played upon the ark. Remember fifteen hundred names The freezing water would not keep, The lifeboats launched with half their frames, The unsinkable, gone down to sleep. The sea that night was black as coal, The stars indifferent and high, And every soul that lost its soul Beneath a North Atlantic sky. And yet—and yet—upon that rail, Two pugs, oblivious and round, Rehearse a love that cannot fail, The only flying to be found. She spreads her paws, a tiny queen Of all the wind and all the spray; He clings to her, a loyal thing, To keep his darling from the gray. No iceberg waits, no hull will tear, No orchestra need play them down— Just two small dogs with flattened hair, The happiest fools in all the town. So let the serious sea recall Its grief, its grandeur, and its cost, And let the pugs, above it all, Reenact the love we almost lost. For history keeps its heavy score, But love, too, must have its turn: Two pugs, one bow, one myth, one roar— "I'm flying, Jack!" — and still we learn That even on the edge of doom, Where water waits and steel gives way, A little love can fill the room, And two small dogs can save the day.
