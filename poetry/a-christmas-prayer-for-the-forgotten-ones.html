A Christmas Prayer for the Forgotten Ones

Look—the sparrow with the broken wing is still singing from the bare branch, its small body trembling in December wind. Who will notice it there, listing sideways, offering its ragged song to the cold sky? And what of the stray dog limping through the alley, its ribs showing, its eyes searching every face for one hint of kindness, one gentle word? Tell me, who has time to stop, to kneel down in the dirty snow, to touch the matted fur and say: you belong here too? Listen—the cat with the torn ear, the rabbit shivering in the roadside ditch, the old horse standing alone in the frozen field— each one is breathing, each one feels the sharp edge of this world, the ache of being forgotten, of being afraid. And yet, look how they persist, how the dog still wags its tail when a shadow passes, how the sparrow keeps singing its small prayer, how the horse lifts its gray head to the morning sun as if it still believes in blessing, as if it still trusts that somewhere there is a warm stall, clean water, someone's kind hand on its neck. What can we offer them this Christmas but our attention, our open hearts, our willingness to see them— not as problems to be solved, but as fellow travelers on this difficult journey, as creatures who know, just as we do, what it means to be cold, to be hungry, to wonder if anyone will come? You do not have to fix everything. You do not have to save them all. But you can pause in your rushing, you can meet the eyes of the dog in the alley, you can leave food for the stray cat, you can speak gently to the injured bird. Tell me, isn't this what prayer is— this noticing, this stopping, this bearing witness to the ones the world hurries past? And maybe this is the Christmas blessing, that we learn to see what we've been taught to ignore, that we practice the difficult art of caring for the broken-winged and the forgotten, for all the soft animals of the world who wait in the cold for someone to remember that they matter, that they belong to the family of things.
