Glass Brought Through

She carries it between two rooms of dusk, The glass inclined, the wine collecting west In crimson, rounded to the glass's rim But not beyond. Her thumb secures the base, Her finger riding on the slender stem To hold the liquid level as she steps Past the table's corner, across the rug's Worn field, toward the wingback chair and him. He let the pencil drop, the ledger close On columns that once tracked the shadow's arc Across the windowsill. He used to mark Each increment of afternoon, the way The light contracted, hour by measured hour, From mullion to the baseboard's molding, down The wall's plane to the floor. But now the sun Has slipped below the frame and he has stopped. The counting stopped, not the decline of light, Which yet proceeds along the wainscot's edge In increments no ledger now records, The shadow's transit that no pen can hold. What crosses through the darkening room to him Brings neither answer nor reprieve, but this: The glass brought whole through dusk, the wine that takes Within its curve the last of afternoon. She sets it on the chair's arm and withdraws. The wine yet trembles, ripples from the rim Traveling inward while the surface clears To one dark oval gathering the room: The ceiling's plaster, window's last red bar, The rug's worn figures, and the empty wall Where shadow meets the baseboard's ogee curve— Crimson on crimson, light on darkening wine.
