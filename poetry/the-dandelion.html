The Dandelion

I am watching a dandelion cross the yard, its seed head loose and pale as a held breath, riding a current I cannot see or name, though the grass bends slightly to mark its going. It passes the chair I dragged outside in April and never brought back in, the one with a cushion the rain has been slowly rewriting all summer. For a moment it hangs above the fence post, turning the way a thought turns when you almost remember someone's name, then lets the wind decide for it. I think of my father's hand closing around a stem exactly like this one, years before I was a fact in the world, his breath doing what the wind is doing now. By the time I stand to follow its path it has already crossed into the neighbor's yard, settling somewhere among the low weeds, none of us any wiser for watching. The chair sits empty behind me, its cushion darkening at the corners, holding the shape of no one in the shape it always holds.
