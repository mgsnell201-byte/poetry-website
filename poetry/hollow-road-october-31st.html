Hollow Road, October 31st

The post leans where the gravel gives out, gray as a tooth, one crow on top of it, and I stand a few feet off, hands loose, thinking of my grandmother’s kitchen, her bird on a fence means somebody’s missing you, all that sweet old-country talk I used to smile at over cornbread. Woodsmoke and wet leaves. No lamp for a mile. The moon does what it wants with the sign. In daylight, HALLOWEEN is paint, a joke somebody’s cousin made. Tonight the letters have gone thin and wrong, the L’s stretched too tall, the whole word breathing a faint green like something held under creek water. The arrow points north. Past the ridge, past the last porch light, where the road turns to a rumor, and the crow has turned its head that way too, as if it’s been listening to something up there since before I came. Kaaah. The hollow takes it and hands it back thinner. Kaaah. My breath hangs white in front of me. I reach in my coat for a lighter, a key, anything, and find only the cold lining. Kaaah. Now the left. Any bird would go left now. That’s how it was told. That’s the whole of the telling. The crow stays. It folds itself smaller on the post, one black eye holding the moon, and I hear myself say it anyway, low, the way you’d speak to a dog you don’t know: carry it kind and far. Nothing moves. Even the leaves wait. I step back once, then again, and the arrow stays pointed at the dark, and the bird stays pointed at me.
