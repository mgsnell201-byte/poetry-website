Go Seek

With small hands pressed over your eyes, you counted slowly— footsteps scattered like leaves, then silence. You search through gardens, closets, shadowed halls where laughter echoed through summer dusk, each discovery a burst of joy blooming again. Every hiding place became sacred: behind curtains, underneath stairs, where breathing slowed to whispers, heartbeats stilled in perfect stillness. But the game grew subtle— seasons turned, no longer bound by childhood's simple rules. You learned to hide from questions, glances, touch. Now they call your name across crowded squares, but corners hold you fast with quiet arms. These spaces, small enough for one alone, have taught you silence as their only gift. Yet somewhere— in this vast and peopled earth— another player moves through shadow-work, their footsteps soft on paths you recognize, breathing matched to rhythms you have always known. Until that moment when someone finds your corner, kneels down beside you in the quiet space, and suddenly the hiding feels too small for hearts that finally dare to be complete.
