Here Lies Me

I've been imagining my headstone for weeks now, and in every version, there's a typo— "Here Lays Me" or maybe "Hear Lies Me," the kind of mistake that would make my English teacher rise from her own grave just to shake her head. It's not that I think death will be careless, more that I was careless in life, the sort of person who wrote "definately" well into his thirties, who could never remember if it was "affect" or "effect" that changed the outcome. I picture tourists in the cemetery, pointing at my stone with a kind of glee, taking photos to text their friends— "Look at this poor guy, couldn't even spell his way out of existence properly." Maybe they'll think it was intentional, some kind of postmodern commentary, me making a statement about the fluidity of language from my permanent position in the ground, when really it was just Harold at the monument shop having a bad day with his chisel. "Here Lies Me, Husband and Farther"— that's the one I dread most, being called a farther, as if I spent my whole life moving away from things, which, to be fair, isn't entirely inaccurate given my history with commitment and vegetables. Or worse: "Beloved Son and Spelling Bee Runnerup," immortalizing that moment in fourth grade when I forgot the second 'c' in "broccoli" and had to sit down in front of everyone, already practicing for this final sitting down. The truth is, I'd probably forgive the error— I forgave myself for so many small mistakes, for burnt toast and missed exits, for conversations where I said exactly the wrong thing and had to live with it for decades after. So let them carve "Here Lies Me" into the granite, grammatically questionable, technically incorrect, a fitting monument to someone who spent his life trying to get things approximately right, landing somewhere in the vicinity of truth, close enough to be understood, which is all any of us can really hope for when we're trying to explain ourselves to strangers standing in the sun, squinting at our particulars, wondering what we meant, and whether we meant well.
