The Architecture of Almost

We build with air, with breath, with sound— each "soon" a brick, each "fine" a stone, until the house is thick with walls where every door is left unthrown. "Love you," mortar, cheap and quick, "Let’s talk soon," the lintel’s trick. "How are you?" the rotted sill that sags beneath the weight of still— still waiting, still believing the ghost of meaning lives here, grieving in rooms where every "I’m okay" paints shadows where the windows were. We are the architects of absence, drafting halls of acquiescence: "No, go ahead," (though go means stay), "It’s nothing," (swallowed whole as day devours stars it swore to keep. We name this kindness. Call it sleep. But listen—how the structure sighs when wind blows through its "not goodbyes." The house will stand until it doesn’t. The heart will bear until it won’t. And when at last the roof gives way, we’ll whisper "just another day" into the dust where meaning fled— not with thunder, but with "said."
