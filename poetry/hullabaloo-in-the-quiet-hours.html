Hullabaloo in the Quiet Hours

I want to tell you about the bear I saw last Tuesday, ambling through the produce section in a floral sundress, the kind with tiny buttons down the back that no bear could possibly fasten alone. This was Baloo, naturally, because what other bear would pair a hulla—that flowing Arabic garment— with such magnificent nonchalance, reaching one enormous paw toward the honeydew melons? The cashiers barely looked up from their registers. The manager continued stacking cans of soup. Here was hullabaloo in its truest form— not the commotion you'd expect, but its opposite, a sloth bear humming something low and drowsy, some ancient lullaby about moonlight and mangoes, while shoppers wheeled their carts around him as if bears in dresses were simply part of Tuesday. I suppose this is what it means to break a word apart— to find the hulla nestled against the baloo, the fabric against the fur, the chaos transformed into the gentlest kind of song, the kind that asks nothing of you except to notice how a bear can wear his strangeness with more grace than most of us manage in our sensible shoes and matching socks. He paid in exact change, I should mention, counting out quarters with surprising delicacy, then shuffled toward the automatic doors still humming, still absolutely himself, leaving behind only the faint scent of honey and the distant notion that maybe all our hullabaloo is just waiting to become a lullaby.
