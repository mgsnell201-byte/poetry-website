Where Have All the Flowers Gone (Reprise)

I remember the Kingston Trio asking "where all the flowers gone," which seemed like a reasonable question at the time, though a bit agricultural for my taste. But lately I've been wondering about something less botanical— where did all the compassion go? Not that I had it marked on a map, a little red pin stuck in the heart of some midwestern state, with a sign that said "Compassion: Exit Here." Maybe it's in the back of a closet somewhere with the fondue set and the bread machine, things we were excited about once and then forgot we owned. Or perhaps it slipped between the couch cushions along with seventeen cents and a broken watch, waiting to be found during the next archeological dig we call spring cleaning. I ask the internet where compassion went and it shows me fourteen articles about why I'm asking the question wrong, three videos of cats doing adorable things, and an ad for noise-canceling headphones, which feels about right. My neighbor tells me compassion moved away, couldn't afford the rent anymore. The guy at the coffee shop thinks it got a better offer in Canada. My mother insists it was never really here, that I'm romanticizing the past, which is probably true. But I could swear I saw it once, back when we used to look at each other instead of through each other, back when someone else's suffering felt like an invitation to help rather than an inconvenience to scroll past. The Kingston Trio sang about flowers picked by young girls, gone to young men, gone to soldiers, gone to graveyards, the whole beautiful terrible circle of it. But where does compassion go when it leaves the room? Does it die like a flower? Does it wait in the graveyard for us to come back and claim it? Or is it here all along, buried under our to-do lists and our arguments about whether we should even have to care, waiting for someone— maybe you, maybe me— to finally bend down, brush off the dust, and remember what it was for.
