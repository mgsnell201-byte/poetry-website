The Visiting Professor

I like to think of hate as a substitute teacher who shows up one morning unannounced, wearing a rumpled suit and carrying a briefcase full of mimeographed handouts from 1952. The regular teacher—let's call her compassion— is out sick, or attending a conference, or simply exhausted and sleeping late, so hate takes attendance in her absence, calling out names with a slight mispronunciation that makes everyone feel a little less themselves. The lesson plan is simple: here is the world divided into us and them, the deserving and the rest. Most students sit through the period doodling in their margins, watching the clock, knowing this isn't the real curriculum, that tomorrow the regular teacher will return with her usual assignments about listening and the difficult grammar of forgiveness. They file out when the bell rings, blinking in the hallway's fluorescent light, already forgetting. But there are always a few—you've seen them— who copy every word into their notebooks, who raise their hands for clarification, who stay after class to ask if hate will be back tomorrow, and the next day, and could they possibly get independent study credit because they've never had a teacher who made so much sense, who finally explained why they feel the way they do. And hate, adjusting his glasses, smiling, says of course, of course, pulls up a desk, and begins the lesson again from the beginning.
