The Matchstick and the Snowflake (An Exercise in Thermal Incompatibility)

A Matchstick sat upon a shelf, a fellow rather fond of friction, Who considered spontaneous combustion not a danger but a personal conviction. A Snowflake floated through the room, all delicate, white and airy, And the Matchstick thought her quite divine, though chemically somewhat contrary. "Good evening," said the Matchstick, "I am pleased to make your acquaintance, Though I fear my natural disposition may require a certain degree of maintenance." The Snowflake blushed—or would have blushed, had blushing been within her constitution, And replied, "Your warmth is most attractive, but presents a problem of dissolution." "I'd gladly give my heart to you," he cried, "and set the world aglow!" "That's very kind," the Snowflake said, "but I'd prefer you didn't, though." They spoke of love and destiny, of thermodynamics and fate, And whether mutual admiration could survive a change of state. And so they parted, sweet and sad, their passion left unmet, For love may conquer many things, But someone always ends up wet.
