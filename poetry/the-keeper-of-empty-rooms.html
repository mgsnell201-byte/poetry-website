The Keeper of Empty Rooms

I built a hotel in my chest where all the leaving things go. Check-in is easy— no ID, no questions, just the weight of their shadow spilling across the front desk. Some stay for minutes, some for decades, gnawing on the minibar of my ribs, ordering room service with a voice like frost on glass. I change the sheets every morning, fluff the pillows of my lungs, scrub the bathroom tiles until they shine like unclaimed promises. The manager says a full house weaves its own devotion. The night auditor swears ghosts tip better than the living. I keep the lights on and the doors unlocked, not because I believe in second chances— but because I’ve tasted the dust of abandonment and prefer the ache of a full house.
