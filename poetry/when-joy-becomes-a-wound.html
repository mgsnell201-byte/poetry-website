When Joy Becomes A Wound

She wears her smile like armor made of glass— Transparent to the world, yet sharp enough To cut those who dare look too close, And slice herself when the pretending's rough. "I'm fine," she says, the words a practiced prayer, A mantra worn smooth by repetition's weight. But fine is just another word for drowning While standing perfectly still at heaven's gate. Behind the brilliant curve of painted lips Lives grief that has no name, no place to rest— The kind of pain that eats you from within While you smile wider at each small request. The mother asks, "How was your day, my dear?" The friend inquires, "You seem a little off." She lifts the corners of her mouth like flags Of surrender, soft but strong enough. Each laugh becomes a tomb for silent screams, Each "How are you?" a test she's learned to pass. The mirror knows the truth she'll never tell: That smiles can be the cruelest kind of mask. At 3 AM, when darkness claims the night, The smile dissolves like sugar in the rain. She sits alone with all she cannot say, With all the weight of unwitnessed pain. But what if someone saw beyond the light? What if the question came without conditions? What if we learned to love the broken parts And hold space for authentic admissions? The bravest souls are those who dare to crack, Who let their pain have voice, have form, have place— Who understand that healing starts the moment When we cease performing joy and show our face.
