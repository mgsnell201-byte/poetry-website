The River That Builds and Destroys

The River That Builds It starts as questions, small and bright, That spark in minds by candlelight— Each answer weaves a thread of lore, Each query opens one more door To truths that time cannot ignore. The child who asks "but why?" today Becomes the one who lights the way— From Galileo's stars to freedom's rings, From marches claiming equal things, They build the world that freedom brings. Yet even here, a shadow creeps— Curiosity, unchecked, can leap Too far, crafting tools too vast, Like splitting atoms in the past, Whose power heals, or burns too fast. The River That Destroys It starts with fear, a whispered claim, That seeks a face to pin the blame— The stranger's voice, the different creed, A scapegoat for a growing need To crush what stirs our inner greed. From whispers grow the urgent cries, Like Salem's trials or Red Scare lies, Till crowds converge with blinded aim, And reason falters in the flame, Yet hope may flicker through the blame. Yet even here, a spark of care— A doubt that questions what's unfair— Can twist toward light, though frail and small, Like voices breaking Berlin's wall, Where hope and ruin rise and fall. The Choice Both rivers rise from one deep spring: The human heart's unending spring. They escalate where motives blend— No path is pure, no stream's sure end, Yet still we choose which tide to tend. The fertile plain or storm's cruel wrath? Each step we take defines the path. When pain tempts hate to steer the flow, Love fights to pull us from below— A struggle carved in every woe. No easy choice, no certain gain, For human hearts bear both love and pain. Each step we take, the tide will swell, To shape a world we cannot tell— Its rise or fall, no heart can spell.
