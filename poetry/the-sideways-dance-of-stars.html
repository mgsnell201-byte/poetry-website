The Sideways Dance of Stars

Old Cancer clicks his claws at dusk— not dawn—where tides exhale, and Scorpio’s tail, long-rusted now, still writes in salt and shale. The starfish sing in minor keys of ships that fled their trail, while constellations, drunk on time, stumble through their tale. The Crab-Kings rode on liquid light, their armies barnacle-born, till Aries came, a wildfire ram, and cracked them hoof by horn. Now Gemini, those twin-tongued jesters, mimic battles gone, and Virgo counts the broken shields but keeps the tally wrong. O Leo, blind in furnace gold, O Libra’s balanced blade— the crab knows weight is just a wave that bows things toward the shade. The moon-child reels, her pirouettes now salt, now sickle, now spade, and every throne the tides erect is sand, is script, is scade. So let Sagittarius nock his dawn, let Capricorn climb stone— the sea will have its skeletons, the sky its stolen throne. For what’s a kingdom but a glint? A sideways glance? A groan? The stars dance on, and none remain to name what they’ve outgrown.
