Poem · No. 104

The Argument from Clarity

He trusts in facts: each measured, weighed, sorted by use, filed in neat rows. His world stands square, its corners true, no room for what the shadow knows. I walk the older path, where briar guards the threshold, where the thread runs out through fingers, leads me down to what the rational mind calls dead. He says my truths are broken images, myths the ancients used to tame their ignorance. I say his clarity is just a smaller kind of claim— a skein stretched tight across the dark, pretending there's no dark beneath. My web, at least, admits the gaps, the maze that winds through fact and faith. We meet at dusk, that border hour, and neither speaks. The briar stirs. His certainty, my doubt—both thorns. Both bleed. And neither of us errs.
