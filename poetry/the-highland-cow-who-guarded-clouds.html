The Highland Cow Who Guarded Clouds

A shaggy cow of clan and cloud, she hums through Highland wind and sun, her fiery coat proclaims out loud that winter’s work is nearly done. At dusk a ghostly sister gleams, a snow-white wisp of sith-born grace, she drifts from mist and feeds on dreams, then helps a farmer mend his place. With solemn, star-black, stubborn stare, a bull conducts the hills like choirs, their hooves keep time in frosty air, their horns hang halos, hooked with wires. Saints bless the kids who brush her tail, “Care well,” they say, “and you will thrive,” she grants them cream in every pail, the wild old ways kept half-alive. Now tourists snap her gentle grin, our hairy hilltop mascot, wow— they call her “fluffalo of tin,” but she just guards the sky. And cows.
