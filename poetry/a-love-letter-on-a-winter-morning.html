A Love Letter on a Winter Morning

The frost has etched its script on windowpanes, A cold that grips the heart and clouds the breath. I hold the letter, frail as winter's grace, Its ink a wound that bleeds beneath my touch. The snow falls soft, a shroud on buried hopes, Each flake a fleeting thought of what was lost. My anger stirs, a ember in the chill, Yet sorrow binds it like a chain of ice. This page, once warm with promises, now fades, Its words a ghost that haunts the morning's gloom. I trace the lines where love and rage entwined, And wonder if the fault was hers or mine. The dawn is gray, no sun to thaw the soul, The letter trembles in my tightened grip. What use is wrath when grief outlives its flame? The cold endures; the heart learns to forget. I fold the note and let the silence speak, Its quiet deeper than my heart's lament. The winter morning holds me in its thrall, And sorrow carves its name where anger falls.
