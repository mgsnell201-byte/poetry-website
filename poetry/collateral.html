Collateral

The envelope’s white window holds a threat, a final number, bold and overdrawn. A sigh deflates the hollow in my chest— this constant debt I cannot settle on. But deeper still, the ledger of the soul shows greater costs, a more disastrous toll. My confidence, a bankrupt company, its assets sold for pennies on the dollar. The interest on my grief compounds in me, a weight that makes my shaking spirit smaller. I paid hope forward in a risky loan, and now I am left utterly alone. The currency of joy has crashed and failed; my emotional reserves are drained and dry. Against my name, success has been derailed, repossessed by some indifferent sky. The cost of living? More than I can bear— a tax of constant, low-grade, silent care. So let the bank call this a failure. Fine. This brokenness is more than a closed account. It is a foreclosure on a design for light, for love, for any kind of amount. The true cost is the sound of your own voice, whispering you have no other choice. Yet in this ruin, where all funds are spent, a stubborn, priceless currency remains: the will to find what wasn't meant for balance sheets or for a banker's gains. The cost was high, the brokenness is real, but even shattered glass can spark and heal.
