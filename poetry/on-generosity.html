On Generosity

A colleague whose work I have always admired — and I mean that in the way one admires a very clean kitchen, everything in its place, nothing left out that might spoil overnight — stopped me after the reading to say how much he had enjoyed my new poems, particularly the accessibility of them, which is the kind of compliment that takes a few days to fully open, like one of those Japanese paper flowers you drop in water and then walk away from. I thanked him warmly and meant it, the way you mean it when someone holds a door just slightly longer than necessary, so you find yourself half-jogging across a lobby you had planned to cross at your own pace. He has a gift, my colleague, for a certain kind of clarity — his poems arrive like invoices, everything itemized, nothing left owing, and I have always thought there was real courage in that kind of plainness, the courage of a man who paints a room white and stands back satisfied, without once wondering what the room might have looked like in a different light, with the windows open, in the middle of a different life. I think about him sometimes when the rain is doing something interesting on the glass.
