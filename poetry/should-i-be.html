Should I Be

You mutter “thank you” as the coffee slides across the counter, its warmth seeping through the cup like a secret you didn’t ask for. “No worries,” she says, her words light as a feather brushing the morning air, and you’re left standing there, wondering if you should be— worried, that is. The room hums with the clink of spoons, the hiss of the espresso machine, and you think, what if she’s right? What if the world’s just a page in a book you forgot to finish, its plot unraveling in the steam rising from your coffee? A mouse of doubt scurries through the labyrinth of your thoughts, nibbling at the edges of calm. You sip, and the bitterness stings, a playful jab from the universe, as if to say, “Relax, but not too much.” She’s already wiping down the counter, her apron a flag of nonchalance, while you sit by the window, watching the light bend through the glass, wondering if worry is just a lanyard you wove to tether yourself to a morning that doesn’t care. And here’s the catch— what if “no worries” is a riddle, not a promise? What if you’re the one writing this story in your head, chuckling at the thought that a simple cup of coffee might be the only thing keeping you from asking if you should be worried about not being worried at all?
