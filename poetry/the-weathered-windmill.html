Poem · No. 415

The Weathered Windmill

Against a saffron twilight glow, A windmill sags, its blades half-gone, Where endless seasons ebb and flow, Through hail, through heat, through dusk till dawn. Its wood, now bleached to ghostly gray, Still reaches toward the waning light, Though time has gnawed its strength away, It stands unbowed through coming night. What whispers might its splinters share, Of reapers’ dreams and ripened grain? Of summers thick with humid air, Of barren years that brought no rain? Perhaps it spun as kinfolk laughed, Then slowed as footsteps wandered far, As battles scarred the land they graft, As vows were sealed beneath a star. With every strained and creaking sweep, It hums to skies in amber dressed, “Though time my sails in tatters keep, My spirit turns, by storms caressed.” I linger on its battered grace, Where evening paints a tender sheen, And see within its cracks a trace A beauty deeper than serene. For in its stance, I find my own, A strength to stand though faults unwind, To meet the gusts that I’ve outgrown, And let my scars with truth align. Yet as the light fades from the hills, A truth shines clear as day grows dim This windmill turns not of its will, But shows which way the wind has been.
