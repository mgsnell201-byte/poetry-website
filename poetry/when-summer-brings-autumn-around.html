When Summer Brings Autumn Around

I want to tell you about the afternoon when summer finally introduced me to autumn, not at some grand party with falling leaves and pumpkin spice everything, but on a Tuesday, in my backyard, while I was reading the same paragraph for the third time. Summer came over first, of course, still wearing her bright sundress, her hair smelling of chlorine and cut grass, and she said, You'll like my friend— she's quieter than me, drinks tea instead of lemonade, prefers cardigans to tank tops. And there was autumn, standing by the fence, her hands in the pockets of a jacket I didn't know I'd been missing, looking at the tomatoes on the vine the way you look at old photographs, with a mixture of affection and goodbye. Summer did most of the talking, naturally, telling stories about our long days together, the 6 a.m. light, the 9 p.m. dinners on the deck, while autumn just smiled and nodded, occasionally pointing out how the evenings were coming earlier now, softer somehow, wrapping themselves around the neighborhood like a shawl someone places on your shoulders when you didn't realize you were cold. I found myself leaning toward autumn's voice, the way she spoke about the changing light, about harvests and wool blankets, about the pleasure of going inward after months of staying out. Summer didn't seem hurt, exactly. She knew this was how it worked. She'd be back, she said, waving as she left, leaving her sunglasses on my patio table as a promise, a deposit on next June. But autumn stayed, settling into the chair beside me, picking up a book of her own, and we sat there together in the gentle afternoon, the sky that particular shade of blue that only exists in these in-between days, when one friend is waving goodbye and another is just saying hello.
