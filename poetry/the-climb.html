The Climb

An old man climbs the building's concrete steps, His shaking hand grips rails of rusted steel, Each stride a tax on joints that creak and bend, Yet he pursues a form that time won't break. Through stairwell glass he sees a city pond, Where one pale swan drifts quiet in the dusk, Its wings too weak to lift it from the pond— The swan he watched here fifty years ago. What game is this, that skin must sag and tire, Yet mind, a spinning flare, would chase the stars? He looks down at the swan, then past the streets, And finds what time has failed to take away: His daughter's face, the theorems he once proved, Still burn as bright as neon in the dark. Will he, grown old, reach out for such fixed forms, Or grin at time's cruel trick, its stubborn trap? He pauses on the landing, breathing hard, But climbs another flight toward the roof. The body fails. The knees give out. The heart Drums warnings that he's learning to ignore. Yet in this weathered frame, he weaves a spell— Each memory a thread that will not fray. A bird's faint call through city smog breaks free, One sparrow singing over traffic's roar. He thinks: this stubborn song against the noise— It mirrors what persists inside his mind. The building's height cuts through the urban dusk, Each step a choice to climb instead of rest, Each thought a rung to lift above the dark, Each word a light—his own—that will not dim. He reaches for the rooftop door at last, Where sky meets steel, where city glows below, And stands above the ground in endless night, Still reaching, still alive, refusing stone.
