Is Chaos Chaotic?

The question keeps me up at night, though Chaos himself sleeps soundly in the armchair, snoring with perfect regularity, his breath keeping time like a metronome. I've been watching him for years now, taking notes in the margins of my life, and I'm starting to suspect he's not what his name suggests. Yes, he knocks things over— the vase, my plans for Tuesday, the carefully constructed arguments I make about the nature of order— but always at precisely the moment I need them knocked over. He arrives uninvited to dinner parties but somehow improves them, spilling red wine in patterns that look like constellations, asking the questions everyone was thinking but too polite to voice. I used to think he was random, a force of pure disorder loose in my living room, but now I see the way he arranges disasters with the care of a florist composing a bouquet. Last week he hid my car keys which made me walk to the store where I ran into an old friend I'd been meaning to call for months. Yesterday he erased the document I was working on, and I rewrote it from memory— better this time, clearer, as if losing it had been necessary all along. I'm beginning to understand that Chaos might be the most orderly thing in my life, that his disruptions follow patterns I'm only now learning to read, like a language I've been speaking without knowing the grammar. Maybe chaos is just order that hasn't revealed its structure yet, the way a seed looks like nothing before it understands it's meant to be a tree. Or maybe I'm overthinking this, and Chaos is just sitting in that chair, grinning at my attempts to make sense of him, pleased that I'm finally asking the right question— not what is chaos, but who, and whether the difference between disorder and design is really just a matter of how far back you stand.
