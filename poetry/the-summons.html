The Summons

She came at four, before the light, and stood beside the bed. I knew her by the cold she brought. "Get up," was all she said. I asked her for an hour more. She pulled the blanket back. I asked her what the work would be. She handed me the dark. The neighbors rose at seven-thirty, drove their children south, returned at five with groceries, and kept their winters out. I envied them. I told her so. She looked at me and smiled— the way a falcon eyes a field before it has been tilled. I've sat here since before the frost. The page has cost me much: two friendships and a decent sleep and most of what I touch. She does not praise. She does not mourn what falls along the way. She only asks if what I've made is true enough to stay. Some mornings it is. Those are hers. The rest belong to doubt. She leaves without a word both times. The candle carries out.
