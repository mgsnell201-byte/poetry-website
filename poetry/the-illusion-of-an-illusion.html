The Illusion of an Illusion

This morning I caught myself pretending to be too busy to call my mother, which was odd because I wasn't actually busy— I was just sitting here eating cereal and scrolling through pictures of other people pretending to be happy at brunch. So I was faking being busy to avoid admitting I was faking being busy, which is like wearing a disguise to a costume party where everyone's already in disguise, and suddenly you're the most honest person in the room by being twice as fake. You can see the problem. My neighbor waves at me every morning and I wave back, both of us performing the theater of neighborliness, except yesterday I realized he might actually like me, which means I've been faking a fake friendship that turned out to be real when I wasn't looking, like those times you practice a conversation in your head and then the person says exactly what you imagined and you have to pretend you're hearing it for the first time. The dog, at least, seems genuine when he wags his tail, though I suspect even he is performing some ancient contract between man and wolf, pretending to love me for the kibble, except now the pretending has gone on so long that it's become the truth, which might be the secret the dog knows that I'm still trying to learn: that if you fake something perfectly enough and long enough, eventually the mask grows into your face, and you wake up one day to discover you've become the person you were only trying to look like, standing in your kitchen, genuinely enjoying your cereal, for real this time, or close enough that the difference no longer matters.
