Two Silhouettes in the Dark

I was thinking about silhouettes the other day, how they are really just the outlines of bodies who have stepped between a light source and a wall, and how two of them standing in the dark are actually four things at once— two actual people with their complicated insides, their grocery lists and lower back pain, and two flat black cutouts that belong to shadow puppetry and the opening credits of Alfred Hitchcock films. It's the kind of thing that makes you wonder if we are all just interruptions of light, brief obstacles in the path of photons that have traveled ninety-three million miles only to be stopped by your torso or the back of your head. And then there's the question of what the two silhouettes are doing in the dark in the first place— are they looking at each other or away? Are they about to embrace or just happened to be standing near each other when the lights went out, the way strangers do in an elevator when the power fails, suddenly aware of breathing, the small sounds of existence in close quarters? I like to think they are philosophical silhouettes, pondering their own flatness, how they have been reduced to edges, all their inner complexity—kidneys, memories, the ability to whistle—completely irrelevant to their current two-dimensional predicament. Maybe one silhouette is saying to the other, "Isn't it strange that we are most visible when we are least ourselves?" And the other silhouette, being equally philosophical, might reply, "Yes, but at least in the dark no one can see us blushing about it."
