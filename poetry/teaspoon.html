Teaspoon

She swept the breadcrumbs from the cutting board into the cup of her palm, the dough she'd punched down still rising in the bowl under the damp towel. He gripped the doorframe, knuckles bloodless on the jamb, jaw set tight, the phone receiver swinging on its cord. She wiped her hands on her apron, crossed the kitchen in sock feet, the gray ones with the patched heel, stepped close enough that he could hear her breathe, close enough that the warmth off the stove reached him. She didn't speak. Just let her mouth soften, the corners lifting— a smile so slight it could fit in a teaspoon. The cat on the third stair blinked once, whiskers catching the late afternoon light. Steam rising between his fingers— and the dough kept rising
