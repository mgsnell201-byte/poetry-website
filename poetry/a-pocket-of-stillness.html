A Pocket of Stillness

The granite shoulder wakes first, a single vein of quartz catching the early flame, gleaming like hidden bone remembering the sun. I stand in the hush of the valley floor where pine needles stitch the earth in copper thread, and the air itself feels newly born— cool, sharp, tasting of snowmelt and stone. The mountain does not hurry. It leans into the light the way an old friend leans into silence, letting it settle across its ridged brow. Yet as the sun climbs, color loosens from the rock— rose into lavender into a blue with no name— and the ridges begin their slow conversation with the sky. I am small here, smaller than the lichen's quiet patience, yet the mountain makes room for me— a pocket of stillness shaped exactly to the curve of one wondering heart. But then the wind arrives, gentle as forgiveness, and the aspens answer with their thousand silver tongues. Light pours through them in sudden shafts, holy and unasked for, turning ordinary air into something it has no name for either. And I understand nothing completely, only that the stone has been dreaming for ten thousand years, and I have been allowed to stand inside its dream. The peaks keep their ancient vigil, carrying the horizon on their shoulders as if it were the lightest thing— a blue promise they have never broken. I turn my face upward, and the mountain turns with me.
