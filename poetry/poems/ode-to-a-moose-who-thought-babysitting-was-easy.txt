Ode to a Moose Who Thought Babysitting Was Easy

There once was a moose named Matilda the Magnificent,
Who thought babysitting twelve ducklings would be insignificant.
"How hard can it be?" she said with a snort,
"I'm enormous and they're the teeny-tiny sort!"

But ducklings, as everyone ought to know,
Are not the type to sit in a neat little row.
They splash and they dash and they make quite a hash,
Of everything tidy—in one mighty flash!

They slid down the banister, honking with glee,
While Matilda bellowed, "Come back here to me!"
But ducklings don't listen when fun is affoot,
They just keep on waddling—giving no hoot!

For when evening arrived and the chaos subsided,
Twelve sleepy ducklings around her resided.
And Matilda discovered, much to her surprise,
That chaos and love often wear the same disguise.

So if someone asks you to watch a small duck,
Remember Matilda and trust in your luck.
But if they ask you to watch a whole dozen,
Well, you might want to call up a cousin!
