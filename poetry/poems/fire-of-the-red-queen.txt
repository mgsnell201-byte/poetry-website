Fire of the Red Queen

In the lands of the Iceni,
Where the marshes meet the sky,
Lived a queen of iron spirit,
With fire in her eyes.

Red her hair as autumn heather,
Tall as standing stones,
She had kept her people's honor,
When the Romans claimed their home.

They had taken what was sacred,
Broken every oath.

But from ash an ember rises,
Rage that will not sleep,
And a mother's blood and fury,
Is a wound too vast to weep.

She braided fire in her hair,
She painted war upon her face,
She looked across the killing fields,
And wept for all her stolen grace.

She was born from storm and sorrow,
She was made from iron and flame,
Raise the spear to Andraste,
Let the earth cry out her name.

She drank the poison,
Rather than be led in chains,
It is said she whispered Andraste's
Name against the pouring rain.

They carved her name in no stone tablet,
Built her no triumphant arch,
But every red-haired daughter of this island,
Still walks proud upon her march.
