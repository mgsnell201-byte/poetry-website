Reflection

The lake this morning holds two faces—

mine looking down, and the one

the water makes of sky. Both shimmer,

neither quite true. I think how the word itself

does this: turns inward to mean thought

folding back on thought, then outward

to mean light returning from a surface

it cannot enter.



I remember my father taking me fishing once

at a pond so still it looked like a photograph of itself.

He pointed at the dragonflies touching down,

said, "Look at that," as the whole sky shivered—

which is what fathers say

when they don't have the words for beauty.



I wanted to ask him if he saw his own face down there too,

floating among the lily pads,

but I was eight and didn't know how to talk

about the strange way water makes you see yourself

as part of the landscape, your features

mingling with clouds and branches.



Now I understand he must have felt it too—

the lake doing what the mind does,

taking in the world each morning

in the truck on the way to work, sending it back

slightly altered, tinted with worry and wonder.



The window does this too. Right now

my transparent face is superimposed on the aspen,

so that I seem to be sprouting leaves from my ears.

It's the kind of thing that makes you consider

how thinking is just a more complicated version

of reflection—light hitting the surface of consciousness

and returning, carrying some image of what it touched.



Maybe that's why we use the same word for both:

the mind and the lake are doing the same work,

receiving what comes to them and offering it back

with their own particular slant, their own

quiet commentary on what they've been given.



I think about this as I watch the tree

sway slightly in the breeze, my face swaying with it,

and I feel a tenderness for the word itself,

for the way it holds both meanings so easily,

as if it always knew they were the same thing—

the world returning to us, and us

returning to ourselves, both of us changed

by the meeting, both of us grateful

for the surface that made it possible.
