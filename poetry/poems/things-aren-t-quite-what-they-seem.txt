Things Aren't Quite What They Seem

There once was a pigeon named Pete,
Who thought he had hooves on his feet.
He’d gallop and neigh,
In a very bird way,
And prance down the bustling street.

He’d strut with a confident flair,
With feathers instead of fine hair.
He’d race with the breeze,
And perch in the trees,
Pretending he didn’t have a care.

He’d try to eat hay from a trough,
But seeds were his favorite stuff.
He’d whinny and coo,
Confusing the zoo,
And making the zookeepers laugh.

One day, a wise owl did say,
“Dear Pete, you’re a bird, not a bay.
Embrace who you are,
A pigeon, a star,
And soar in your own special way.”

So Pete, with a flap and a cheer,
Flew high, leaving all doubt and fear.
He’d still sometimes prance,
Given half a chance,
But now he knew he belonged here.
