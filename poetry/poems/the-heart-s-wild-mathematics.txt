The Heart’s Wild Mathematics

The heart speaks in crimson whispers,
its lessons written in the salt of tears
and the copper taste of first kisses.

It tells the brain: You are not the only oracle here.
Your maps of logic cannot chart
the geography of a mother's worry,
the fault lines of forgiveness,
the deep ocean currents of grief
that pull stronger than any tide table.

Listen, it drums against the cage of ribs,
there are truths that cannot be dissected
under your fluorescent laboratories—
how a stranger's smile can resurrect hope,
how the scent of rain on summer pavement
can unlock doors you didn't know existed.

The brain, with its filing cabinets of facts,
its careful calculations and measured responses,
learns slowly that some equations
have no solutions,
only the wild mathematics of feeling
where two plus two might equal infinity
or the square root of sorrow.

The heart teaches patience—
not the kind that waits for buses,
but the kind that holds space
for contradictions to coexist:
loving someone who has left,
finding joy in the midst of breaking,
being brave enough to be foolish.

It whispers: Sometimes the smartest thing
is to leap before you look,
to trust the parachute of intuition,
to let your compass spin wildly
until it finds its own magnetic north
in the warm pulse of another's hand.

And finally, drumming its ancient rhythm,
the heart teaches the brain its hardest lesson:
that wisdom isn't always knowing—
sometimes it's the courage
to feel everything fully,
to let the flood come
and trust that you will learn to swim
in waters deeper than reason,
more necessary than breath.
