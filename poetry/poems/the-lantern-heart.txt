The Lantern Heart

I began as a traveler
with a map full of bright highways,
confident in my chosen route
toward a destination called Success.

But life took my compass,
tore my careful plans,
left me standing at the edge
of an unmapped forest
thick with the kind of darkness
that swallows footsteps whole.

So I learned to walk by feel,
my hands becoming eyes,
fingertips reading the braille
of bark and stone.
Each stumble taught me
where the roots reach out
to trip the unwary.

In the deepest part of the wood,
where no sunlight penetrates,
I discovered something extraordinary:
mushrooms glowing pale blue
in the rotting logs,
their bioluminescent bodies
writing poems in the dark.

I found streams that sang
even when I couldn't see them,
their liquid voices leading me
toward something I'd never
put on any map—
the underground river
of my own resilience.

Months later,
when I emerged blinking
into ordinary daylight,
I carried with me
a different kind of brightness:

the knowledge of how to navigate
by internal stars,
how to find water
by listening for its song.

Now I draw maps
for other lost travelers,
marking not just the safe paths
but the beautiful detours,
the dark places where
unexpected gifts wait:

the cave of quiet courage,
the valley of deep rest,
the mountain peak of perspective
you can only reach
by first learning to fall.

My new compass points not North
but toward whatever needs
illumination—
the forgotten corners
where light pools
in puddles of possibility,

where those who thought
they'd lost everything
discover they were simply
learning to see
in a different spectrum.

I am no longer the person
who feared getting lost.
I am the cartographer
of found things,
the guide who whispers:

Yes, it's dark here.
Yes, you're afraid.
But look—do you see
how your own heart
has become a lantern?
