The Smile Room

Through crumbled walls,
silence seeps,
a rotted hallway stretching into shadow.
An open door yawns
above it, "The Smile Room"
scrawled in red paint,
faint, like a murmur
from something broken.

Inside,
dingy, desolate,
no echo of life
just dust hanging heavy,
a tomb untouched by time.

Was this a place for laughter,
comedians weaving joy from thin air?
Or something darker
hands pressing smiles onto faces,
teaching lips to curve
without reason, without soul?

Did we forget how to smile?
Did we stumble here, blank-eyed,
relearning what was lost,
mimicking joy until it stuck?

The air thickens.
Shadows twist along the walls.
A chill claws up
from the splintered floor.
What happened here,
what died behind this door?

I step in.
Darkness bites the edges of my breath.
Dust swirls—ghosts of forgotten grins.
Then a flash—
a mirror ignites in the gloom.

My face stares back,
hollow as the room,
a stranger carved in glass
wearing my skin like a lie.

No jests, no lessons,
just the weight of realization:
I’m alive, alone,
and this room
is where joy came to scream its last.
