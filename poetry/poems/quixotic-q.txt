Quixotic Q

I want you to imagine the letter Q

nestled in a bowl of alphabet soup—

your spoon lifting it where it bobbles,

suspicious of its tail, that curl that’s superfluous.



I have always been partial to Q’s curiosity,

the way it insists on company—U at its side always,

like an anxious friend who can’t bear silence alone,

or a host fetching an extra chair for the party.



You find it in quirks—quiet quirks—

in queenly quilts, quaint questions that start with “why,”

in quiver of wings at dusk, in quarts of lemonade spilled by kids

running too fast in the backyard, squealing.



And yet, Q can vex us—clunky in crosswords,

quandary in spelling bees, quirky in accents—

it demands respect, though it’s small, eccentric;

we forgive it because it gives us something curious to love.



So here’s to the Q—imperfect, peculiar,

a letter that, in its oddness, reminds us

we need oddness, just as we need “you.”
