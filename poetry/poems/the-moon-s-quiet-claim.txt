The Moon's Quiet Claim

The Moon bends close—not touch, but near—

a breath that stirs the tide’s long ear.

The sea rolls over in its bed,

drawn by the light, by longing led.



All night it combs the waves with silver,

spilling where the shallows shiver,

and though the dawn will steal its glow,

the tide still sways the ache to go.



Oh, mark the phases, slow and sweet—

now crescent-shy, now swollen fleet—

how every pull is just a plea:

Stay close enough to hold, yet free.



And when the current ebbs at last,

the Moon lets go but still holds fast,

for even as the distance grows,

the ocean hums the tune it chose.
