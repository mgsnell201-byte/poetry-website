On the Gift of Scorn

How long I wandered, pen in hand, half-blind,

Through gardens where no serpent dared to coil,

Where praise fell soft as Eden's fruitless soil,

And comfort wrapped my vision like a rind.

Then came your words—a flame to loose and bind—

That scorned my verse as derivative toil,

As ego's mask; a cluttered, worthless foil:

The grit I lacked, at last, for pearl refined.



For what is light that never knew the deep?

What triumph rises where no battle came?

You gave me chains, and taught my hands to break them;

Your bitterness became the mine I keep,

The fuel I lacked to kindle up my flame—

So thank you, friend; I've learned at last to take them.
