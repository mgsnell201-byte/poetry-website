The Stone Steps

I was admiring the front steps of a house
on my street this morning, the way
the marble has been worn down in the middle
by a century of comings and goings,
the stone hollowed out like a soup spoon.

You can see where everyone walked,
right up the center, avoiding the edges
the way we all do, following the path
of least resistance, or maybe just
the path everyone else has taken.

It made me think of my neighbor, Mrs. Chen,
who sits on her porch most afternoons
watching the street like it's a movie
she's seen before but doesn't mind seeing again.
Her face has that same quality—

weather-worn by years of tears,
smoothed down in places by grief
the way water shapes rock,
the way footsteps hollow out stone,
slowly, imperceptibly, until one day

you notice the depth of it.
But here's the thing: those steps are beautiful.
More beautiful, I'd argue, than new marble,
than any surface that hasn't been touched
by the patient friction of living.

Mrs. Chen smiled at me this morning,
and I could see in that smile
all the stairs she's climbed,
all the thresholds she's crossed,
the weight of every arrival and departure

pressed into the architecture of her face.
And I thought, this is what we're doing,
all of us, wearing each other down
and being worn down in turn,
making hollows where the light can pool.
