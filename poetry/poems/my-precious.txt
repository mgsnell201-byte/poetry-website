My Precious

For years I called my daughter this

at bedtime, at the breakfast table,

over the phone when she was away at camp,

the two words arriving as naturally

as a hand finding the top of her head.



Then she turned eleven

and wanted to see the movie,

the one where the creature

crouches on his rock in the dark,

stroking his ring and hissing

those same two words

into the green water of his face.



I remember the moment she looked at me

from across the living room,

a single eyebrow raised

like a drawbridge going up.



After that, I was careful.

I tried "sweetheart". I tried "kiddo".

I tried her actual name, which sounded

strangely formal, like a subpoena

served to someone you had tucked in

every night for a decade.



Sometimes now she calls me "Gollum"

when I hold on too long at the door,

and I laugh because what else

can a father do

when his daughter has seen

what devotion looks like

from the outside—hunched, obsessive,

its small treasure clutched

against a world that is only

trying to take it back.
