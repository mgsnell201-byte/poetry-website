Day after Day

The morning light is filtered through the blinds,
a house of blueprints and of master plans.
We polish up the edges of our minds
and hold the world with trembling, steady hands.

We trace the lines of who we're meant to be,
a perfect sketch of life in symmetry,
but shadows pool beneath the kitchen floor
and silence knocks upon the bedroom door.

It isn't about the cure that sets you free
or finding where the broken pieces fit.
It's learning how to live with what we see
and finding grace within the thick of it.

We don't need "happy," "perfect," or "sublime" —
just something we can carry through the time,
a little light to help us through the storm,
not quite normal, but something close to warm.

The mountains that we climb are made of ghosts,
of memories we're told we should forget.
We cling to what we think we need the most
and pay a price we haven't measured yet.

But medicine can't dull the sting of truth
and lightning cannot jump-start stolen youth.
The walls we build to keep the sorrow out
are the same ones built of agony and doubt.

Maybe the hole is meant to stay wide open,
maybe the hurt is how we know we're here.
There is a dignity in being broken
that never grows inside a life of fear.

You have to let the memory take its shape.
There is no easy logic or escape,
just the courage to stand up and let it go
into the vast and terrifying know.

So let the stars come out into the black.
We're moving forward, never looking back.
The sky is gray, the wind is rising high,
but we are walking underneath the sky.

Day after day.
Light.
Just give us light.
