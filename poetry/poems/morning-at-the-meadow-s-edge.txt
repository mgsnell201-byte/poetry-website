Morning at the Meadow's Edge

The light was breaking through the pines

when a deer and a fawn stood just beyond

the clearing where I'd stopped to watch

the world begin again.



How still they were, breathing the morning,

their dark eyes holding secrets we forget—

that we belong here, walking this earth

beneath the indifferent and magnificent stars.



The mother's ears turned, catching sounds

I couldn't hear, her body a question

the forest answered with silence.



The fawn, all legs and newness,

stepped closer to where grasses bow

and the dew makes everything glow.



Tell me, what else could matter

in this moment when time slowed

and two wild ones allowed me to see

what patience looks like, what grace means?



They didn't need my wonder,

but they permitted it—

these creatures who know without knowing

that the world is generous and hard

and that we must love it anyway.



When they left, moving like water through trees,

I stood in the emptied space they'd blessed,

grateful for what I'd been given:

this one morning, this brief meeting,

this reminder that we are not alone

in our fumbling toward beauty.
