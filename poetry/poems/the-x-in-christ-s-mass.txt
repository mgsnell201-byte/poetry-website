The X in Christ's Mass

Beneath the green boughs of the tree shining bright,
I pondered a puzzle one still Advent night:
Why some folks write "X-mas" on banners and signs,
As though they'd cross Christ out of Christmas's lines.

And then I recalled what the old scholars knew:
That X is the Greek letter Chi—and it's true!
It starts Christ's own name in the Gospels' first Greek,
So X still means Christ, if it's Christ that you seek.

And "mas" is the Mass, where the faithful still kneel,
Where candles are glowing and hearts start to heal;
So X-mas, read rightly, as you now can see,
Spells Christ at the Mass, just as Christmas should be.

But let me be honest and say what I think:
Most X-mas today is just saving some ink.
It's not scholars of Greek with a reverent pen,
Just hurrying shoppers, cut-corners again.

They'll spend hours wrapping and stringing the lights,
And baking and shopping through long, busy nights,
Yet can't spare five letters, a second or so,
To honor the Name of the Babe long ago.

Now there is a fellow of rockets and cars,
Who's building great ships to go sailing to Mars;
He took a blue bird and renamed it with X,
And ponders what marvel he'll launch to it next.

So here's a suggestion, all friendly and kind,
For one with such rockets and stars on his mind:
Your X is half there, so just add on the Mass.
Come kneel where the hymns ring from organ and brass!

For no rocket climbs to the height of the Star
That guided the Wise Men who traveled so far,
And no feed or app, though it circles the earth,
Can share news as good as our dear Savior's birth.

So write it in full; we've no need of the X,
Except for that fellow, whose Mass may come next!
The Christ in the Christmas, the Child in the hay,
Is the reason we gather this glad holy day.
