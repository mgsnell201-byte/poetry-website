The Little Christmas Train Of

The little Christmas train starts up again,
pulling out from the station of what I meant to say,
chugging past the wrapped boxes and nativity scene,
through the tunnel of chair legs draped with tinsel,
heading somewhere important, I'm sure of it.

I watch it gather speed under twinkling lights,
this determined traveler with a clear destination—
the perfect words waiting at the end of the line,
the right way to explain about the cards I forgot to send,
the apology that's been sitting in the depot all week.

It's picking up momentum now, rolling past the coffee table,
through the crossing where I should mention the poinsettia,
connecting that somehow to what I wanted to say about dinner,
the freight cars linking up beautifully—
Christmas memories, goodwill, reconciliation—
all the pieces finally coming together.

But wait, there was something about the children,
wasn't there? Something about their toys, or maybe
it was the wrapping paper, or was it—
the train slows down near the tree,
starts to drift, losing steam.

What was I saying? The track ahead goes fuzzy,
the station I was heading toward disappears in fog,
all those carefully loaded boxcars of meaning
suddenly uncoupled, rolling backward,
the engine stalled somewhere between
what I meant and what I'm saying,

and now I'm standing here with my hand out,
reaching for the caboose of a thought
that's already left without me,
all that precious cargo of silent-night wishes
and peace on earth scattered along an empty track
I can't quite see anymore.

What were we talking about?
Oh yes—trains.
