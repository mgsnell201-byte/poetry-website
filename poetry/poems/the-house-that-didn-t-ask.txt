The House That Didn’t Ask

I used to be a plain red building,
brick and mortar, nothing more,
until a hand came, slow, unyielding,
and painted every wall and door.

I did not ask to wear this pattern,
did not choose to be a joke,
but now the tourists stop and flatten
their faces to the glass, yet no one spoke

to warn me I would become a story—
a house too loud for its own street,
too bright to blend, too full of glory,
too strange for anyone discreet.

The yellow house beside me whispers.
The other homes turn heads away.
I stand between two shy companions,
both safer in their quieter way.

My chimneys hold their smoke like secrets,
my windows blink behind the vine.
The flowers at my feet don’t ask it—
they bloom regardless, wild and fine.

Maybe that’s the trick of living:
loud enough that no one’s sure
if it’s the paint or you that’s giving
the street its courage to endure.
