The Impatient Patient

I have been sitting in this waiting room so long

I have memorized the fish in the aquarium—

there are seven, though one hides behind the plastic castle

most of the time, which I respect. Opening and closing

their mouths as if they had something to say about being patient.



I have read about celebrities I've never heard of,

their beach bodies and their breakups,

and I have studied the anatomy poster on the wall

until I am nearly qualified to perform my own surgery.



The receptionist has looked up at me twice,

both times to smile apologetically,

a smile I have come to understand means

"You are still here, yes, we see you, you continue to exist."

Or was that a look of puzzlement: "I see you're still here"?



I wonder if this is a test,

if somewhere in a back room the doctor is watching me

on a closed-circuit television, taking notes:

Subject shows signs of fidgeting at the thirty-minute mark.

At 45 minutes, subject has checked phone six times.

By the 60-minute point, subject appears to be considering

whether the plant in the corner is real or silk.



I could be doing so many things right now—

I could be at home not doing them, which is different

from sitting here not doing them,

the way being alone is different from being lonely,

though both involve a certain quality of waiting.



Perhaps impatience is just a form of faith,

the belief that your life is happening somewhere else,

in some other room where you are supposed to be,

and all you need is for someone to call your name,

to open the door and wave you through

into the rest of your life, which has been delayed

but is surely, surely about to begin.
