Thaw

This morning I walked down
to the pond where the ice
had pulled back from the banks,
and the cattail buds,
those blunt green fists,
had broken through the mud.

I put my hand in.
It was cold, but not
the cold of January.
It was the cold
of something about to change.
Under the surface I felt the silt
soft as the skin behind the ear.

In the field the wild plum
had opened overnight,
every blossom a small white mouth,
pollen thick on the anthers,
and the bees pressing in
with their furred bodies dusted gold,
nudging the stamens
the way a face nudges into a hand
it has missed.

The smell came across the field
and I stood in it
the way you stand in warm water
when you haven't been warm in a long time.

I used to think
that spring was about beauty.
Now I think it is about
the body's stubborn answer
to the question the cold keeps asking.

Have you felt it —
that first morning when the air
is different on your skin
and something in your chest opens
that you did not know was shut?

The plum goes on
offering its blossoms
to the bees, to the wind,
to whatever comes.
