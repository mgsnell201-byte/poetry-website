The Silence Inside

I held the phone to my ear this morning,
that smooth black rectangle I've been feeding
with passwords, with photographs, with the shape
of my thumb pressing down on glass
like a prayer I didn't know I was making—
and the screen went dark.

Not dramatically. No flash, no warning buzz.
Just mid-scroll through someone's careful life,
their vacation photos, their new haircut,
their coffee arranged beside an open book,
and the light simply folded itself away.

I pressed the button. Nothing.
Pressed it again, harder, as if insistence
could wake what had decided to sleep.
The same nothing, smooth and absolute.

There's something almost honest about it,
the way it doesn't pretend to struggle,
doesn't flicker hopefully when I shake it,
doesn't drain itself in slow percentages
like the old phones did, clinging to one bar,
one percent, one last gasped message
before the final dark.

This one just stopped. A small abdication.
And now I'm sitting in the sudden quiet,
the way you might sit in a room
after the music ends, hearing the shape
of what's no longer there—no hum, no glow,
no need to answer, scroll, perform, become
whatever the screen asked me to be.

Just my hands, strangely light now,
holding this warm weight of nothing,
this small brick of silence I can't throw away,
this thin door that won't open again,

and behind it, somewhere, the strange relief
of being unreachable, the world still turning
without my thumbs moving across it,
my face no longer lit from below,
just breathing here in the sudden darkness,
perfectly still, waiting to be seen.
