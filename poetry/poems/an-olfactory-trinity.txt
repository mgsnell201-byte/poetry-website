An Olfactory Trinity

The first proof is a secret, spun
From pulses slow and meals undone.
A personal, perfumed paradox,
A key designed for one sole's locks.
It blooms a warm and brief surprise
Beneath our own familiar skies—
A bodily released confession,
A fragrant, personal impression.
It’s there, then not—a ghostly trace,
A knowing smirk upon the face.
This gastro-symphonic event
Makes potpourri seem heaven-sent.

The second proof is marital,
A test of love that's not comparable.
For my true love, by fate's design,
Lacks any sense of scent—divine.
She cannot smell the fresh-cut pine,
The sour milk, the rose, or wine.
And so, with heart sincere and clear,
I tried to make the truth appear.
I said, "My dear, you don't smell good,"
And in the silent pause she stood.
She heard an insult, harsh and crude—
The proof that meaning can elude.

The third proof walks on two feet,
And makes the very air feel… sweet?
A pungent, potent, personal cloud,
Of musk that makes the meek feel cowed.
She haunts the space next to the water cooler,
Where musk mocks every molecule-er
Her name is Jean, it’s true,
And politeness is my debt and due.
But conversation seems a task
Behind my own supplied air mask.
So when our lonely pathways cross,
I calculate the social cost
And offer just, “Hi, Jean,” through filtered grace—
The proof I’ve braved this scented place.

Thus, these three smells, both bold and grand,
Are proof of life, close at hand.
From gut, from home, from workplace sphere,
They shout: You are alive and still here!
