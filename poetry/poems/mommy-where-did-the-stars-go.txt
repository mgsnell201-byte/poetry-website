Mommy! Where did the stars go?

Last night she saw the stars for her first time—
Those silver points that pierce the summer night
Above our meadow where the fireflies
Dance lazy circles through the cooling air.
She'd never stayed awake past nine before,
But bedtime prayers had stretched to wonder long
At how the heavens hold such tiny lights
Without them falling like the autumn rain.

Her small hand pressed against the window glass,
She counted seven, then gave up the count
When more kept blooming in the deeper blue.
"They're beautiful," she whispered, eyes grown wide—
I thought she'd drink the whole night sky complete
And carry starlight in her sleeping dreams.

But morning came the way it always does:
The sun climbed over Johnson's pasture hill
And painted gold across our kitchen floor.
She woke and ran to that same window sill
Where she had watched her first night's wonder grow,
Then turned to me with panic in her voice:
"Mommy! Where did the stars go? Are they dead?"

How do you tell a child that certain things
Hide but never leave? That stars are patient,
Waiting behind the blue we think is void,
Like seeds beneath the snow that trust the warmth?
I lift her to the sill and point above
Where Polaris keeps its faithful guard,
Unseen but constant as the morning tide.

"They're sleeping now," I say, "the way you slept.
Tonight they'll wake and light their lamps again."
She nods, content this simple truth feels right—
How stars need rest behind the light of day
To shine their brightest when the darkness falls.
