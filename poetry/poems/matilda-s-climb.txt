Matilda's Climb

Matilda climbed to find out WHY
The grown-ups all forgot to fly,
Why everyone just stayed below
And said, "That's life," and "That's the show."

She passed a crow in business shoes
Who said, "Go back! You're gonna LOSE!"
A squirrel moaned, "It's way too HIGH!
What if you fall? What if you DIE?"

A pigeon sighed, "I tried before—
Got halfway up, my wings were sore.
Turned back around, went home instead.
Now look at me—I'm safe," he said.

Her hands were cramping, knees were bent,
She wondered what the climbing meant.
But still she grabbed each leaf and stem—
She didn't want to end like THEM.

At last—the Giant, ten feet tall,
With gentle eyes and not mean at all.
It whispered soft, "What scares you, kid?"
She said, "That I'll stop. Like they all did."

The Giant smiled. "Then you're not small.
The only ones who truly fall
Are those who never leave the ground.
Keep climbing, kid. That's how you're found."
