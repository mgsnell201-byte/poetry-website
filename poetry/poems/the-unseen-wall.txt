The Unseen Wall

I stand in the corner where the light
does not reach. The dust settles
on my shoulders like a second skin.
No one turns when I enter.

The room continues its business—
chairs scrape, voices rise and fall,
coffee cups find their saucers.
I could be air. I could be stone.

But watch—the window holds a sparrow
pressed against the glass, searching
for the opening that isn't there.
Its throat pulses. Its claws grip
the narrow ledge. It does not leave.

I know something about waiting
against transparent barriers.
I know the weight of being unseen
while the world moves through you
like water through a net.

The bird tilts its head. Studies
the room it cannot enter.
I study the bird I cannot reach.
Between us, the glass holds
both reflection and distance.

Then—it finds a seam. A gap
where the frame meets the wall.
One careful shift of weight,
one breath held and released,
and it slips into open sky.

I breathe with it. The room
notices nothing. But something
has changed. The dust on my sleeve
catches light I didn't see before.

Invisibility is not absence.
It is a kind of gathering.
Like roots under snow.
Like stone remembering water.
Like the archer's bow holding
tension before the release.

I step from the corner.
The floor accepts my weight.
The air parts for me.
I am here. I am here.
