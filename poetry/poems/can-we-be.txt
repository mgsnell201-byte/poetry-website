Can We Be

I loved you in the quiet way
the moon loves the tide—
relentless, unseen, pulling
even when the shore resists.

You left like a season that forgot
to turn, leaving me stranded
in the wrong kind of light,
half-alive, half-eaten by shadows.

I begged the silence for answers,
but silence only knows
how to hold its own breath.
(You were always better at that.)

Rejection is a language
spoken in reverse—
I learned it fluently,
while you pretended not to hear.

And yet—
here I am, stitching my ribs back together,
not with thread, but with the memory
of how your laughter once fit between them.

I do not hate you.
I do not even blame you.
But tell me this—
when the dust settles,
when the ache becomes a story
instead of an open wound...

Can we be
the kind of strangers
who still leave flowers
on each other’s graves?
