What the Body Keeps Without Asking

Mercy is not what you think.
Not a dove released, not a judge’s pardon—
it’s the way a streetlamp hums
after the fight downstairs is over,
how the light stays on
even when no one looks up.

Emotions—
don’t call them storms or wildfires.
They’re the slow collapse of a porch step
under too many winters,
the way the wood remembers
every footfall but never cries out.

Then, the twist:
they are the same unfinished thing—
Mercy, the space between knife and skin
when the hand hesitates.
Emotions, the blood
already rushing to the cut
before it’s made.

Ask them apart if you want.
They won’t answer.
They’ve always been the echo
and the voice
shaping the same word.
