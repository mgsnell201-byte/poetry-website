Ode on Peter Rabbit's Christmas Transgression

Of disobedience first I sing, and of that youthful hare
Who scorned maternal warnings wise and cast aside all care,
And ventured forth on Christmas Eve when starlight blessed the snow
Into McGregor's garden-realm, forbidden long ago.

What moved thee, Peter, tender soul, to break thy mother's word?
What appetite for earthly root within thy breast was stirred
That made thy timorous feet to tread where mortal peril lay,
Where man's dominion, harsh and stern, o'er frozen garden held sway?

Beneath the holy night there stood that plot in silver's gleam,
Where carrots shone like garnets bright, and cabbages did seem
As emeralds encrusted white in solemn, tempting rows—
A paradise of winter's store beneath the moon's cold glows.

With rapture unrestrained he ate, as Adam did consume
The fatal fruit in Eden's bower that sealed our mortal doom.
But lo! The keeper of this plot, McGregor fierce and fell,
Espied the small transgressor there and raised a wrathful yell!

Then fled poor Peter through the beds where winter cabbage grew,
His jacket lost—as bright and fair as heaven's vault of blue—
His shoes abandoned in his flight, like Eden's exiled pair
Who left that garden paradise in sorrow and despair.

He squeezed beneath the garden gate, his trembling spirit vexed,
While sisters virtuous at home their evening meal came next—
Through obedience they supped in peace on bread and wholesome fare,
As Peter shivered in his bed, repentant in his lair.

Yet when the morn broke radiant forth on Christ the Savior's birth,
His mother came with healing herbs of gentle, tender worth.
With chamomile and loving kiss upon his fevered head,
She spoke forgiveness, mercy's balm, and grace where judgment fled.

His sisters brought him Christmas sweets and held him close and warm,
Though they might justly scorn the fool who braved McGregor's storm.
Instead with charity divine they shared their festive store—
Thus mercy conquers rigid law, as Heaven taught before.

The jacket dried beside the hearth, the shoes in order placed,
And Peter learned that though we fall, we may through love be graced.
On this most holy Christmas morn, let every heart discern:
Redemption waits in home's embrace for all who would return.
