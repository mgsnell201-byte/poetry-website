The Cartographers

Your hands move like dusk settling on riverstones—

mapping the quiet places where light pools

in the hollow of my wrist,

the bend of my elbow,

those secret coves only moonrise knows.



You are no witch, but a scholar of luminous things—

your fingertips chart constellations

in the freckles scattered across my shoulders,

connecting them like villages

along some forgotten pilgrimage route.



We sit cross-legged beneath the patient stars,

passing a cup of silence between us,

its rim still warm from your lips.

The night air hums—

not with spells, but with the slow vibration

of a tuning fork struck long ago.



When you press your palm to mine,

the lines of our lifelines align—

an old story written twice,

in slightly different dialects.

The fire between us doesn't roar,

but glows like foxfire in damp wood—

a cool, persistent radiance

that outlasts the match.



At dawn, we'll fold our maps differently—

you'll carry the imprint of my pulse,

I'll remember the weight of your gaze,

and the space between us

will thrum with all the unsung verses

of this quiet, endless song.
