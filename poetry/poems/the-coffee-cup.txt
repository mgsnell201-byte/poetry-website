The Coffee Cup

Forget the dozen roses wrapped in cellophane,

the heart-shaped boxes of chocolates at the drugstore,

the restaurants booked solid with couples

trying to prove something to each other with prix-fixe menus.

I want to tell you about the kind of love

that happens on a Wednesday in February

when she's still half asleep in her bathrobe

and you're pouring coffee into her favorite mug—

the one with the faded picture of a lighthouse

that neither of you can bring yourself to replace.



It's not the love of Valentine's commercials,

not the kind that needs violins or moonlight

or declarations shouted from rooftops,

not the love that arrives once a year

in red envelopes with somebody else's words inside.

It's quieter than that, more like the sound

of two people breathing in the same room,

the small music of ordinary life.



This is the love that knows

how you take your eggs, which side of the bed

you prefer, the exact temperature you like the house

before you're willing to get up.

It's the love that hands you your reading glasses

before you even realize you're squinting at the newspaper.



Sometimes I think real love is just this:

the way she laughs at your jokes even when they're terrible,

how you both reach for the remote at the same time

and somehow never argue about what to watch,

the fact that she saves you the last piece of pie

even though you both know pie is her weakness.



So let them have their Valentine's Day—

the teddy bears clutching satin hearts,

the couples dressed up and uncomfortable,

the pressure to be romantic on schedule.

Give me instead this everyday devotion,

love, it turns out, is not so much a thunderstorm

as it is the steady rain that makes things grow—

patient, persistent, showing up day after day

to water all the small moments

until they bloom into something

you can't imagine living without,



like that lighthouse mug between her hands,

still warm, still holding something necessary,

still here.
