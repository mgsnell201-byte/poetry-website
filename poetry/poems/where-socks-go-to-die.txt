Where Socks Go to Die

Every few months, I open the drawer,
expecting a reunion, a neat parade of socks,
but there’s always one missing,
slipped through some cosmic laundry chute.
I imagine it wandering, a cotton ghost,
haunting the dryer's lint trap or the space
behind the washing machine,
where dreams of pairing go to die.

I’ve searched the usual places—
under the bed, where dust bunnies
plot their quiet rebellions,
or tangled in the sheets,
like a lover who left without a note.
But socks don’t just vanish;
they choose exile, don’t they?
Fed up with being stretched over heels,
tired of the spin cycle’s endless waltz.

Perhaps they're transitioning into sock monkeys,
or maybe they’ve found a secret sock Valhalla,
a place where single socks gather,
no longer mismatched, no longer judged,
sipping coffee in some parallel universe
where washing machines hum lullabies
and no one folds them into submission.
I picture them laughing,
their threads unraveling in joy.

And yet, I keep the odd ones,
those widowed argyles and plain whites,
stacked in the corner of the drawer,
a monument to hope or laziness.
Each time I do laundry, I half-expect
one to limp back, sheepish,
muttering about a wild night in the hamper.
But they never do. They’re gone for good,
leaving me to wonder if I, too,
might one day slip away
to wherever socks go to die.
