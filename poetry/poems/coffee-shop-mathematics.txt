Coffee Shop Mathematics

There's a mathematics to longing—

how one smile multiplies into

a thousand imagined conversations,

how a stranger's glance becomes

the cornerstone of a house

you'll never live in.



I built cathedrals from her silhouette,

sculpted forever from the way

she looked away that first time,

pretending not to notice when I caught her watching.



But she was watching, whispers the fool

who lives between my ribs,

the one who still believes in

fairy tales and coffee shop magic.



My friend's words landed like stones

in still water—she's got a boyfriend, man—

and suddenly my cathedral was just

wishful architecture.



She said "yes" before I finished asking,

claimed him like territory

I never knew was contested.

And there I stood,

a cartographer of nowhere,

holding maps to a country

that was never mine to explore.



Still, I practiced letting her go

the way you practice piano scales:

badly, with no real intention

of getting better.



Because how do you unlove

someone who never knew your name?

How do you mourn a connection

that existed only in stolen glances?



The cruelest part:

she still looks.

Even with his arm around her,

her eyes still find mine

like a habit she can't break,

like I'm still part of some story

only she knows the ending to.



So I carry this impossible weight—

the happiness of being seen

and the pain of being dismissed,

the joy of recognition

and the agony of imagination,

learning that sometimes

the most beautiful feelings

are the ones that destroy you

one glance at a time.
