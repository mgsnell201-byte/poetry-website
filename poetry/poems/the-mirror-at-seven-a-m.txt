The Mirror at Seven A.M.

I used to greet the bathroom mirror

with something like indifference,

the way you'd nod at a neighbor

whose name you can never quite recall.



But lately it has become an oracle,

this silvered rectangle above the sink,

offering prophecies I never requested—

news of a crease near the mouth,

a strand of hair staging its silver rebellion,

the slight sag where my jawline

used to make a cleaner exit.



The mirror, it turns out, is a terrible liar.

It refuses to remember me

the way I remember myself,

that version from—what was it—

eight years ago? Ten?



Sometimes I catch my reflection mid-shave

and think I see my father

peering back through the foam,

as if he's been hiding in there all along,

waiting for the right moment

to wave hello through my own face.



This morning I tried squinting,

that old trick of blurring the details,

but the mirror adjusted for the light,

sharpened its focus like a camera

determined to capture the truth.



So I've decided to lower my expectations.

The mirror is just doing its job,

reporting the daily news without commentary,

a faithful journalist covering the slow,

unremarkable story of a face

learning to look like someone else—

someone I'll probably grow to recognize

if I stand here long enough,

patient as the glass itself,

which has been practicing this same reflection

every morning, without complaint,

for years.
