The Other Side of Harvest

In maple-red autumn, we opened our arms,
With gifts of corn, and stories to tell.
The rivers and roots, our sacred lands,
Laid bare to you, who came to settle.

The seasons turned; we shared our breath,
Your hands defiled our graves with scorn,
The eagle songs mourned stolen rest,
Our fires you razed, and trust was torn.

When winter howled and shadows fell,
Lodges burned, our families erased,
A cry rose up, a mournful yell,
Like leaves to frost, we were displaced.

We set a feast, a bond to mend,
You ate our gifts with fleeting grace,
Then seized the lives we could not defend—
Our blood, our earth, our rightful place.
