The Silent Boundary

Something draws me to the forest's edge,
Where tangled undergrowth meets mown grass,
A line that nature never meant to hold
But humans carve with posts and twisted wire.
The pine trees lean as if to whisper secrets,
Their bark peeled back like pages from a book
Forgotten in the rain, now curling soft.
I pause here often, feeling the divide
That separates the farm from deeper shade,
Where light filters through leaves in dappled patterns,
Awakening a quiet ache within.

Beyond this fence, the woods extend their arms,
Inviting yet forbidding, full of promise
That pulls at buried longings in my chest.
The air grows thick with scents of damp earth,
Moss clinging to the trunks like ancient grief,
And ferns unfurl in slow, deliberate grace.
No path leads straight into that dim realm;
Instead, faint trails diverge among the roots,
Worn by deer or perhaps by my own steps
From times when duty yielded to desire,
A momentary lapse into the wild.

Yet boundaries persist, as if the land
Itself conspires to keep us in our place,
With gaps that tempt but rarely let us through.
The apples on the orchard side hang heavy,
Their skins flushed red with unspent ripeness,
While ice from winter storms still scars the bark
Of pines bent but never fully broken.
I mend these fences yearly, stone by stone,
Though something in the frost heaves them apart,
A force that mocks our efforts at control,
Revealing how thin barriers truly are.

In moments like this, standing at the rim,
The farm behind me hums with daily tasks,
But passion stirs beneath the calm facade,
A restrained fire that burns without a flame.
The night will come to cloak these woods in sleep,
Yet underneath, the roots entwine and reach,
Defying lines we draw upon the ground.
What if I stepped across, just once, to feel
The undergrowth embrace my weary frame,
To sigh with winds that travel unchecked paths?
The difference might be small, or vast as years,
But here I linger, bound by unseen chains.

The snow will fall again, blanketing all,
Erasing edges till the world seems one,
But spring returns to sharpen every gap,
Reminding us of choices left untaken.
This tension holds me, intense yet concealed,
A passion buried deep like hidden streams
That carve their way through soil without a sound.
The woods watch on, indifferent to my gaze,
Their mystery a mirror to my own,
Where farm and forest meet in silent pact,
And I remain, observing from the verge.
