Before Anyone Told You About the Sun

Let me tell you something about mornings,

and how I used to think the light arrived

like a gift left on the doorstep—

something you didn't ask for

but were glad to find.



I was wrong about that.



Consider the 9th-grade high school hallway,

which is its own kind of weather,

the lockers like closed faces,

the floor a long uncertainty

you have to cross every single day

wearing your actual body,

aware that older kids own the walls

and you are still learning the geography of brave.



Here is what nobody mentions

in the literature on sunrise:

the dark doesn't wait to be rescued.

It thins because you moved through it.



You texted someone first.

You sat down next to the kid doing geometry at lunch,

the one nobody sat next to.

You raised your hand

not because you were sure

but because your hand went up anyway,

brave and slightly embarrassed,

like a bird that forgets it knows how to fly

and then remembers mid-fall.



That's when the shadows lose interest in you.

That's when the birds begin their editorial.



And later—

maybe weeks, maybe the length of one good friendship—

you'll notice the sky has gone completely gold,

and you'll think: when did that happen,



not knowing yet

that you were always

the one making the light,

the small and ordinary sun

of your own Tuesday morning,



shining, as suns do,

without being asked.
