Grandpa's Rhyme

In Grandpa's lap, on creaky chair,

He'd weave a world with gentle flair.

"Hickory, dickory, dock," his voice would chime,

The clock's tall tale, it passed through time.



A tiny mouse, in rhyming verse,

Ascended timepiece, bold and terse.

Each bong! a step, a furry blur,

Down the face, adventures stir.



My tiny heart, with wonder filled,

Believed the clock with stories willed.

For days I watched, eyes wide and bright,

For that brave mouse, in morning light.



The hands they swept, the moments flew,

No whiskered scurry, ever true.

But Grandpa smiled, his eyes so kind,

"The magic whispers, in your mind."



Now years have passed, the pages turn,

The clock still ticks, lessons yearn.

In tales untold, and dreams alight,

Grandpa's voice, a guiding light.



So when I hear that rhyming beat,

Of hickory, dickory, down the street,

I see a boy, a clock, a dream,

The magic spun, a joyful gleam.



For even though the mouse won't show,

The time we shared, forever grows.

And in that rhyme, so sweet and small,

A love for stories, blooms for all.
