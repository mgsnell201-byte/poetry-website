Between Two Doors

I. Birth

They say the first sound is a scream —
mine was, wet and furious,
torn out of a woman
who was screaming too,
both of us doing the same violent work
from opposite sides of the same door.
Somebody laughed. Somebody wept.
I did not choose the room,
the light like a struck match,
the hands that weighed me like fruit
and called it love.
I arrived the way everyone arrives:
uninvited, undeniable —
a mouth that hadn't chosen its first word,
no history yet,
no name that meant anything
until they gave me one.

II. Choices

But then the days accumulate like teeth.
I learned to want things —
that was the first betrayal of sameness.
I said no when I meant yes and yes
when the word cost me something.
I loved the wrong mouths on purpose.
I stayed in rooms with the windows painted shut
because leaving felt like proof I didn't belong there.
I built a self out of small refusals:
the job not taken, the woman not married,
the country left, the god put down
like a coat that no longer fit the weather.
Every choice was a little death of who I might have been —
the other selves pile up behind me
like coats on a hook, unworn, unmourned,
and I am only ever the one
still standing in this skin,
still choosing, which is the same as still becoming.
No one else has my wreckage.
No one else has my particular hunger,
shaped exactly to the shape of what starved me.
This is the cruelty and the gift of the middle:
that nothing here was given —
I built it with my own two furious hands
out of what the world put in front of me
and what I refused to let it keep.

III. Death

And at the end, they will gather again,
the same weeping in a different key —
grief this time instead of joy, or maybe not
though the body doesn't know the difference,
salt is salt.
Someone will hold my hand
the way they held it when it was new,
small and curling,
except now it will not curl back.
I will leave the way I came:
without my consent, without my say,
the room narrowing to a single point of light
then closing like an eye that has seen enough.
They will remember the specific things —
not that I was born, not that I died,
everyone does that, it means nothing alone —
but the way I said their name,
the choices that made me unmistakably myself
and not some other man
who also screamed his way into the world
and also, someday, will scream his way out.

Between two doors that open the same way for everyone,
I did something only I could have done.
That is the whole of it.
That is enough.
