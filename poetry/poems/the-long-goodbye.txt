The Long Goodbye

The autumn of my life arrives

unannounced and unwelcome, like a thief

stealing warmth from the bones,

leaving only shadows to inhabit.



Memories congeal on the edges of perception,

like frost on a pane that has long been still.

The fire that once burned bright is reduced to embers,

a faint glow that flickers, then dies.



I am a traveler without a destination,

turning homeward through landscapes of regret.

Each step, a weight that presses down,

each breath, a reminder of the passage of time.



In this twilight, I search for meaning,

but find only fragments of what's been;

the shards of shattered dreams, the splinters of love

that once seemed whole and complete.



And yet, even in decay, there is beauty;

a softening of edges, a quieting of pain,

a surrender to the stillness that gathers

like a slow snowfall on an autumn's day.



In this twilight, I find my own pace;

a gentle slowing of the heart's wild chase.

The world outside recedes, and I am free

to face the shadows that have long been me.



My eyes, once bright with hope and desire,

now dimly see the paths that I've conspired to take,

to leave behind, to hold on tight to love, to loss,

to life's uncertain light.



And when my time is finally done,

I'll let go of the ghosts that have been won,

the ones that lingered in memories of old will

fade away like autumn leaves grown cold.
