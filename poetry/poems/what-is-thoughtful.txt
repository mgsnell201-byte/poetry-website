What is Thoughtful?

I saw a heron this morning standing
in the shallows, so still
you might have mistaken it for a stick,
or a shadow, or nothing at all.

But it was thinking—I'm sure of it—
the way it held its body,
the way its eye followed the small movements
beneath the surface of the water.

Is this not thoughtfulness: to wait,
to watch, to let the world
reveal itself in its own time?
Not to rush, not to demand?

The grasses along the shore were bending
in the wind, each blade
leaning into the morning light,
and I thought: they are considering something too.

Perhaps thoughtfulness is not what happens
in the mind alone, but what the body does
when it pays attention—
the way you stop on the path

to let a beetle cross, the way you notice
the particular blue of today's sky,
different from yesterday's,
the way you hold a question

without needing to answer it right away.
Tell me, when did you last stand still
long enough to hear your own breathing,
to feel the pulse in your wrist,

to remember you are alive,
here, now, in this moment
that will not come again?
What else could matter more than this?
