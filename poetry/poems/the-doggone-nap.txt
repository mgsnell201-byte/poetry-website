The Doggone Nap

There once was a hound hilariously dozy,

In polka-dot pajamas, wrinkled, weird, and wild.

He dreamed of bacon—soft and sweet and cozy,

Then snored so loud, the curtains nearby smiled.



His flannel held the scent of squirrel and kibble,

A hint of bunny from his chasing spree.

Each snort escaped in rhythmic, dreamy dribble,

As drool pooled near the shoes beside his knee.



The whiff of tennis balls lit up his brain,

His tail began to thump in dreamland flight.

A wheezy grin broke through the drool and strain—

“This is the life!” he barked with delight.



But then he woke—a twitch, a startled leap,

The bacon’s scent had tugged him from his doze.

Reality returned with smells so deep—

He’d soaked his jammies. A soggy tale, I suppose.
