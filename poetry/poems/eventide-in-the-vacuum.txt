Eventide in the Vacuum

The ship’s husk spins, a carcass stripped of heat

while distant suns, like static, flicker weak.

No dirge, no clock—just silence, thick and sweet,

a lullaby for those too tired to speak.



The weak can’t speak, yet gasp their numbered air,

each breath a spark swallowed by endless night.

The stars don’t watch. They never learned to care,

though men once swore they burned for human light.



Light lies. It shows me Earth, that sapphire ghost,

still turning, still untouched by all I’ve lost.

I touch the glass—my hand a fleeting frost—

a stain that fades where no one counts the cost.



Now darkness takes the debt I couldn’t pay:

a name untombed, a sun gone cold as clay.
