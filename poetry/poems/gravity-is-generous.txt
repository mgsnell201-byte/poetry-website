Gravity Is Generous

First, nothing in the morning blue,
then, soft as breath, a drifting white,
a weightless someone, small and new,
with falling hidden in its height.

We rise as saplings in a gale,
all knees and nerve and stubborn green,
and call each gust a foe turned frail
while roots drink what once fell unseen.

Then thaw: the river, brown and loud,
carves restless names in banks of clay,
its swollen current, fierce and proud,
still blind to what it bears away.

Then summer: oak in heavy green,
sheltering shoots that seek the glow,
storing each year we’ve ever been
in rings of flood and drought below.

Then autumn takes the borrowed green
and lets the glow fall on each shoot;
the boughs that summer kept unseen
stand bare above the buried root.

We settle, leaf by leaf, below.
Our fallen leaves feed roots anew;
the shoots exhale; the breezes blow
a thread of white through morning blue.
