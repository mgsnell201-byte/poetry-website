The Unread Postcard

You find it in a drawer,
a postcard from some beach town,
its edges curled like a smile
you forgot to return.

What if it’s been waiting there,
its palm trees and sunset
mocking your oversight,
a message from someone
who thought you’d care enough to read?

You flip it over,
the handwriting faded,
like a whisper from a radio
left on in another room,
and there’s a soft ache in the absence,
tempered by a grin at your own neglect.

The twist comes softly—
you slip it back into the drawer,
not because you don’t care,
but because some gaps
are funnier left alone,
like a joke you laugh at
without knowing the punchline.
