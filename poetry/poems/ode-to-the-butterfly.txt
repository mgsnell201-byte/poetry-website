Ode to the Butterfly

Oh, fluttering whisper of painted sky,
Dancing sunbeam, you float so high—
A living petal, by zephyrs blown,
Nature’s stained glass, in light half-grown.

You are joy, stitched into flight,
A poem the air itself must write.
The child who chases, wide-eyed, slack,
Sees magic wear a velvet back.

Yet—butterfly—what curious name!
As if your wings were spread with grain,
As if you’d flutter from the churn,
A dairy sprite—oh, take your turn!

(No milkweed ever squeezed you tight
Enough to justify that right.
But “bread-and-jam-wing”? “Sugar-sly”?
No—butterfly must stay. Good try.)

So drift, you flicker of soul set free,
Proof that the earth still dreams in glee.
The world sighs awe where’er you pass—
Tiny masterpiece… on legs of glass.
