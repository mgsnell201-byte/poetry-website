A Field in Bloom, A Sky in Pieces

The sun spills gold upon my woven threads,

A meadow’s loom where wildflowers spread.

Their breaths, a perfume, rise and sway,

Yet I, the meadow, feel roots begin to fray.



A tremor comes—not wind, but something frail—

Petals un-tethered, caught in a fleeting gale.

They are not insects, but fragments of a sky,

Stained-glass wings that forget to fall, and fly.



I watch them pulse, a breath I cannot hold,

A heart-thrum rhythm, tender, yet so old.

A clockwork wound by sunlight’s fleeting hand,

Their dance a tick that shifts my ancient sand.



These butterflies, my painted, trembling guests,

Stitch light through blades where shadows come to rest.

Their flutter—lace that dares the air to bend—

A whispered joy, too bright to comprehend.



The daisy, pale, extends her fragile lap,

A stage for velvet feet, a tender tap.

The violet, shy, her purple bruised by time,

Feels a brushstroke fade beneath the climb.



The poppy, bold no more, her petals fall,

Yet holds her heart for one last winged call.

I am the meadow, keeper of their pact,

Of nectar given, beauty brought intact.



But I am weary—my roots drink shallow now,

My blooms, my jewels, must soon take their bow.

A silent poem, of gold and fleeting red,

Lives in the pause where wing and flower wed.



My fading writes each line—no theft, but gift,

Each loosened root that lets the beauty drift.

I am the clockwork wound by my own decay,

The urgent spring that calls the wings to stay.
