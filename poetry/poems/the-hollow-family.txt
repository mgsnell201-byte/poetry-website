The Hollow Family

A man, half his head gone,

skull a red-rimmed void,

stands beside a woman,

her chest a hollow circle,

where a heart should be—

her face calm, eyes blind to feeling.



A girl between them,

small hands holding their losses:

a brain, pink and tagged like a soldier’s,

a heart, red and pulsing.

She’s the keeper of what they lack—

mind that won’t think,

heart that won’t love.



A family of absences,

bound by what’s missing,

the child carrying their weight.

What is a family without thought, without love?

A shell, a void, a burden.

Yet her grip whispers hope—

to mend the broken,

fill the hollows,

teach them to live anew.



What is a family without thought, without feeling?

A shell, a void, a child’s burden.

Yet in her hands, there is a quiet plea—

to stitch them whole,

to fill the hollows with something new,

to teach them that even the broken

can learn to live again.
