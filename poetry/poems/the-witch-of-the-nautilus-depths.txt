The Witch of the Nautilus Depths

In the hush where drowned cathedrals weep,
she rules from a spire of spiraled sleep—
a witch with a crown of knotted tides,
her laughter the groan of a sinking ship’s side.

Her skin is the sheen of oil on waves,
her nails, barnacle-sharp, carve sailors’ graves.
The nautilus curls where her heartbeat thrums,
a clockwork of echoes where lost breath hums.

She brews the storm in a clamshell bowl,
stirs lightning with fingers woven from stolen souls.
Her voice is the net that hauls men deep,
a lullaby sung to the hulls that weep.

Touch her—you’ll wear the sea’s cold brand,
salt in your lungs, her mark on your hand.
But kneel with a gift she cannot resist,
and she’ll bare you the pearls in her fathomless fist.
