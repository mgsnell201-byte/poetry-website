At the Casement

The casement holds a last intensity

Of western light that trembles on the glass,

As though the pane itself were loath to see

The source of day relinquish dominance.



A wren alights upon the weathered sill

And tilts its head toward fields already dim,

Where one broad furnace cools against its will

And something pale ascends to balance him.



They pass in silence measurable as breath,

The old provider and the quiet thief

Of color from the world; then sudden red

Suffuses all the margin of the west,



As if a murmured judgment, cool and keen,

Had touched the sun’s broad pride and made it flush.

What word could make the maker of the scene

Betray such warmth before his daily death?



Perhaps the moon remarked how even fire

Must learn the temper of a softer blade,

How day’s fierce forging leaves the world entire

But night’s reflection shows what day has made.



The casement darkens; on the sill the wren

Lifts from its perch and vanishes in flight

Against the bronze that deepens into slate,

Where one last ember settles out of sight.
