Moon Jelly at Ebb Tide

The ebb had drawn the bay out of itself.

Boats leaned in the mud, their ropes slack,

and under the pier the last of the day

lay in thin brass on the pools.



There I found it: a moon jelly,

no bigger than a dinner plate,

its four dim rings showing through

as through a window breathed on.



I slid a split plank underneath.

Its bell tightened once—not a hand,

not a mouth, but the whole soft body

trying the element it had lost.



The sun dropped behind the fish sheds.

Night came up the beach without a sound

and took the colour from the barnacles,

the orange weed, my red sleeve.



I carried it down the shining channel.

Cold entered my boots. The plank

grew weightless as the water deepened,

and the jelly slid from it.



For one pulse it hung beneath me,

then another—so faint

I might have given it that second

out of my own wanting.



Behind me, the flow began.

Not day returning, not a pardon:

the sea put its cold mouth

to every footprint I had left.
