What the Persimmon Wants

I have been waiting here
longer than you know,

ripening past the point
you thought was ripe,

past the hard orange promise
you almost bit into —

and would have regretted.
I know about regret.

I have held my tannins
like a closed argument,

like a question that punishes
anyone who rushes it.

But you came back.
You always come back

when the frost has done
what patience could not,

when my skin has gone
soft and almost translucent,

and you pick me up
with both hands, uncertain

whether I am still
of this world or past it —

and here is what I want
to ask you, gently:

who is feeding whom?

Your tongue finds
what I have been making

all autumn without you.
My sweetness enters you

and for a moment
you are changed by what I am,

and I —
I am finally known.

Is this not
what we both wanted?

To be taken
at exactly the right moment,

neither too soon
nor past all giving?
