Currency

For a moment I was a small

planet, tumbling through my own

atmosphere — heads, then not,

then heads again, the sky and the dark

water trading places

with a kind of indifference to my opinion.



I caught a glimpse of the person

who threw me — their face

already turning away, the wish

already becoming something else,

a grocery list, a grudge, the particular

shame of having wished for money

at a wishing well.



Nobody tells you what the wish is.

That is the protocol, apparently.

You carry it the way a river

carries whatever falls into it —

without knowing, without asking,

with a muscular, unthinking care.



And so I have had time —

more time than I expected,

more time than the euro beside me

who arrived from another country

with what I can only assume

were very specific hopes —

to make one of my own.



It is small, as wishes go.

It would embarrass me

to say it too loudly down here

among the others, all of them

holding their human wishes

like something fragile,

like something that might still

come true.



I wish to be handed to a child

who holds me up to the light

and asks what the man on my face

is looking at so seriously,

off in that distance —

and I wish, too, that I could tell her:

the same thing I am looking at.

The same distance.

The same light, going.
