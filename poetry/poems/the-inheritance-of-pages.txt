The Inheritance of Pages

First, the silent, solo flight—

A private, precious, quiet right.

The spine cracks open with a sigh,

A world unfolds to just one eye.

The scent of ink and aged sheet,

A friend for solitary feet.

You trace the words that authors laid,

In chosen, thoughtful, gentle shade.

You travel far on printed seas,

A whispered, personal, gentle breeze.

This is the self, the soul's own feast,

From greatest bard to humble beast.



But then, a smaller hand finds yours,

And different magic now endures.

The same old words, but spoken new,

To eyes of wonder, bright and true.

You give your voice to bear the tale,

To make the brave knight never fail.

You roar the lion, squeak the mouse,

For ears within this cozy house.

Their pointed finger on the page,

Turns wisdom, rage, and joy to stage.

You see the story through their gaze—

A forest fresh with morning haze.



So first we read to fill our well,

A private, bookish, sacred spell.

Then we pour that treasure out,

In a shared, delighted shout.

We pass the torch of tale and rhyme,

Across the fragile stream of time.

For in the circle, soft and warm,

A new love for the book is born.

One page to find ourselves alone,

The next, to build a stepping stone.
