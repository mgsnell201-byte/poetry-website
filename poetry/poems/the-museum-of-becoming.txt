The Museum of Becoming

The mirror swallows your same face each morning—

your teeth, your eyes, the scar above your lip

like a comma in an unfinished sentence.

Your coffee cup holds its familiar weight, steam spiraling

the same tired dance while newspapers yellow

in neat stacks, each headline a door

you never opened.



Clock hands crawl like injured spiders

across their web of numbered hours.

Tuesday bleeds into Wednesday bleeds

into a month of Wednesdays until

the calendar becomes a graveyard

of crossed-out promises, crossed-out

days.



But somewhere between sleep and waking,

the house has grown new shadows.

Your childhood bedroom—a museum

of someone else's dreams. Photographs

on the mantle stare back

with stranger's eyes: your mother's laugh lines

carved deeper now, like riverbeds

in drought.



The tree outside your window stands

where it always stood, yet its branches

reach toward different stars. Your hands,

moving through familiar motions, belong

to someone who learned to hold loss

like water cupped in trembling

palms.



When did the mirror learn

to lie? When did your footsteps

start echoing in hallways you thought

you knew? The walls remember

a different voice calling goodnight

to ghosts who no longer

answer.



Time moves like mist through open fingers—

imperceptible as the lengthening of hair,

quiet as dust accumulating

on books you swore you'd read

again. Until one day you turn

around and find yourself standing

in the ruins of who you used

to be, surrounded by the archaeology

of a life lived one forgotten Wednesday

at a time.
