Soundtrack for a Long Marriage

It arrived a year late to our wedding,
that song—"Precious and Few"—
climbing the radio charts in 1972
while we were already learning
how to share a bathroom sink,
how to sleep through each other's breathing.

I like to think Climax wrote it for us anyway,
that soft-rock prophecy with its gentle guitar,
the way it promises moments together
will be precious and few—
not the marriage itself, mind you,
but those crystalline instants
when you look up from the ordinary
and remember you've chosen this.

The song understood something
we were just beginning to learn:
that a life together is mostly
coffee cups and grocery lists,
but punctuated by these small gems—
a glance across a room,
a hand reaching for yours in the dark,
the way her laugh still catches you
off guard after fifty-some years.

"Precious and few are the moments we two share,"
the chorus insists, and it's right,
even when you've shared breakfast
eighteen thousand times,
even when you know every story
she's going to tell at dinner parties.
The song knew the math—
that abundance and rarity
could somehow be the same thing.

Now it's 2025, and when that melody
drifts from a supermarket speaker
or an oldies station in the car,
I don't hear nostalgia exactly.
I hear a song that arrived
just in time to narrate the truth:
that we are still here,
still precious, still few,
still learning what Climax
tried to tell us all those years ago
about the quiet arithmetic of love.
