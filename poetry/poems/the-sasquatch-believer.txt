The Sasquatch Believer

I want to tell you about the plaster cast
sitting on my desk next to the coffee mug,
the one that holds the imprint of something's foot—

seventeen inches long, five toes spread wide
like the fingers of a hand reaching up
from the bottom of a lake.

My wife calls it my paperweight of delusion,
but I call it evidence, the way a man
might call a single strand of hair a love letter.

You have to understand, I've been looking
for thirty years now, every weekend
driving into the mountains with my camera

and a bag of apples because I read somewhere
that Sasquatch has a fondness for fruit,
which makes sense when you think about it—

who among us doesn't want something sweet
after a long day of avoiding civilization,
of being the world's most successful introvert?

I've collected hair samples, analyzed scat,
spent thousands of hours reviewing footage
of what might be a creature or might be Gary

from the hiking club in a gorilla suit.
But I believe. I believe the way a priest
believes in God, without ever shaking His hand.

And last Tuesday, I finally saw him—
eight feet tall, covered in matted fur,
standing beside my tent at dawn.

We locked eyes for what felt like an hour
but was probably three seconds, and then
he spoke to me, his voice like gravel and honey:

"I've been trying to reach you about
your car's extended warranty."

And reader, I ran.
