The Weaver's Game (Long)

In a forest where shadows twist and play,
A weaver sat by the break of day,
Her loom aglow with threads of fire,
Spinning fates with a quiet desire.
Her fingers danced, her eyes agleam,
Each knot a choice, each thread a dream.

I stumbled upon her, lost in the haze,
Through brambles thick and a twilight maze,
Her voice like silk, she called me near,
"Come, child, see what I craft from fear."
She showed me a tapestry, vast and wide,
Where my life unfolded, a turbulent ride.

Here I was born, a babe in the sun,
There I ran free, my laughter spun,
But then a shadow—a choice gone wrong—
A lover betrayed, a friend turned to song.
The threads turned red, then black as night,
A storm of grief, a withering blight.

"Wait!" I cried, "Can this be undone?
Can you weave me a path where I’ve already won?"
Her smile was sharp, a crescent moon,
"Step into my game, I’ll grant your boon."
She pulled a thread, the air grew thin,
And the forest spun as I fell within.

I stood in a meadow, my past erased,
A crown on my head, a smile on my face,
My lover returned, my friends all near,
The sun so bright, no trace of fear.
But the ground felt hollow, the sky too still,
A creeping dread, a whispering chill.

I turned to run, but the meadow stretched wide,
No edge, no end, just an endless tide.
The weaver appeared, her laughter cold,
"You wanted a tale where you’re brave and bold,
But every thread comes with a cost,
A perfect life means a soul that’s lost."

She snapped her fingers, the scene dissolved,
Back to the forest, my fears unresolved.
I seized her loom, I tore it apart,
"I’ll weave my own fate, I’ll follow my heart!"
The threads unraveled, the air turned black,
But then—a mirror stared right back.

The weaver was me, her face my own,
Her voice my whisper, her craft my throne.
I’d spun my own cage, my fears, my game,
Trapped in a loop, no one to blame.
And as I screamed, the forest fell,
Replaced by a room, a sterile shell.

A hospital bed, a beep so slow,
A coma’s grip, a mind’s cruel show.
The weaver, the forest, the threads of fate—
All dreams I wove while I lay in wait.
My family wept, their voices near,
"She’s gone," they said, "she’s no longer here."
