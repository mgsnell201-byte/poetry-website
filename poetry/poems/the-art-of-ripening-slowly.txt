The Art of Ripening Slowly

If I were a passion fruit,

I'd wear my contradictions on my skin—

purple-brown and wrinkled,

unpretty by conventional measures,

a weathered leather purse

forgotten in someone's closet.



You'd pass me by in produce aisles,

choosing the polished apples,

the blushing peaches,

while I sit patient as a monk,

knowing what treasures

lie beneath my humble shell.



My exterior speaks of storms weathered,

of sun-baked afternoons

and rain-soaked nights,

each dimple a story,

each crease a lesson learned

in the art of ripening slowly.



But crack me open—

go ahead, be brave—

and I'll spill golden secrets,

seeds suspended in amber nectar,

a constellation of possibility

swimming in concentrated sunlight.



I taste like summer lightning,

tart as first love's kiss,

sweet as forgiveness whispered

at three in the morning.

I am the flavor that makes you

close your eyes and remember

something you never knew you'd forgotten.



My fragrance is intoxication itself,

tropical winds carrying promises,

the scent that lingers

on fingers and lips,

that follows you home

and haunts your dreams.



I am not for everyone—

my intensity overwhelms

the timid tongue.

Some find me too much,

too complex,

too unapologetically myself.



But for those who understand

that beauty isn't always

smooth and symmetrical,

that the most profound gifts

often come wrapped

in unlikely packages—



I offer everything:

the sharp awakening of truth,

the sweetness earned

through patience,

the reminder that inside

every weathered exterior

pulses a heart

worth discovering.



So if you see me there,

small and unremarkable,

don't walk away.

Take me home.

Let me teach you

what passion really tastes like.
