The Second Cup

She sets the coffee mug down
with the handle facing my hand,
a gesture she has made
for so many mornings
her fingers know the angle
without consulting her eyes.

The table has recorded
our positions over the years.
The wood has darkened
where my elbow rests,
and the chair legs have scraped
shallow grooves in the floor
at exactly the distance
between her chair and mine.

This morning I reached
for the pepper
and found her fingers
already there,
our hands meeting
over the same wooden mill
for the thousandth time.

We never discussed
where the sugar bowl would live
or how the salt shaker
would migrate to the center,
the way our chairs
have drifted closer
over the years.

Two mugs on the table,
their handles
facing each other.
