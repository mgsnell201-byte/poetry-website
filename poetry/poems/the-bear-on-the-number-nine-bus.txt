The Bear on the Number Nine Bus

A bear boards the bus in London town,
He squeezes through the folding door,
His fur is thick and grizzly brown,
He shuffles past a dozen more.

The passengers all shift aside
And clutch their papers, glance away,
They dare not stare with British pride—
Perhaps someone invited him today?

He sits beside a nervous man
Who coughs politely, checks his phone,
The bear begins to lick a can
Of honey with a happy groan.

The driver calls out "Mind the gap!"
The bear gets off at Trafalgar Square,
A woman whispers, "What a chap,"
But no one dares to say "There's a bear!"

For asking seems incredibly rude—
Perhaps he's someone's special guest?
And so they choose their solitude,
Assuming someone knows him best.
