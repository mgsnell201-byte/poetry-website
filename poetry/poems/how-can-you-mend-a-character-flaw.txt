How Can You Mend a Character Flaw

I've been watching those home improvement shows

where they sand and seal and repaint everything,

where a man with a tool belt can fix a sagging porch

in under thirty minutes, minus commercials.



How can you mend a character flaw?

I want to know if there's a hardware store for that,

an aisle between plumbing and paint

where they sell whatever it takes to stop me

from saying the wrong thing at dinner parties.



The Bee Gees were asking about broken hearts,

which seems straightforward—all that tender

talk of tears and years and living lonely.

But nobody writes ballads about being chronically late

or interrupting people mid-sentence.



I tried affirmations once, standing in the bathroom

saying I am punctual, I am present, I am kind

to my reflection, which looked back at me

with appropriate skepticism, toothpaste on its chin.



My friend swears by therapy. He goes every Thursday,

comes back talking about patterns and triggers

and the inner child, who apparently has a lot to answer for.

Mine is probably still in there somewhere,

forgetting to return the neighbors rake, losing gloves.



Some days I catch myself being patient,

listening without planning my response,

and I think this is it, I've cracked the code,

I'm fixed, I'm whole, I'm finally that person

who remembers birthdays and sends thank-you notes.



Then Tuesday happens, or Wednesday,

or any day that ends in Y,

and there I am again—foot in mouth,

promise broken, apology forming on my lips

like a song I know all the words to.



How can you mend a character flaw?

The honest answer is probably very slowly,

with setbacks and small victories,

with the patience of someone sanding down

a table that's been warped for decades.



Or maybe you just learn to introduce yourself accurately:

Hi, I'm someone who tries, who fails,

who shows up flawed and keeps showing up.

The Bee Gees never got their answer either,

but they sure made it sound beautiful, the asking.
