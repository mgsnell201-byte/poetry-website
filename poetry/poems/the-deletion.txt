The Deletion

I finally deleted your number today.

Not in a fury of taps and swipes,

but with the slow, sure pressure

of a stone settling on a grave.



For months, it was a tiny, digital shrine—

that string of ten digits, a slim shard of light

holding the ghost of your "hello,"

the fossil of your laugh.



My finger hovered like a hesitant witness

over the "confirm" button—a red zero,

a period at the end of a sentence

I never learned how to speak.



This was not an act of forgetting.

It is the opposite.

It is to know your name by heart now,

instead of by memory.



It is to untether you from a signal,

to set the thought of you free

from this cold, glass-and-plastic prison,

back into the wilds of my mind.



It was the smallest of funerals.

No one sent flowers.

Just me, in the silent cathedral of my kitchen,

burying you.
