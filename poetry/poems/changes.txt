Changes

I did not know I was waiting,
which is the purest form of waiting,
the kind that has no clock to watch,
no window to look out of,
only the warm curved fact of everything
pressing in from all sides
like a very small and serious opinion.

Something large moved over me once
and the warmth changed its mind briefly
before coming back,
and I held myself the way you hold
a thought you almost lost —
tightly, without knowing you are holding.

Then the cold arrived,
the way it always does,
without introducing itself,
and I had no word for it
because I had no words for anything yet,
which was, I can see now,
a significant advantage.

The cracking, when it came,
was my idea and not my idea,
the way most important things are,
and suddenly there was a leaf
and the leaf had a name I didn't know
and a bird above it
whose intentions were not friendly,
and hunger,
which explained everything
and nothing
about why I was suddenly here
and moving.

I will not describe what happened next
inside the darkness I made myself —
suffice it to say that I came undone
in the most literal sense,
which philosophers have been gesturing at for centuries
with considerably more words
and less result.

And now I am standing on the outside
of the last self I remember,
wings I did not order
drying slowly in the morning,

and the air is just there,
enormous and available,

and I am almost ready
to find out what I think about that.
