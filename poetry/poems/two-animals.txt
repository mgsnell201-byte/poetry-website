Two Animals

The puppy pants against my shin,

her spotted coat, her tucked thin skin.

I press my palm against her ribs

and feel how her small breathing grips.



She dreams, her paws claw at the air—

a leopard watching somewhere there

inside her blood, that ancient eye,

still guarding, still standing by.



She wakes and presses to my side,

her wet nose cold, her mouth open wide.

She pants, she watches, keeps me near,

the leopard pacing, still, still here.



My chest aches, dull, the IV mark

still greens my arm. The yard goes dark.

She leans against me, warm, her weight,

her flank that rises, falls, and waits.



Two animals, both breathing, pressed

together, her fur against my chest.

Her paw against my open hand,

her breath, her pulse, where we still stand.
