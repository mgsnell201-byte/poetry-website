The Barred Owl

The owl dropped

from the pines

to the clothesline post,

the wood still warm

from the afternoon sun,

and then the head swiveled

and the black eyes

met mine

across the cut lawn

and the hydrangea

going pale

at dusk.



Its talons gripped

the post's split top

where the sheets had dried

that morning,

and the breast feathers

shivered

as though breath

had touched them,

though nothing

stirred.



I stopped

on the back step,

my palm flat

against the door's

peeling paint,

and the owl

watched

while the yard

darkened

and the first cricket

scratched

from the weed bed.



Tell me,

when did you last

stay still enough

for something with talons

to look at you?
