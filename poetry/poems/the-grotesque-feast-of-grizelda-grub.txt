The Grotesque Feast of Grizelda Grub

In the village of Slimington-on-Grime,
Lived Grizelda Grub, who thought it sublime
To collect all things vile, putrid, and gross,
The things that make normal folks hold their nose!

"Oh wonderful!" Grizelda would shriek with delight,
As maggots came crawling into her sight,
She'd scoop them up gently in bowls made of rust,
Mixed with some toenails and gray basement dust.

Her pantry was stocked with delectable treats:
Jars full of earwax and moldy pig's feet,
Pickled eyeballs that stared back at you,
And bottles of bile—a sickening brew!

For breakfast she'd feast on some curdled milk soup,
With lumps that would jiggle and wiggle and droop,
She'd slurp it up loudly with slobbering sounds,
While rats in her kitchen went racing around.

Her lunch was a sandwich of fungus and slime,
With mustard that bubbled and fizzed all the time,
The bread was so moldy it grew its own hair,
And smelled like a skunk mixed with underwear!

But dinner—oh dinner!—was Grizelda's best meal,
She'd cook up a stew that would make strong folks reel:
With boogers and belly-button lint for the base,
And snot from the nose of a walrus's face!

She'd add rotten cabbage that oozed purple goo,
Some socks that had festered inside someone's shoe,
A handful of beetles, still squirming about,
And fish scales that made the whole mixture sprout!

So if you should meet her while walking one day,
Just smile and be polite, then quickly walk away,
For those who stay longer might just get invited,
To dinner—where normal stomachs get frightened!

And though her cuisine makes most people quite queasy,
Old Grizelda proves that being gross can be easy,
She lives life with gusto, with slime, and with grime—
The queen of revolting, disgusting, gross rhyme!
