The Octosyllabic Octopus

(Sung to the tune of "Hang Down Your Head Tom Dooley

by the Kingston Trio)



Hang down your arms, Octavius,

Hang down your arms and weep n' cry,

Your inky verse drowns readers' minds,

Your eight-beat lines make patience sigh.



You wrote your rhymes, Octavius,

In patterns tangled like seaweed strands,

Each line with eight unyielding beats—

A sunken song from murky lands.



The critics came, Octavius,

They shook their heads in weary dread,

"Your syllabic waves of ink

Have drowned our will to forge ahead."



Your publisher, Octavius,

He tore his hair in briny woe,

"These verses flood like tidal surges,

What can we do with your ink's flow?"



So hang your arms down, Octavius,

Let go your stubborn, squid-inked pen,

Try scrawling something fresh and free

Before you sink to depths again.



Yet still he writes, Octavius,

Beneath the dark and churning sea,

With eight arms spilling endless ink—

His doomed eight-beat poetry.
