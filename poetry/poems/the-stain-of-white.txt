The Stain of White

In shades of gray where choices dwell,

We cloak our lies as "kind" or "well,"

But paint them how we will, they stay—

False threads that fray the ties we sway.



Why white? As if the hue could cleanse

The rot where honest words commence.

No sugar coats a bitter core—

A lie remains, though masked as more.



"Your cooking shines!" (though salt forgot),

"You’ve aged like wine!" (when time has not).

Each "harmless" untruth, soft and sweet,

Is theft—of truth, of minds that meet.



You say, "But kindness justifies!"

Then watch the doubt grow in their eyes.

For even mercy, built on air,

Leaves ghosts of "What else isn’t there?"



The doctor lies to spare despair,

The friend hides faults to "show they care"—

But hearts, once burned by hollow light,

Grow cold to all that shines too bright.



Where’s the line? It’s drawn at none.

No lie forgives what’s left undone.

The smallest slit begins the fray—

No bind so firm it won’t betray.



So stand where light and shadows meet,

And choose the words that burn, not cheat.

For trust, once torn by "soft" disguise,

Is trust that never heals—but lies.
