The Green Edge

Dawn comes first as a rumor at the railyard—

gold licking the tracks to life,

while nightshift workers wipe their brows

and become, for one breath, gilded.



The river, though it swallows

rust and rain in equal measure,

still births herons from its mist—

each wingbeat a stubborn letter

in light's unfinished alphabet.



The bulldozer naps in its muddy cradle,

but already the wild grapevine

stitches its green thread

through the chain-link gospel,

mending what was torn.



Children at the bus stop

toss their laughter like seeds

between the cracks of commerce,

while the oldest oak on the block

leans down to slip an acorn

into the wind's pocket—

a small, planted fury.



What is hope but the earth's

relentless arithmetic?

Steel decays, but the sunflower

still knows east from west.



Even the scuffed soles

of the shoeless man

print their own compass rose

on the pavement—

his shadow stretching longer,

darker, surer,

as the sun climbs its ladder.



And when at last the streetlights

shudder into daytime silence,

you'll find the proof:

a blade of grass in the pavement's split lip,

its green edge

incandescent.
