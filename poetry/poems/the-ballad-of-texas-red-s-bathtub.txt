The Ballad of Texas Red's Bathtub

Texas Red, a dusty soul, with eyes like sunlit sand,
Roped in a porcelain palace, a bathtub in the land.
No gilded throne, no velvet sheets, just copper gleaming bright,
But in that tub, with sudsy bliss, he bathed in pure delight.

He'd sing of mesas, canyons deep, and stars that blazed so bold,
His washboard rhythm, splashing tunes, a story to be told.
The desert wind would whisper secrets, through cactus spines so sharp,
As Texas Red, with soapy grin, would scrub away the scarp.

His boots, they'd rest upon the rim, his hat upon a hook,
And in that bathtub, free and clean, he'd find a hidden nook.
A solace from the sun's harsh glare, a haven from the trail,
Where dust and sweat would melt away, and dreams would softly sail.

One day, a rustle in the brush, a giggle in the air,
A curious sight, a petticoat, and eyes of sunlit hair.
Texas Red, he blushed and grinned, his sudsy song grew shy,
But in that bathtub, shared with her, beneath the desert sky,
He found a reason, warm and sweet, to keep that tub so grand,
For in its depths, he'd learned to love, not just the dusty land.
