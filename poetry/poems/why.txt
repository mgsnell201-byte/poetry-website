Why

I have watched the song sparrow
on the fence post all week,
the way it grips the rail
with its wiry feet
and tilts its russet head
and opens the small hinge of its beak
before the sun has cleared the trees.

I do not think it is joy,
though joy is in it.
I think the body of the bird
is a door that only opens outward,
and what we call singing
is what happens
when nothing is held back.

And the willow —
I used to think it was grieving,
the way it lets its branches down
into the pond,
that green curtain
trailing in the shallows.

But have you stood beneath one
and watched the way each limb
reaches toward the water
the way a hand reaches
for a face
it has not touched in years?

The willow is not weeping.
It is bending
toward what it loves.

And the wind that comes
across the marsh with its mouth open,
pressing itself
against the cattails, the clapboard,
the stiff face of the pond —

I think it cries out
because it has no body of its own
and must press against everything it crosses
just to know
that it is here.

Meanwhile the morning
opens around all of us —
the sparrow on its post,
the willow in its leaning,
the wind on its way to the sea.

You who have been given
a throat and a body
and two good hands
to press against the rough bark
of this life —

tell me,
what would you do
but sing?
