The Kitchen Window

I was thinking about love again

this morning, the way it refuses

to stay in the places we assign it.

I stood at the kitchen window

holding yesterday’s coffee,

watching a single drop of rain

slide down the pane, hesitate

at a smudge, then keep going

as if it had somewhere better to be.



The book you left on the sill

lay open to a page you never finished,

a margin crowded with your faint pencil marks—

little arrows pointing at nothing,

a question mark curled like a cat.

I didn’t read it. I just liked

how the paper had softened

where your thumb must have rested,

the way cheap ink had bled

into the fibers, turning one word

into a small gray pond.



Outside, the neighbor’s dog barked once

and then thought better of it.

A car went by, spraying water

that caught the light for half a second

before it disappeared.

Nothing dramatic. Just the usual

Tuesday traffic of small things

doing what small things do.



And yet I stood there longer than I meant to,

holding the mug that has your lipstick

faint as a rumor on the rim.

I wondered if love is less like the ocean

we always claim it is

and more like this—

a slow leak under the sink,

a bookmark made from a torn ticket stub,

the quiet refusal of a drop of rain

to fall straight down.



I closed the book gently,

set it back exactly where you left it.

Some things, I’ve learned,

you don’t press too hard

or they stop telling you the truth.
