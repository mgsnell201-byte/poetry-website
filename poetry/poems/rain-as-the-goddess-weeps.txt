Rain as the Goddess Weeps

She sends the rain not out of grief
but ritual—the way she wept
for Tammuz, and will weep for each
of those she marked, and those she kept

too briefly. I have stood in fields
where rain fell warm as her regard
once fell on me. The body yields.
It learns to open. This is hard

to speak of plainly, but I must:
love enters as rain enters earth,
not asked for, merely given. Trust
is not required. Nor is worth.

The wound she made, no doubt, still bleeds.
The scar, at least, has learned to shine.
Rain drums the roof. The garden needs
whatever falls. And so do I. So mine

is the wet face, the sodden coat,
the standing in it like a fool
who knew the words but missed the note—
who thought that he could keep his cool.

She is the storm. I am the field.
The rain comes down. I do not shield.
