The Thorny Process of Letting Go

Like roots that drink from poisoned wells,

We clutch what cuts us to the bone—

The bramble-love that tears and swells,

The wounds we tend and call our own.



What is this pull, this buried need

That drags us back to dig and dwell

Where thorns we planted still take seed

In soil that reeks of old farewell?



Each day we tend with careful hands

What long ago should have died—

The bitter fruit our grief demands,

The thorns we've trained to grow inside.



We save the last voicemail's thread,

Each playback wearing thin the sound—

To delete would mean we've shed

The only proof that can be found.



Perhaps we fear the hollow space

Where quiet grows when fury dies,

The emptiness we'd have to face

If we stopped feeding old goodbyes.



But we can choose what seeds to sow—

The hands that claw must learn to peel.

What starves will wither; what we grow

Depends on what we no longer seal."
