Shut Up

I had been at it for weeks,

holding a cracker near the cage

and repeating simple words—

"hello, pretty bird, hello"—



the way you might teach a child

except the child doesn't stare back

with that flat, prehistoric eye

that sees you and sees through you

at the same time.



I was trying "water" that Tuesday morning,

the coffee going cold on the desk,

when he said it—

cleanly, without hesitation,

the way a judge pronounces a verdict—



"shut up."



I put the cracker down.

Outside, a car moved slowly past the window.

A pigeon landed on the wire across the street,

looked around once,

and flew off without explanation.



The parrot turned on his perch

and faced the wall,

done with me for the day,

done with the whole project of language

and what I had imagined it was for.



The cracker sat on the desk beside the cold coffee.

Somewhere in the house, a door

opened by itself in a small draft,

then slowly closed.
