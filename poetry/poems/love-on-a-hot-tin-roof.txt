Love on a Hot Tin Roof

You perch where the sun-struck shingles gleam,
a breath away from burning, yet you stay—
the roof remembers every whispered dream,
each vow that slipped and melted in the day.

The tin exhales the heat of yesteryears,
It sighs like echoes warping time’s cruel spine.
You trace the scars of laughter, salted tears,
A love that stuck like tar to summer’s vine.

The air is thick with longing, slow and sweet,
Like syrup on the skin it cannot soothe.
The past curls up like smoke around your feet,
A ghost that lingers in the heat of truth.

You press your palm to where the metal sings,
A tune of all the fires it could not keep.
The evening hums with rust on broken wings,
And memory is just the art of sleep.

So let the stars fall silent from the sky,
Like paint that flakes from promises once bright.
You’ll hold what heat remains before you die,
Though dawn will bleach it all to bone-white light.

And when the roof forgets the weight you bore,
When time has warped the nails and drowned the rain,
You’ll be the shadow stretched across its core—
The love that burned, then cooled, and burned again.
