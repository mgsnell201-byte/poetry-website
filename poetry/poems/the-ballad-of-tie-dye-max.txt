The Ballad of Tie-Dye Max

He came in a van, a paisley haze,
To find the field of Woodstock's craze.
His ticket was lost, but he paid with a grin,
And a slightly suspicious, home-grown gift within.

He lost his ticket, shrugged, "No sweat,"
Through mud and rain, a grateful wet.
He shared his peach, a fuzzy prize,
With sun-kissed strangers, blissful eyes.

A band played loud, the earth did thrum,
He air-guitared a psychic strum.
The bass, he swore, aligned his soul,
And made his broken spirit whole.

He hugged a cop, said, "Be at peace,"
Then fought a tie-dye for release
From a patch of mud that held him tight,
A groovy, brown, and deep delight.

He talked for hours bout the vibe,
With a girl who’d danced with Lucy's tribe.
They planned a trip in her VW Bug,
Then lost the thread and gave a shrug.

Now, when it’s mentioned, Max just sighs,
A distant, hazy, fond surmise.
"I swear I heard Jimi play…
…or was that just the second day?"
