The Space You Left

I've stopped setting the table for two,
though the salt shaker still stands
where your hand would reach for it
without looking.

Your slippers have migrated
from their post by the door
to the closet's far corner -
not put away,
just resting.

I drink my coffee black now,
having forgotten the exact number of sugars
you took after all these years.
The spoon still tinks against the cup
the way it did when you stirred yours,
a sound I pretend not to hear.

The bed has become
an ocean of cold sheets,
and I navigate its waves
by starlight,
learning new constellations
in the ceiling's cracks.

Your favorite chair
has begun to forget the shape of you,
its cushions slowly rising
like bread dough proving overnight.
I don't correct them.

Sometimes I catch myself
humming that song you hated,
the one you'd groan at
and change the station.
Now I let it play to the end,
smiling at the empty passenger seat
where your protests should be.

The seasons turn without consulting me,
bringing back the flowers you planted
with their stubborn persistence.
They bloom as brightly
as if you were still here to see them,
which is how I know
you are.
