The Festival of Dusk and Dawn

The sun dips,

a Holi maestro spilling its palette,

saffron cascading,

a molten river kissing the earth goodbye.

Red flares soft,

a tiger’s breath glowing amber,

yielding to the sky’s next act.

Purple blooms,

a lotus unfurling in twilight’s embrace,

its velvet hush a bridge between worlds.

Orange twirls,

a ribbon of flame unwound,

tying the day’s last light to the horizon.

Pink drifts,

a sprinkle of rosewater mist,

settling gentle as a prayer.



Then the moon rises,

no mere shadow of the sun,

but a festival of its own hues.

Silver ignites,

a cascade of molten pearls,

washing the night in quiet radiance.

Ivory gleams,

a swan’s wing brushing the stars,

its soft arc a serenade to stillness.

Pale gold flickers,

a buttery thread woven through,

the sun’s echo cradled in lunar glow.

Blue whispers,

a sapphire veil thin as breath,

draping the moon in mystic calm.



The colors dance—

sunset’s riot softening into moonrise’s hymn,

a handover of pigments,

vibrant to serene,

a Holi sky where day and night entwine,

each shade a celebration,

each glow a gift.
