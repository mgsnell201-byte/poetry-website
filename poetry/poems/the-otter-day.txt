The Otter Day

All morning the otter has been swimming
in the cold creek, diving and rising,
shaking the water from its whiskers,
being exactly what it is—
nothing more, nothing less.

I have been standing here on the bank
watching, my hands in my pockets,
my breath making small clouds in the autumn air,
and I keep thinking: this is enough.
This is what I came for.

The otter doesn't know I'm here.
It doesn't perform or hesitate.
It simply moves through the current,
its body a dark arrow,
its purpose clear as the water itself.

How long have I been trying
to be something other than what I am?
How many years spent apologizing
for the shape of my life,
the quiet way I move through the world?

But the otter—
the otter has no such concerns.
It catches a fish, eats it on a rock,
slides back into the stream,
and the morning continues, blessed and simple.

You do not have to be remarkable.
You do not have to save anyone or anything.
You only have to let your body
do what it was made to do—
to swim, to breathe, to be present in the cold water.

The sun is climbing now above the trees.
The otter dives again, surfaces again,
its fur sleek and shining.
Somewhere a bird is calling.
Somewhere the world is asking nothing of us.

Tell me, isn't this enough—
to stand here watching, to feel
the morning air on your face,
to know that you, too, are swimming
in the one wild current of your life?
