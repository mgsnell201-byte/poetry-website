The Weight of Questions

What if the self is just a story
we tell the mirror each morning,
a fiction so convincing
we forget we are the author?

I watch my hand write these words—
whose will moves the pen?
The neurons firing in sequence,
or something that observes the firing?

Time drips forward like honey,
each moment dissolving
into the next, yet here I sit
convinced of my continuity.

Was I the same person
who woke this morning,
or am I a fresh consciousness
inheriting yesterday's memories?

The universe expands
into what? More universe?
And if it contracts tomorrow,
will meaning collapse inward too?

I love, therefore I suffer.
I think, therefore I doubt.
I exist, therefore I question
the very ground of existing.

Perhaps wisdom lies not
in answering but in learning
to hold the weight
of unanswerable questions—

to dance with uncertainty,
to find peace in the pause
between heartbeats, in the space
where knowing meets unknowing.

The cave wall flickers
with shadows we mistake
for truth, but what if
the shadows are enough?

What if the real freedom
is not in breaking chains
but in choosing, moment
by moment, what to believe?
