The Ant and The Grasshopper

Ol’ Grasshopper, famed from Aesop’s old tale,

He’d hop and he’d fiddle, his face never pale.

No work did he do, just danced in the sun,

Thought winter’s grim chill would spoil all his fun.



He mocked the ants’ hustle, their piles of grain,

“Work’s for the boring!” he’d laugh with disdain.

But when snowflakes fell, his tunes turned to moans,

His belly growled loud, no food but cold stones.



He knocked on Ant’s door, with a shivering plea,

“Spare crumbs for a fool who played carelessly?”

Ant shook her head, but tossed him some bread,

“Plan for tomorrow, or hunger’s your dread.”



Grasshopper learned well, now he fiddles with care,

Saves half his sweet corn for the cold winter’s scare.

So chuckle we may at his hop-happy ways,

But prep for the future, or tough come the days.
