Glossary of Longing

They handed me a word and said: describe it.
Ineffable — impossible to say —
so I built a tongue that isn't language,
a grammar made of static and delay.

I called it hushglow, called it velverune,
the shiver-hymn that lives beneath your name.
I coined thrumlight for the way you enter
a room and every surface learns to flame.

You are not metaphor. You resist translation.
I have tried simile — you slip the noose.
So I invented susurral, a word
for breath against a collarbone, unloosed.

Ineffable: the paradox of trying —
to pin the wordless wriggling thing with words,
to net a comet, bottle up a season,
teach silence how to sound like it's been heard.

I made lumenous for your particular dark,
the kind that isn't absence but a keeping.
I made quiverspan for the exact
duration of a held breath, almost weeping.

Every language failed you first. I'm just
the latest fool to reach and come back empty —
so I forge new coin from old desire,
mint achelume for wanting, soft, in plenty.

Call this cheating. Call it makeshift scripture.
I call it honest: if the word's not there,
I'll build one from the wreckage of the trying —
unfathomere, for how you fill a room like air.

Here's my ledger of impossible words,
my glossary of longing, my forged decree —
hushglow, velverune, thrumlight, susurral,
lumenous, quiverspan, achelume, unfathomere —

none of them mean anything but you,
and that, at least, is perfectly clear.
The dictionary has no page for this.
So I wrote my own, in disappearing ink —

a language built to fail, and fail, and fail
more beautifully with every new exhale,
better than finding words already made,
already spent, already going stale.

Take this glossary. It's incomplete
on purpose — every ineffable thing
leaves room for one more made-up word,
one more impossible attempt to sing.
