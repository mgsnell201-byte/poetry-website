The Meeting

I remember the coffee was terrible that morning,

the kind that tastes like it's been sitting

on the burner since dawn, which it probably had.

Someone made a joke about it—I forget who—

and we all laughed, the way you do

when you're waiting for something important to begin.



There were eight of us around the table.

I sat near the window where I could see

a gardener trimming the hedges outside,

his shears making that soft clicking sound

that seemed louder than it should have been.



The President spoke about acceptable losses,

a phrase I'd heard before but never really

considered the way you might consider

a piece of furniture you walk past every day

until someone asks you to describe it.



Acceptable. Loss. Two words

that don't belong together, like saying

"the friendly fire" or "the necessary evil."

But there they were, side by side on the page,

and someone was reading them aloud

in a voice that suggested they made perfect sense.



I thought about my daughter that morning—

she would have been four—asking me at breakfast

why the sky was blue, and me trying to explain

something about wavelengths and atmosphere,

knowing even as I spoke that the real answer

was simply "because it is."



There was a vote. I don't remember

if I raised my hand or just nodded,

but I remember the gardener outside

had moved to a different hedge by then,

still making that soft clicking sound,

still doing the work he came to do.



Years later, I would read about the shadows

burned into walls, the permanent silhouettes

of people going about their morning routines,

and I would think about that meeting,

the terrible coffee, the clicking shears,

and how we all went home for dinner that night

as if we'd done nothing more remarkable

than approve the minutes from the previous meeting.



I still don't know if there was another way.

But I know that somewhere in Hiroshima,

while we were deciding, a mother was probably

making breakfast, a gardener was probably

trimming hedges, and someone was probably

telling a joke that would make everyone laugh.



I know this doesn't make it better.

I'm just telling you what I remember.
