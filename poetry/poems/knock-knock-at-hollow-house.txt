Knock-Knock at Hollow House

I wore a jester’s cap and tail—
A black cat stitched from midnight’s veil.
Rachael, raccoon, in witch’s guise,
Had candle-flicker in her eyes.

And Freddie—fox beneath a sheet—
Made silence echo at his feet.
We tiptoed past the lanterns’ leer,
Each pumpkin carved to mock our fear.

The house loomed like a whispered sin,
Its windows blinked with bone-white grin.
I knocked—a sound, a knuckle’s crack—
Sent whispers crawling down my back.

No voice replied, but curtains swayed—
Like breath from something long decayed.
A mirror flashed—a face, then two—
Not ours, but something passing through.

Its smile was mine, but stretched too wide—
Like laughter drowned in formaldehyde.
We fled. The woods grew teeth and tongue.
The path behind us came undone.

And though no soul had said hello,
That night, my dreams began to glow.
Its voice returned in whispered rhyme—
“You knocked too late. Come back next time.”
