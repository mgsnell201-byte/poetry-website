The Rooms We Leave

The boxes came on Saturday

We lined them by the wall

Dad took the pictures down at last

And left the shadows tall

Her Sunday dress was in the chest

Her scarf still smelled of rain

My sister pressed it to her face

Then folded it again



The rooms we leave still hold her voice

The floors still know her tread

We pack the plates, we wrap the frames

We fold the quilt upon her bed

No ribbon tied around a box

Can hold the years we've known

We take the love she left behind

And leave the house alone



I found her gloves beneath the sink

My brother read her recipe

With flour on his sleeve

We laughed until the laughter broke

And nobody could leave

At dusk we sat around her chair

And held her wedding band

No one could bear to put it down

We passed it hand to hand

Then quietly Dad sang her song

His voice began to shake

And through the window roses swayed

Like she was still awake

No words could make it right

Her scarf was warm around my neck

Like her, it held me tight
