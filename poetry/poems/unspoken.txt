Unspoken

She does not speak her need, nor should she—
Words are the cool web that would betray
What silence holds more close than any vow.
A glance across the room will do, or how
Her hand finds mine when dusk comes on, the way
Old soldiers know their ground instinctively.

We have made peace with what cannot be said.
The lexicon we've learned runs deep as bone:
Her shoulder touching mine means "I am here";
My palm against her cheek translates as "near
Enough". We do not name what we have known—
To name it would be to wish the language dead.

Some loves announce themselves with fanfare, claim
Their territory loud as conquering kings.
Ours keeps its counsel. When she brings me tea
Exactly when the morning requires it, we
Have spoken volumes. All the murmuring
Of sweethearts seems to us a kind of shame.

The Goddess does not ask to be adored
In words—she knows them false, however fine.
Better the brief pressure of her hand,
The knowing smile I need not understand
But answer with a touch along her spine.
We serve her best by what we leave unscored.

And so we go through days like monks who've taken
Some vow of silence, though we have not sworn
To anything but this: that love runs truer
In the unspoken current than the fewer
Or greater protestations others make each morn.
We are most faithful when we are forsaken

By speech. Last night she woke and touched my face—
No cause, no reason, just her fingers pressed
Against my cheek as if to check I breathed.
I did not ask her why. We have bequeathed
Our meanings to the dark. It knows us best.
The word for love is: leaving it no trace.
