Autumn’s Ache and Winter’s Silence

Between your empty chair at dawn

And mine that waits in time,

I wake to count each labored breath

Like coins I’ll never find.



Autumn lingers in the air—

its ache within my chest.

I touch where you once lay, then face

the mirror’s quiet theft.



Your absence carves my hollowed frame,

my lungs clutch shallow rest.

Death’s echo haunts our silent hall,

a chill I can’t suppress.



Yet here between these grieving walls

I trace your fading tune.

Your lamp still burns with trembling light—

its glow a waning moon.



I switch it off as dawn arrives—

the dark feels kinder yet.

The window holds soft light at noon,

where time and memory met.



Your voice still lingers in the glass,

my pulse frays thin with pain.

As evening bleeds to night I hold

this ache like April rain.



Losing you rehearsed my soul

to face its own embrace.

Winter’s hush now walks these halls—

a silence I retrace.
