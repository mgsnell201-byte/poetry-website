Autumn's Masquerade

In my town, autumn lurks 'round every bend,
A trickster god, making nature pretend.
Old oaks dress in reds that'd make cardinals stare,
While birches go blonde, as if they just didn't care.

The wind's gotten nippy, with teeth sharp and cool,
Whispering secrets learned in summer school.
Squirrels dash madly, their cheeks puffed with loot,
Hoarding acorns and nuts, winter's backup pursuit.

At Farmer Joe's patch, pumpkins sprawl plump and bright,
Sun-kissed orbs glowing orange in fading light.
Each gourd a promise of lanterns to come,
Of spicy pies and doorsteps made fun.

Nearby, apples dangle, red-cheeked and gleaming,
Their branches bent low, with sweetness teeming.
Crisp scents mingle - fruit, smoke, and spice,
A sensory brew of autumn's device.

Geese honk goodbye, sounding drunk as they fly,
Their V's more like scribbles across the paint-splattered sky.
Below, the hills roll out in a patchwork so bright,
As if autumn had spilled all its colors overnight.

Days shrink like my socks fresh out of the dryer,
As porch pumpkins flicker with mischievous fire.
Autumn reigns now, in all its splendid glory,
A beautiful disaster, telling summer's last story.
