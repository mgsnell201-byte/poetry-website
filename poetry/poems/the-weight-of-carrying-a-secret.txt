The Weight of Carrying a Secret

Beneath my ribs—a stone takes residence
With edges sharp against my breathing wall,
Each swallow carrying lead through narrow paths
While shoulders bow beneath this silent mass.

The weight grows dense with morning's first approach
As consciousness returns to find it there—
This burden wrapped in flesh and sinew tight,
A gravity that pulls my spine toward earth.

My chest constricts around its jagged truth,
The secret pressing outward from within—
But why do I hold fast to what burns through
My lungs like captured smoke, like borrowed flame?

The muscle memory of carrying loads
Knows nothing of this phantom architecture:
How words unspoken gather substance here
And transform to iron in my hollow spaces.

At night it shifts—settles deep in my bones,
A fever without heat, an ache unnamed.
My body bends around what can't be told,
Creating caverns where the silence dwells.

Yet still I bear this contraband of thought—
This freight of knowledge wrapped in flesh and time.
What makes us choose to house what wounds us so?
Until I am the vessel it requires.
