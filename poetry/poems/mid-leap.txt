Mid-Leap

He hammers shoes from dusk to dawn,
then sets his hammer-rhythms on
his heels—and does the sean-nós-stomp
through clover-pots, stone, and sun-streaked air—
the bog and swamp? His spirit’s there!

His foot lifts up—
will it come down?
He beckons with his toe at town.
A clover’s stuck between his lace,
spreading luck all over the place!

The craobh goes TWEEDLE-
EEDLE-EE,
the stones remember, so does he—
each step the same as hammered nails:
tap-tap-tap where the music spins!

His súil goes wide,
his súil goes bright,
he’s dancing all the old ones’ night—
and when his foot comes down at last,
the ground remembers every blast!

And from the arch—black as a púca’s grin—
a shadow taps its foot... and joins the din.
