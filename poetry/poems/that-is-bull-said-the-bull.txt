That is Bull said the Bull

I like to think the first bull to hear the phrase

"That is Bull" must have been standing

in a field somewhere, chewing some grass,

when a farmer pointed at a broken wagon wheel

and said those exact words.



And the bull looked up, confused,

wondering what he had to do with any of this—

the wheel, the wagon, the man's frustration—

as if his entire species had just been conscripted

into the service of human disappointment.



Now every time someone dismisses a story

or waves away an exaggeration,

there's a bull in the metaphor, standing there

like an innocent bystander dragged into court,

guilty by association with... what exactly?



Stubbornness? The tendency to charge?

Or maybe it's the other thing,

the barnyard thing we're too polite to mention

in daily conversation, though we're happy enough

to invoke it when we think someone's not telling the truth.



Either way, I feel for the bulls,

how they've become shorthand for nonsense,

their muscular bodies and dark eyes

somehow containing all our human doubt,

all the times we've heard something that didn't add up.



And I wonder if somewhere in a pasture right now

there's a bull thinking, "That is Human,"

watching us scurry around with our little schemes,

our elaborate excuses, our wagon wheels that keep breaking,

trying to figure out what we have to do with anything at all.
