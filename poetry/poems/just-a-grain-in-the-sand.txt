Just a Grain in the Sand

Among ten thousand grains, I feel alone—
A single speck in this vast field of stone,
Where countless others glimmer, sharp and bright,
Yet none can touch me in this endless night.

We drift in silence, countless though we are,
Each grain a world, each world a distant star—
The weight of solitude grows soft and brown,
As isolation's gravity pulls down.

Perhaps there's beauty in this crowded space,
Where multitudes can never touch a face—
The way we nestle close yet never meet,
Like strangers passing on a city street.

I taste the salt of tears I cannot shed,
Feel hollow spaces where connection fled—
These shifting masses whisper what they've known:
That even in the crowd, we die alone.

So let me rest among these shifting shores,
Where waves erase what permanence implores—
For in this gentle dissolution's art,
I find the tender breaking of my heart.
