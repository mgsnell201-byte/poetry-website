Dusk on the Shore

The ocean hums beneath the fading sun,
Its waves roll in, unhurried, to the shore.
The light is soft, a glow of amber flame,
That dances on the water’s endless face.

A gull swoops down, its wings cut through the dusk,
Its call a thin, sharp note against the tide.
The crabs move slow, their claws like tiny flags,
As if they mark the edge of sea and land.

What is it in the sea that holds the mind?
Its ceaseless motion, or its hidden depths?
The dolphins weave through the surf, their bodies sleek,
Like shadows playing in the dying light.

I stand upon the beach, my feet in sand,
And feel the pull of something vast and old.
The sun slips low, its fire now nearly gone,
And stars begin to speckle out the sky.

The tide keeps on, its rhythm never still,
As if it speaks a language I can’t learn.
A fish breaks free, a fleeting arc of silver,
Then dives back to the dark from which it came.

The dusk grows deep, the world feels strange and wide.
I turn for home, the salt still on my lips.
