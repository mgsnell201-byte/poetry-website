What the Water Remembers

She isn’t sleeping—

she’s listening to the crack in the sky

pour its slow confession

into the sea.



Her dress, half salt, half ghost,

floats like a surrender flag

or a bride who missed her vows.



The rocks cradle her, but barely—

as if she’s already less body

than boundary.



When the tide pulls,

it doesn’t tug at fabric,

but at the space between

almost and gone.



One day, the sky will finish breaking.

One day, the ocean will forget

which of them was the reflection.



Until then, she lies there—

not waiting, not weeping,

just being the question

the world isn’t ready

to ask aloud.
