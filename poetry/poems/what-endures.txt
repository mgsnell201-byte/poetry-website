What Endures

In Abydos, wrapped in linen fine and old,
Queen Weret rests beside her cat of gold.
The priests had carved her name in stone with care,
and inscribed  the feline's name right there.

Three thousand years the darkness held them close,
the queen who ruled, the creature she loved most.
Her tomb contained the oils, the bread, the wine,
but extra space was made for the feline.

The cat wore beads of turquoise at its throat,
had golden claws and silk Egyptian coat.
A bowl of alabaster by its head,
as if it might still need to be fed.

Today the scholars come to contemplate
this woman's power, her choices, and her fate.
They study hieroglyphs and royal seals,
debate what ancient queenship reveals.

But in the museum gift shop down below,
it's stuffed toy cats that steal the entire show—
while Weret's postcards gather dust on shelves,
her cat's the one that practically sells itself.
