A Cliffside Walk

The wind hums a sad song, a low and lonely wail,
It tugs her frayed coat under a cold, pale moon’s veil.
The path’s a thin line carved where the cliff’s edge falls,
One slip, and the dark drop would silence all her calls.

Her thoughts swirl like the wild sea crashing far below,
A storm of old hurts, of dreams she’ll never know.
She came for the quiet, for the raw and biting air,
To shed the heavy load of her heart’s deep despair.

She stops at the cliff’s rim, feels the pull of the deep,
The whisper of nothing, where all pain goes to sleep.
A life of small wounds, a love that broke and strayed,
Seems answered by the waves of the salt-soaked bay.

She shuts her weary eyes, feels hope slip and blur,
And braces for the fall, giving in to the lure.
The tale feels familiar, a sad and worn-out scene—
A shadow on the cliff, lost to the sea’s cold green.

She leans to the void, ready to let it win…
But her shoe snags a stone, and the world comes rushing in.
In that jolt of fear, her wide eyes catch the spark,
Not of endings, but of life, burning fierce in the dark.

And in that split-second, with her heart’s frantic beat,
She sees not death’s call, but a life still incomplete.
Her hand claws a root, rough and twisted with age,
A lifeline in the chaos, a tether to her rage.

She pulls back from the edge, collapses on firm ground,
And shakes with a new sound—a sob, alive, profound.
Not a cry of defeat, but a fierce, thankful roar,
For the world’s bright pulse she won’t ignore anymore.

The end wasn’t falling. The end was her fight.
The story wasn’t leaving, but clinging to the light.
A heart that gripped the root, that chose to push and strive,
Found, teetering on the edge, the fire to stay alive.

She walks back from the precipice, slow but steady and sure,
Carrying not the end, but a soul that will endure.
