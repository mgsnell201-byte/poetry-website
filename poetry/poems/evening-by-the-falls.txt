Evening by the Falls

The water tumbles hard from cragged stone,
A boy, alone, I stand where mist ascends,
The evening light through spruce and pine is thrown,
Its golden haze where falling water bends.

The wind hums low, a rustle in the leaves,
Like whispers of a choice I’ll soon regret—
To chase the rainbow’s arc that water weaves,
Or hold my place where firmer paths are set.

The birds pipe clear, their notes a fleeting call,
While insects drone in circles near the spray.
I watch the colors dance where waters fall,
And wonder if I’ll lose myself today.

The woods are deep, their shadows cool and dense,
Each step I take could lead to joy or cost.
The falls keep singing, wild with eloquence,
Their voice a pull toward something nearly lost.

I pause, my heart half-caught by what I see—
The fleeting gleam, the mist, the endless sound.
To wander free or turn back sensibly?
The falls don’t care which path my feet have found.
