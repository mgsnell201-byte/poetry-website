Never Built for Clouds

They cut the strings the year the soldiers came,
Hung the hollow frame above the hall.
Said a people with no music left to play
Would soon forget they were people at all.

But a boy kept the tuning in his fingertips,
Played the air above the empty wood,
And every soul who’d lost the name that year
Heard it whole, and understood

It was never built for clouds.
It was built for carrying all the weight
A voice can’t hold alone,
And the names it’s scared to sing.

So let it ring, let it ring, not for the angels.
Let it ring for the ones who stay,
For the broken, for the grieving, for the ones still standing
At the threshold, unafraid.

It’s a door with strings across it,
Every hand that touches it walks through.
Let it ring, let it ring.
It was never heaven’s, it was always for me and you.

Across the sea, a griot tunes his kora
Under a baobab by the edge of town,
Sings a thousand years of father’s father’s names
So not a one of them can ever be lost or drowned.

The thumb keeps the rhythm, fingers tell the story:
Weddings, wars, and who still owes a cow.
His heart talks back, it teases, it remembers,
Every praise song is a promise: I’m here now.

It was never built for clouds.
It was built for carrying all the weight
A voice can’t hold alone,
And the names it’s scared to sing.

So let it ring, let it ring, not for the angels.
Let it ring for the ones who stay,
For the storyteller, the mourner, the stubborn ones
Still singing at the threshold, unafraid.

It’s a door with strings across it,
Every hand that touches it walks through.
Let it ring, let it ring.
It was never heaven’s, it was always for me and you.

Now a woman wheels her harp down a hospice hallway,
Room 12, and an old man’s breath like paper-thin.
Once a soldier, once a father, and now afraid,
Restless as a king with the dark closing in.

She doesn’t play a hymn, she doesn’t play a lullaby,
Just a slow, uneven river of a tune,
And his hands stop shaking on the blanket
Like David never left the room.

But a string will snap sometimes, mid-phrase, mid-breath,
A crack like a sentence left undone,
And the silence that follows is part of the song,
Not a failure, not a fault,
Not the end of the one.

Because a harp that only ever soothes
Would be a liar at the door.
Every note you love is already leaving.
That’s what the strings are for:
To make the leaving beautiful,
To teach the leaving how to sing, to soar.

So let it ring, let it ring, not for the angels.
Let it ring for the ones who stay,
For the boy with the empty frame,
The griot, the dying, and the ones who sit and wait.

It’s a door with strings across it,
Every hand that touches it walks through.
Let it ring, let it ring.
It was never heaven’s, it was always for me and you.

The soldier’s boy, the griot’s thumb,
The hand beside the bed,
One harp, a thousand doors,
And every one of them said:
Stay a little longer.
