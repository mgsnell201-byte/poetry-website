The Blind Date

I have been thinking about the blind date,
not the kind where two strangers meet
in a coffee shop with sweaty palms and rehearsed anecdotes,
but the other kind—the fruit that cannot see.

Picture it there in the bowl on your kitchen counter,
that wrinkled brown oval among the bright oranges
and the self-satisfied apples,
unable to admire its own sweetness,
oblivious to the sunlight streaming through your window.

It has traveled so far—from some palm tree
in California or Tunisia, dried under a sun
it could never witness, boxed and shipped
in darkness it couldn't distinguish from light.

And now it sits there, sightless,
which is probably for the best
because if it could see, it might notice
how often you reach past it for the banana,
how you've forgotten it's even there
among the seeing fruits, the ones that seem
to catch your eye as you rush past.

But here's what I admire about the blind date:
it doesn't need to see you to be sweet.
It doesn't require your recognition
to contain all that concentrated sugar,
that dense, dark flesh that tastes
like honey's older, wiser cousin.

The blind date asks for nothing—
no eye contact, no second glance—
just patient faith that eventually
you'll remember it's there, waiting,
and when you finally reach for it,
you'll discover it knew all along
exactly what it had to offer.
