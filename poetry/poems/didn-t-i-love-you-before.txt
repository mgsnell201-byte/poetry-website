Didn't I Love You Before?

Didn't I love you before—

when you were stumbling through your own life

like a young deer through brambles,

uncertain where to place each hoof?



I think I did.

I think I loved you when you didn't know

the names of things yet,

when your hands were clumsy

and your words came out all wrong.



Look—the sparrow outside my window

is learning to crack open

sunflower seeds. She drops more

than she eats. She tries again.

Does the morning love her any less?



You were never perfect.

Neither was I.

We were like two streams

moving through the same valley,

sometimes clear, sometimes

muddied by spring rains,

sometimes barely flowing at all.



But didn't we keep moving?

Didn't we keep trying to find our way

back to each other, to ourselves,

to that place where the water runs clean?



Tell me—what is love

if not this: the willow bending

in the storm but not breaking,

the grass flattened by snow

rising again in April,

the body returning, always returning,

to what it knows by heart?



I loved you before you were whole.

I love you now, still becoming.

The world doesn't ask the oak

to be finished growing.

Why should we ask it of each other?
