The Famous Child

At every party they ask for her by name:

e to the i pi, plus one, equals zero,

five old strangers holding hands in a single line.

Pi came in from the circles, still dizzy.

E arrived from the banks and the bacteria, always growing.

I, the one they called imaginary, stood at the door

waiting for someone to admit she was real.

One brought the counting. Zero brought the quiet.

Together they balance, and the guests applaud,

and someone always whispers the word “beautiful.”

But nobody invites her father, Euler’s formula,

e to the i theta, who never sits still,

who spins sines and cosines the way a potter spins clay.

He is down in the wall sockets right now,

humming sixty times a second.

He is inside your earbuds, sorting the drums from the voice,

carrying this poem through the air to your phone.

He doesn’t mind the spotlight going to his daughter.

He only stopped once, at the angle of pi,

long enough to make her, and then kept turning.
