The Racing Clock of Life

In childhood's realm, each day stretched wide,
A summer afternoon could last for years—
Each Christmas Eve an endless tide
Of waiting, wishing, hopeful tears.
The clock moved slow as honey poured,
Each birthday felt a lifetime earned,
While eighteen loomed like some great sword
That time itself had barely turned.

Then marriage bells and cradles rocking,
Four small hands reaching for your own,
The years began their gentle mocking—
How swift the seeds of time were sown.
From eighteen's gate to fifty's door,
Life picked up pace like morning rain,
Each decade holding so much more,
Sweet chaos mixed with joy and pain.

But fifty brought a sudden shift,
As if some cosmic gear had changed—
Time became a wind-caught drift,
The calendar rearranged.
Now seventy-six and racing still,
I'm strapped inside this speeding car,
Each season bends to time's strong will,
The finish line no longer far.

The grandkids grow between two blinks,
The seasons blur in rushing streams,
While memory selects and thinks
Of scattered moments, faded dreams.
Yet in this race against the clock,
I've learned what matters in the end—
Not how fast the years unlock,
But how we love, and laugh, and mend.

So let the Indy car race on,
Let time accelerate its pace,
For in each rapidly passing dawn
Lives beauty in this human race.
The speedway of our numbered days
May blur the scenery we pass,
But love remains in all the haze—
The only thing that's built to last.
