When Does a Push Become a Shove

The child's first steps—

a gentle push from behind,

or the first shove

toward inevitable falling?



We call it encouragement

when we nudge our daughters

toward mathematics,

but resistance when they push back

against our binary world.



The protestor's sign

pressed against the wind—

is this democracy's push

or tyranny's shove?

The line moves with the crowd's mood,

shifts with the news cycle,

bends with whoever holds the camera.



Your therapist pushes you

to examine the bruises

you've been calling love marks.

When does healing become violence?

When does silence become complicity?



The tech CEO pushes innovation—

connecting the world, he says,

as algorithms shove loneliness

deeper into teenage bedrooms,

as notifications push dopamine

until attention becomes addiction.



I pushed you away

the night you wanted to stay.

You called it self-sabotage.

I called it self-preservation.

Neither of us mentioned

the shove we both felt

when vulnerability

knocked at the door.



Consider the comma,

that small push

between clauses—

when does punctuation

become the shove

that changes meaning entirely?



The earth pushes up mountains

over millennia.

We call this creation.

The earth shoves continents apart

in seconds.

We call this destruction.

Same force,

different speed,

different story we tell ourselves

to sleep at night.



Your mother pushed you

toward piano lessons.

You shoved the bench back

after every recital

until music became the enemy.

Twenty years later,

you buy a keyboard,

wondering when rebellion

became regret.



The question isn't

where the line is drawn—

the question is

who holds the pencil,

who gets to erase it,

and why we pretend

there's a difference

between the pressure

we apply

and the pressure

we receive.



Perhaps push and shove

are the same motion

judged by who’s watching.

The real question is:

when did we stop asking

whose hand we’re holding—

and whose throat?
