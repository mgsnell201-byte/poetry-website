Fever Dreams

The body hijacks its own blueprint,
sends the blood down strange tributaries,
temperature rising like a rebellious tide
that floods the careful architecture of days.

What was meant to be Tuesday
becomes a liquid landscape
where time moves sideways
and the ceiling breathes
in rhythms not your own.

You planned to finish the report,
call your mother, water the plants—
but fever has other plans,
pulls you into its humid kingdom
where thoughts grow moss
and memories drip like condensation
from windows you can't remember opening.

In this place between sleep and waking,
your mind takes the long way
through rooms that shouldn't exist,
conversations with people made of steam,
while somewhere distant
your phone buzzes with the abandoned world,
its urgency meaningless as rain
on a house made of clouds.

The thermometer holds its verdict:
this body is no longer governed
by schedules or intentions,
only by the ancient wisdom
of rest, of surrender,
of letting the current carry you
until you wash up, days later,
on the familiar shore of yourself.
