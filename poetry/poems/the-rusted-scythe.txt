The Rusted Scythe

Beneath the weighted branches of the oak,
Where silence wraps itself in winter's breath,
A rusted scythe lies buried in the drift—
Its blade forgotten by the turning world.

The steel remembers seasons long dissolved,
When summer grass surrendered to its edge,
And farmer's hands would guide it through the field
While larks rose singing from the morning ground.

Now frost has claimed the handle's weathered wood,
And snow obscures the metal's ancient curve.
No harvest calls, no wheat stands proud and gold—
Just emptiness that stretches past the trees.

I watch this tool of labor left behind,
A witness to the rhythm of decay.
The woods accept what human purpose dropped,
And winter covers all without regard.

Yet something stirs within this quiet scene:
The blade endures though seasons overwhelm.
Perhaps in spring the snow will melt away
And earth will show what time cannot destroy.
