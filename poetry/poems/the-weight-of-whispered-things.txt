The Weight of Whispered Things

Do words, once spoken, ever die?

Or do they linger in the air,

woven into the breath of the sky,

a whisper the wind cannot spare?



Do they travel beyond the ear

that was meant to hold them close—

do they slip into the atmosphere,

where secrets and storms compose?



What of the words sharp as knives,

hurled in haste, in rage, in pain—

do they cut the soul that thrives,

or do they rust in the rain?



And what of the tender, the true,

the confessions soft as dawn—

do they root where they were sown,

or are they carried on and on?



Perhaps the tongue is but a spark,

and speech a fire none can tame—

every syllable leaves its mark,

echoing, endless, in flame.



So speak—but know the air remembers,

and the world has ears unseen.

Your words may fade like dying embers,

or live where the winds careen.
