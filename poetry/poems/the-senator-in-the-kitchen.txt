The Senator in the Kitchen

Flour dusts the wooden board
like snow that never melts.
I press my palms into the pale mound
as it pushes back, soft and silent under my tongue.

I watch the pale surface crack under my thumbs,
the word yielding like the paste itself,
a soft coin, blank page we leave empty.
The lamp hums and the dog sleeps.

But then I remember dough names the senator
who bent toward the South from his desk in the North,
the burnt bread, the thin wallet.

The dough forgets its dark syllable.
I fold it again, noticing the crease
catch the light.
