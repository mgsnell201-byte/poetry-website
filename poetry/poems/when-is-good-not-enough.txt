When Is Good Not Enough?

The candle flickers, dutiful and bright,

but the night is a hungering throat—

it swallows the glow like a miser’s coin,

and good is a bridge with no moat.



The baker kneads his tender loaves,

golden as a morning sun,

but the starving dream of feasts untold—

good is a song left unsung.



The gardener tends his roses well,

each petal a whispered vow,

yet the passerby craves orchids rare—

good is an unclaimed bough.



The sailor steers by steadfast stars,

his compass true as bone,

but the storm demands a lighthouse beam—

good is a voice alone.



Then comes the child with mud-streaked hands,

offering a daisy, torn.

The world, so vast, still stops to hold

this good—enough, and more.
