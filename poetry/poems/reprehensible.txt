Reprehensible

I have been thinking about the word "reprehensible",

how it arrives at the breakfast table

dressed in formal wear, refusing toast,

insisting we acknowledge its moral superiority.



Four syllables marching in polished shoes,

each one more pompous than the last,

not like the casual words we use—

"bad" or "wrong" or "pretty messed up"—



no, this word demands we pause,

adjust our posture, clear our throats

before announcing that someone has committed

a grievous breach of civilized conduct.



And yet I find myself oddly fond of it,

the way it clutches its pearls,

gasps at our everyday transgressions

as if each one deserves a formal trial.



Meanwhile, I once ate the last cookie

and blamed it on the dog,

surely a "reprehensible" act,

though I prefer to call it opportunistic,



or perhaps understandable, given how good

that cookie looked on the kitchen counter,

catching the afternoon light,

unguarded and glistening with chocolate chips.



This is what I love about our grand vocabulary—

how we build these elaborate courtrooms of judgment,

these cathedrals of moral disappointment,

and then go on living our small, cookie-stealing lives,



the serious words hovering somewhere above us

like disapproving clouds that never rain,

magnificent in their stuffiness,

waiting for us to measure up,



or simply to notice them there

in their Victorian waistcoats,

while we stand in our kitchens at 3 a.m.,

guilty and hungry and wonderfully human.
