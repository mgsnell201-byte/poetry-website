The Gilded Labyrinth of Almost-Truth

There is a moment, thin as breath,
When understanding meets its death—
Not in the absence of the mind,
But in the web too well-designed.

When scholars craft their towers tall
With words that glitter, shine, and fall
Like coins into a wishing well
That's deeper than their truths can tell.

The simple seed becomes a tree,
The tree becomes complexity,
But somewhere in its branching sprawl,
The roots forget why trees grow tall.

A child asks "Why?" with eyes so clear,
The expert's answer breeds more fear—
For in their need to sound profound,
They bury truth beneath the sound.

When jargon wraps around the core
Like ivy choking what came before,
When clarity becomes the cost
Of seeming wise while truth is lost.

The purple prose, the gilded thought,
The wisdom that can't be bought
Or sold or understood by those
Who need the truth the purple chose.

Purplexicity blooms when minds
Forget that light through darkness shines
Not by building walls of night,
But by letting in the light.

The deepest truths are often small,
The greatest minds don't need to crawl
Through mazes of their own design—
They speak in ways that clearly shine.

So complexity serves its place
When it unveils, not when it hides the face
Of understanding from our view—
The purple hue obscures what's true.

When knowledge kneels before its crown,
When wisdom seeks to write renown
In syllables the heart can't hold,
The story's warmth grows crystal cold.

The moment comes, that razor's edge,
When we step off wisdom's ledge
Into the purple-tinted mist
Where meaning can no longer exist.
