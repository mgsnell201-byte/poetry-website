The Ghost Who Only Haunts During Commercial Breaks

The spirit in my living room appears
exclusively during advertisements,
which gives him roughly two and a half minutes
every quarter hour to manifest his presence,
make his point, and fade back
into whatever holding pattern ghosts maintain
between their scheduled hauntings.

He died, I think, sometime in 1952,
before remotes, before streaming services,
back when you had to physically rise from the couch
and walk across the room to change the channel—
a trip he must have made ten thousand times,
wearing a path in the carpet that outlasted him
and possibly explains why he's still here.

His limitation is that he can only materialize
in the blue-gray flicker of television light,
which means he's utterly helpless during podcasts,
during reading, during actual conversation,
and once, memorably, he tried to appear
during a power outage and managed only
a faint static charge in the curtains.

Instead of moaning or rattling chains,
he makes a sound like channel-surfing—
that rapid mechanical clicking of stations changing,
scrolling through frequencies that no longer exist,
searching for programs that went off the air
before I was born.

I've tried to explain streaming to him,
how there are no commercials if you pay extra,
but this only confused him, and for three days straight
he kept appearing at random intervals,
flickering in and out like a failing bulb,
unable to find his rhythm.

Now I've learned to keep regular broadcast television on,
at least in the background, so he knows when to arrive,
and sometimes during the ads for pharmaceuticals
or car insurance, I'll see him standing
by the screen, one hand raised
as if he's about to adjust the antenna,

still trying to get the picture clearer.
