The Last Oak Leaf

An oak stood gnarled, bent and old,

A weary tree in autumn’s grip,

Rough bark revealed a tale untold,

A frame where life began to slip.

One leaf hung on, so frail and thin,

With all its might it gripped the bough,

Its green now gold, a fragile skin,

A whisper of what once was now.



The dawn crept in with chilly air,

A mist lay thick on hill and plain,

The leaf shook soft, alone up there,

And fought against the season’s reign.

Its veins grew weak, its hold unsure,

Yet still it clung with quiet might,

A thread of hope, so small, so pure,

To hold its place in fading light.



A breeze blew in, so soft, so kind,

Through frosted fields it hummed a tune,

It tugged the leaf, its will to bind,

And pulled it free by morning’s noon.

The leaf resisted, tried to stay,

But strength gave out, it couldn’t last,

It fell with grace that cool fall day,

The branch released its hold so fast.



The air then caught it, light and free,

Above the river’s shining face,

It soared where water gleamed to see,

A dance through beauty’s wild embrace.

Past meadows bright with flower and weed,

A world alive in bloom and strife,

The leaf drifted on, its heart to bleed,

Still aching for its former life.



With edges browned, its spark grew dim,

The wind now twirled it high and grand,

Its golden hue was growing slim,

A soul that sank in autumn’s hand.

The earth below rolled vast and wide,

Its journey long, its sorrow deep,

A silent wish it couldn’t hide,

For roots it lost and couldn’t keep.



The breeze slowed down, the air grew still,

It dropped the leaf on shadowed ground,

A cold, dark spot beneath a hill,

Where quiet secrets wrap around.

No eye could see the twist unfold,

As under that oak it came to rest,

Its home reclaimed, so soft, so bold,

Back where its life had once been blessed.
