Supper for One

The tap runs brackish for a minute,

the color of weak tea, then clears.

I boil a turnip, since it was there,

the one thing left in the drawer

that wasn’t already soft.



The milk has begun to curdle

in a glass I forgot by the sink.

Out back the garden has gone feral,

beans over the fence, a squash

the size of a sleeping dog.



I eat the turnip standing up.

It tastes like a coin held in the mouth.

Rain has come in through the window

I have meant to fix since spring

and stands in a line along the sill.



But then I remember the cabinet door,

the hinge that has hung loose for years,

and how tonight I tightened it.



I open it and close it twice.

Inside, a thimble, and in the thimble

your molar, which the dentist said was mine to keep.
