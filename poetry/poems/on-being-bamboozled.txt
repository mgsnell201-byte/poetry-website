On Being Bamboozled

I want to tell you about the time I was hoodwinked

by a lie so elaborate, so beautifully constructed,

that when the truth finally revealed itself

like a magician pulling back a velvet curtain,

I found myself applauding.



It was the kind of deception that required blueprints,

a three-act structure, supporting characters

who had clearly rehearsed their lines.

The kind of bamboozling that takes real commitment,

the way a good novel takes commitment,

or a marriage, or learning to play the bassoon.



I was befuddled, yes, and certainly flummoxed,

hornswoggled in the classical sense,

but here's the thing—I was also impressed.

The architecture of the lie was stunning.

Each detail fit into the next like Russian dolls,

or those ships they build inside bottles,

and I couldn't help but admire the craftsmanship.



You know how sometimes you watch a movie

and halfway through you realize you've been misdirected,

that the real story was happening in the background

while you were focused on the wrong thing?

It was like that, only I was living in the movie,

wandering around with my popcorn and my certainty.



And when it all came tumbling down—

when the house of cards became just cards again,

scattered across the table in no particular order—

I wasn't even angry.

I was too busy being dazzled

by how long someone had held their breath underwater,

how many plates they'd kept spinning,

how they'd managed to make me believe

that up was down and Tuesday was Friday

and the cat had been a dog all along.



There's an art to being fooled this thoroughly.

It requires a certain surrender, a willingness

to trust the teller of the tale.

And maybe that's the real trick—

not the lie itself, but the invitation to believe,

the temporary gift of living in someone else's fiction,

even if the gift turns out to be empty-handed.
