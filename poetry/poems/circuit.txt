Circuit

Water pushes against my tail
and I push back, stirring
the only current in this sphere,
circling the same three inches
I have always circled.
My tail beats and the water moves —
the plastic plant sways,
the gravel shifts,
but the clear wall
never yields an inch.

I know each fraction of this loop:
the shadow where the filter hums,
the green tuft that never grows,
the bend where light warps
my whole world outward.

Then your face arrives,
filling the curved window,
one finger pressing flat
against the outside,
tracing my path as I circle.

You tap and tap, trying
to scratch through glass
that does not give,
your finger sliding
over the shape of my orbit.

I turn toward your hand
every single time.

Three inches of water.
Your palm, warm on the glass.
