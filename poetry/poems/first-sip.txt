First Sip

The hummingbird comes while fog holds the marsh

and its throat catches the first gold

through honeysuckle

and the vine has opened every pale trumpet

and waits.



I watch the needle beak enter the deepest fold

and the tongue flick once, twice

and the wings hum and blur

and the small body drink what the dark left--

dew and nectar and the last cold breath

off the black pond.



Then the bird lifts

and the blossom nods, emptied

and the light spreads across the wet grass

and touches the cracked shell at the water's edge

and touches my knee

and touches the pine boughs

and everything it reaches

gleams this one morning.



Whoever you are, no matter how long you have slept,

the dawn does not wait.

The honeysuckle opens. The bird drinks.

The light crosses the field

and does not ask permission.



Will you meet it where it falls?
