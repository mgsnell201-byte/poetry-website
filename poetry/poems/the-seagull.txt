The Seagull

Behold the seagull, most un-gullible of birds,

Who speaks in screeches instead of words.

It practices sneaky snatch-craft at the beach,

Keeping your sandwich forever out of reach

Until it's in the gull's gullet,

Where it joins yesterday's mullet.



The seagull is afflicted with grab-biness,

A condition causing considerable scrabble-ness

In its table manners and social graces—

It steals french fries from babies' faces!

This feathered pickpocket shows no remorse

As it commits sandwich-theft by force.



I've observed that seagulls are tremendously foolish

In their assumption that all food is gullish—

That is, belonging to gulls by natural right.

They suffer from take-itude, day and night.



So should a gull consign your lunch to fate,

Don't curse the bird or fulminate—

You're not a victim, but a congregant

In the Church of the Endless Picnic Covenant.

Instead, consider yourself honored to participate

In the ancient rite they celebrate:

To communicate with the Spirit in the sky,

Who demands that all snacks learn to fly.
