Monday Morning Staring At The Glass

I have been looking at the corner of the window pane,

where a tiny spider, no larger than a match head,

has decided to construct a modest web

between the wood and the glass,

unaware that this is a pane of glass and not the open air,

unaware that I am sitting here in my favorite armchair

drinking coffee that has gone slightly cold.



It is a remarkably poor location for business.

No flies ever come to this side of the house,

except for the occasional disoriented gnat

that usually ends up drowning in my mug anyway,

leaving us both a little disappointed.

And yet, he works with the quiet intensity

of a medieval monk illuminating a manuscript,

stretching his invisible silk from anchor point to anchor point,

as if the future of the world depended on this three-inch kingdom.



Watching him, I am reminded of how we spend our days

building these neat, intricate frameworks in the corners of our lives,

fully expecting the universe to deliver exactly what we asked for.

We check our watches, we adjust our glasses,

we turn the page of a book we have already read twice,

as if the mere act of paying attention

could persuade the world to behave.



But then, I remember a summer forty years ago,

standing on a dock in British Columbia,

watching the salmon boat crews patch their nylon seines by hand.

They used long plastic needles, moving with a rhythm so fluid

it looked less like work and more like a slow, heavy dance,

mending holes big enough to let a whole school of silver slip away.

They didn't look at the sky, or the water, or the looming fog;

they just looked at the knot right in front of them.



And I suppose that is what we are all doing here.

The spider has finished his architecture now

and is sitting motionless at the very center of his grid,

waiting for a breeze or an inheritance.

I lift my cup to take another sip of the lukewarm brew,

careful not to lean too close,

not wanting my breath to cause a hurricane

in a world that is already so fragile,

and so perfectly, uselessly square.
