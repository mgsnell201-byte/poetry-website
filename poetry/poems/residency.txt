Residency

There is this tiny little spider

that has taken residency in the corner

of my bathroom window.

He hangs there like a comma

at the end of a long sentence of glass.



I brush my teeth and watch him watch the yard.

He is waiting for a fly to wander in,

or perhaps he is just listening

to the hum of the house settling,

the pipes singing behind the wall.



We have come to an agreement, he and I.

I will not spray him with anything,

and he will not drop down on a thread

to land in the sink while I am shaving.

It is a fragile treaty based on silence.



He knows the texture of the glass better than I do.

He knows the vibration of the sash.

When I tap the pane, he does not move.

He trusts the structure of his chosen angle.



But then the morning light shifts slightly,

and he becomes a speck of dust

that has decided to remain suspended.

And I realize we are both just holding on

to whatever corner we have found,

waiting for the day to bring us something

we can catch, or simply let go.
