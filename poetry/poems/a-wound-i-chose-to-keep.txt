A Wound I Chose to Keep

I remember missing you—a clock upon the shelf,

whose weights I wind each evening to maintain my fragile self.

Its pendulum of silver, a hypnotic, swaying sigh,

marks time I cannot spend with you, yet cannot let pass by.



The cogs are forged from moments I have polished with my breath,

each tick a tiny hammer beating reminders of your death.

The chime, when it releases, is a burden I have borne,

a note that hangs like smoke within the quiet of the morn.



I carry this machinery within a velvet case,

lined with all the echoes of your voice, your touch, your face.

Its heft is not of metal, but of things I cannot leave—

the weight of all the mornings that nothing can relieve.



But I have held it for so long its constant ache

has carved a sacred hollow that my soul will not forsake.

The gears have worn smooth grooves where sorrow pools and stays,

small reservoirs of longing fed by all these counted days.



For this remembered missing is a wound I chose to keep,

a well of ancient rainwater, both bitter-sweet and deep.

So let the mechanism turn, let the silver pendulum swing—

this burden is a testament, this clockwork of remembering.



And in its constant motion, in its precisely measured pain,

I prove that some goodbyes are not an ending, but a reign.
