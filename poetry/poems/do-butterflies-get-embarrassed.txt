Do Butterflies Get Embarrassed?

When they emerge wrinkled from their cocoons,
Wings still damp with possibility?

I wonder if streetlights gossip
about the secrets they’ve illuminated:
first kisses tasting of cherry chapstick,
last words drowned under a bus’s growl.

Do mirrors ever lie on purpose,
Showing us the person we could be
Instead of the one brushing teeth
In yesterday's pajamas?

And when you almost say something—
your throat a dam of unsplit light—
do they harden like amber in your chest?

Does loneliness send itself mail
To feel less alone?
And when forgiveness gets dressed up,
Who taught it to tie its shoes
With thread that glows in the dark?
