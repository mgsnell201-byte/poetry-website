The Fly on the Wall

I noticed a fly on the wall this morning, a speck of black jitter buzzing faintly, parked above the bookshelf, near a lamp that’s been flickering like a tired eyelid. It’s just there, legs thin as whispers, watching me shuffle through papers and dust. What’s it doing, this tiny critic? Taking notes on my coffee-stained mug, my habit of talking to the radio, as if it’s got opinions on the news? I smirk at the thought—me, a spectacle, my life a stage for this insect audience. But what if it’s not just a fly? What if it’s a philosopher in disguise, pondering the weight of my small routines— the way I leave crumbs on the counter, or how the clock’s tick outpaces my plans? Maybe it sees what I miss: the slow fade of light through the window, the room’s quiet hum. I could flick it away, end its vigil, but there’s something in its stubborn perch, like a poet who lingers too long at a desk, searching for meaning in a spill of ink. It’s almost noble, this fly’s patient gaze, making me wonder if I’m the one who’s been buzzing in circles all along.
