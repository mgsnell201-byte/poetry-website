What She Becomes

I've been thinking about the zombie bride who got married last Halloween night, how she stood at the altar, dignified, in her dress with its lace sewn tight. Not undead wife, which sounds like a complaint you'd file with customer service somewhere. Not zombie spouse, though the term is quaint— the wedding announcement didn't go there. The certificate doesn't have a box to check for previously deceased, I'd bet. No line that asks about the wreck of dying, or how long since death you met. I like to think she's simply married now, that love doesn't care about your pulse, doesn't ask questions about the how or measure the warmth that results. She's what we all become in the end— someone trying to hold ceremony near, someone who believes a ring can mend and transform us into something dear. The groom adjusts his tie with nervous care. She holds her plastic bouquet tight. Two people standing there, a pair who still believe in Halloween's light.
