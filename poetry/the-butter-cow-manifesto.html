The Butter Cow Manifesto

The Butter Cow Committee convened at dawn, with clipboards clutched and worried faces drawn. "No margarine!" they cried. "We must be pure! Six hundred pounds of butterfat, for sure!" They churned the cream with patriotic zeal, declaring only dairy makes it real. "Trans fats are terrorism!" someone screamed. The crowd erupted. Someone's child ice-creamed. They brought the blocks in coolers, five feet tall, and sculpted udders in the Varied Hall. "She's ripening!" a judge announced with pride, while families pressed their noses to the glass divide. The cow stood golden, glistening, refined— a monument to what the heartland's refined. No shortcuts here, no substitutes, no fakes: just cream and salt and Iowa's high stakes. "Remember ghee!" the keynote speaker roared. "Clarified commitment! Truth restored!" They wept. They clapped. They bought a corn dog meal. The butter cow just stood there, stoic, real. So bring your children, teach them what is true: that excellence means never misconstruing a polymer for product of the teat. (The cow begins to soften in the heat.)
