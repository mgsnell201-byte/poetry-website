Transparent

What are transparent poems? are they the ones you can see right through like the parents who aren't there anymore— transparent, the ones who've crossed over. My father is transparent now. I can hold my hand up and watch the light pass through where he used to be, through the space at the kitchen table, through the driver's seat of the car, through the doorway where he'd stand asking if I'd checked the oil, locked the back door, remembered to call my mother. Is that what makes a poem transparent— when you can see straight through the words to the shape of what's missing? The way a window is really just an organized absence, a hole we've agreed to call architecture? I asked my friends what transparency means in poetry and they say clarity, they say truth, they say a poem that doesn't hide behind metaphor. But I think they're wrong. I think transparency is what happens when something becomes so thin with leaving that light passes through it without stopping, without even noticing there was something there to illuminate. My mother is becoming transparent too. Not gone, but going—each day a little more see-through, her voice on the phone a little more like wind, her memory of my childhood a little more like a window someone forgot to put glass in. And here's what keeps me up at night: if a transparent poem is one no one sees, and transparent means the parent who has moved through, then what am I becoming as I watch them go? What does it mean that I can feel myself growing more transparent, standing here in the autumn of my life, understanding finally that we're all just light on our way through something, that being seen was only ever temporary, that the most honest poem might be the one that disappears completely the moment you finish reading it— Would you even know it had been there? Would you feel the space where it used to be? Or is that the point: that transparency isn't about being invisible, but about becoming a window through which someone else might see what's on the other side of being here at all?
