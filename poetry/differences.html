Differences?

Between us there is a here and a there— but where is the where when we both start to stare? If I step toward you, am I a-go or a-come, a-near-ly-there-um with my heart going thrum? If you walk toward me, are you arriving or late, or partly departing your old single state? Are you just leaving your lonely behind, or finding a both-of-us state of mind? Suppose we’re two beetles on one broomstick, tiny tick-tack feet going clickety-click, each little inching an almost hello till broom, bug, and bravery all start to glow. Or squirrels on opposite sides of a tree: you scoot up, I scamper down, silly as can be. We swirl in circling squirrulations around till—same branch, same bark, same meeting-ground. Or think of two raindrops on one window wide: one slides from the left and one from the side. Both slipping and tripping in gravity’s giggle, bumping into one drop with one happy wiggle. So maybe love’s less about who first began, and more about who stays to share the plan. Less about first move and more about next to, less “Who chased whom?” than “I met you, you met me too.” Call it you-coming-to-me, or me-going-to-you; call it a both-ways, two-ways, true-way view. Either way, as our near and our far coalesce, the distance between us goes out of business.
