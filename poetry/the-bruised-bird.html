The Bruised Bird

A bird with a bent wing flops into my kitchen, feathers smudged like a chalkboard erased wrong. I thought it was a clock at first, ticking backward on the counter, its beak a needle stitching air to nowhere. Hope’s a reckless kite, caught in a tree’s teeth. The window yawns, but the bird just stares, as if gravity’s a joke told badly. I offer it a crumb, and it sings— not a song, but a car alarm in a dream. The bruise blooms purple, a tiny galaxy spinning on its chest, whispering fly anyway. Then I realize I’ve lost the sky’s address, left it scrawled on a napkin in a diner. The bird hops onto a spoon, declaring itself captain of a sinking ship. Fate’s gate swings, but we don’t pass through— we dance, clumsy, on a floor of cracked plates. A lightbulb hums, pretending to be the moon. The bird’s eye glints, a coin flipped for luck. Loss lingers like ash in a windless room, yet this bruised thing flaps, wild, toward a crack in the wall where chaos kisses chance. I laugh, tender as a bruise, and let it go.
