Poem · No. 13

After You, No, After You

Four small trick-or-treaters stood At the edge of a crooked wood, Where a gravel drive climbed up the hill To a house lit up and eerily still. The windows glowed like candy corn, The sky above looked cold and torn, And everyone was thinking, “Gee, That’s quite a walk for little me.” “You go first,” the witch declared. “Not me,” a pumpkin said, scared. “Well, I’m not going!” “Nor am I!” “You go on up.” “No, you! Not I!” Now look at the pumpkins, if you please: Their grins are turned back toward the trees, Their costumes spun the wrong way round, All pointing back to safer ground. So there they stand as the daylight fades, Four small ghouls in their masquerades, Still insisting, “After you!” While the old house sighs, “Well, someone do.”
