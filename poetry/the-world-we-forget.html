The World We Forget

When I say "goodnight" and draw my blinds, Somewhere else the morning sun climbs high, Coffee brewing in awakening minds While I dream beneath a starlit sky. My casual words drift through the air— "Keep your pecker up!" I cheer a friend, Not knowing how across the ocean there Those same sounds make different meanings bend. What lifts spirits here might cause offense, What's innocent becomes obscene, Lost in translation's consequence, Misunderstood in spaces between. The rubber becomes an eraser's twin, Fanny packs turn into bum bags, While lifts and elevators spin The same journey with different tags. I forget that while I'm having lunch, You're settling down for evening tea, That my tomorrow is your midnight hunch, My yesterday, your tomorrow's spree. The world spins on, a carnival of time, Each zone a different act in play, Where my perfect moment's pantomime Is your ordinary middle-of-the-day. So when I speak of "here" and "now," I should remember earth's great dance— That somewhere else, somehow, My words arrive by circumstance. In lands where different meanings dwell, Where night is day and day is night, Where words I know so very well Might dim another's inner light. The pitfall of the self-made world: Forgetting others share our sphere, That as our own lives are unfurled, A billion others persevere. In different tongues, in different hours, Under different skies they roam— Yet sharing these same earthly powers To make their corner feel like home.
