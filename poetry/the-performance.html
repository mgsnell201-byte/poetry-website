The Performance

There's a particular expression I've perfected over the years, a kind of theater of disappointment I put on when someone mentions the event I didn't attend, the gathering I somehow managed to avoid through a combination of legitimate excuses and what I can only describe as strategic forgetfulness. "Oh," I say, letting my face fall just enough to suggest genuine regret without overdoing it, "I completely forgot that was last night." And then I listen—really listen— as they describe the cramped room, the warm wine, the conversation that circled endlessly around someone's remodeling project, the strange cheese that nobody touched. Inside, though, there's a small celebration happening, a quiet parade moving through the chambers of my heart, led by the part of me that spent the evening in slippers and reading glasses, a book open on my lap, the cat asleep on the arm of the chair, the world reduced to the pool of lamplight and the gentle tick of the clock on the wall. I've learned that sympathy is the currency we use to purchase our solitude, that a well-timed "That sounds wonderful, I'm so sorry I missed it" can be traded for another evening of exactly this— the dog at my feet, the tea gone cold, the blessed absence of anywhere I need to be or anyone I need to become for a few hours. "Next time for sure," I say, meaning it the way we all mean the kind things we say to make the world feel a little softer, already knowing that next time there will be another book I haven't finished, another evening when the couch will be exactly where I left it.
