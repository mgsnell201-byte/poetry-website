The Cartomancer at Breakfast

I should tell you that I'm not very good at this, shuffling these oversized cards at the kitchen table while my tea grows cold in its blue cup. The Fool keeps turning up, which seems about right, and I'm never sure if the Tower means my life is about to collapse or if it's just referring to the stack of unpaid bills listing near the toaster. You'd think by now I'd have figured out whether to read the future in these painted figures or simply admire how the medieval artist made the hangman look so peaceful, dangling there upside down like a bat at rest, reconsidering the world from a different angle. My wife Carolee says you have to trust your intuition, but my intuition is usually telling me to have another cookie or avoid that phone call, so I'm not confident it's the best guide for navigating swords and pentacles. Still, there's something companionable about sitting here with these enigmatic strangers— the Empress in her pomegranate garden, Death on his pale horse who might just mean it's time to clean out the garage, the Two of Cups suggesting that love is simply two people agreeing to drink from adjacent vessels. Maybe the real trick is not to play them right at all but to let them play you, the way a good book rearranges your thoughts without asking permission, the way the morning light decides which objects in the room deserve to be illuminated. So I'll leave the Star card face-up on the table, that naked woman pouring water into water, and finish my tea, which has achieved the perfect temperature for revelation— not too hot for truth, not too cold for hope.
