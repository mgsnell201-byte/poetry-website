To the Reader I'll Never Meet

I don't know your name or where you're sitting as you read this—maybe at a kitchen table with coffee going cold in a mug beside you, or on a bus, the city scrolling past the window like a film you've seen before but can't quite place. Maybe you're the type who dog-ears pages, or maybe you're more careful than that, the kind who uses proper bookmarks, those thin slips of paper that mark where you stopped believing, or started again. I imagine you have a favorite coffee cup, chipped perhaps, or perfectly intact, and that you've held it a thousand mornings while the kitchen filled with light, thinking about everything and nothing, the way we all do when we're alone with our thoughts and our caffeine. You've probably stood in a doorway somewhere, deciding whether to stay or leave, your hand on the doorknob like it was the lever that could change everything, which, of course, it was. And I bet you've lain awake at three a.m., listening to the house settle around you, all those small sounds that mean nothing and everything—the refrigerator's hum, a tree branch scratching at the window, the particular silence that exists only in the middle of the night when you're the only one awake in what feels like the entire world. Here's what I know about you without knowing you: you've lost something you can't name, found something you didn't know you were looking for, and spent more time than you'd admit wondering if anyone else feels the strange weight of being alive, of walking through days that are both utterly ordinary and somehow impossible. You're reading this, which means you're still curious, still willing to spend a few minutes with a stranger's words, and that, I think, is worth something— this small agreement we've made across whatever distance separates us, you there with your cooling coffee, me here with my cooling coffee, both of us wondering if the other knows what it means to be so alone together.
