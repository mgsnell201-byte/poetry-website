The River After Rain

The river swells like a held breath released, dark water surging past the dock where I carved my initial in the summer’s lazy grammar. Today the current carries a broken branch, a rusted can— oil slicks shimmer where fish swam. You stand beside me, my shadow of wonder, silent. I know you’re fading. Your light dims like fireflies in smog, here, then gone. Did I trade you for screens, for noise? The river’s roar accuses stone—and me. I want to call you back, but words are minnows slipping through my fingers, silver and impossible to hold. I wade in, hands grasping at sludge, but the current laughs, cold. The rain returns, soft, then drums the planks like fingers thumping on a table, impatiently. A drone’s hum drowns the river’s song. I am one boy and his ghost watching water rise, knowing it will carry away the old pier, the rope swing, the name I gave this place— Willow Bend—like it held my secrets. What do we keep when the world forgets its own?
