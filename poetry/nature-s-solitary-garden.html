Nature's Solitary Garden

The garden sleeps in winter's grip— Each bloom lies still and cold, While love once warm begins to slip Through fingers, growing old. The oak stands stark against the sky, Its branches bare and lone, As seasons pass and mortals die In silence, on their own. Yet in this hush of frost and stone A tender voice rings clear— Though death may claim what we have known, Love lingers, drawing near. The earth will wake, the buds will break From winter's icy hold, And what seemed lost for sorrow's sake Returns in green and gold.
