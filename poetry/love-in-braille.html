Love in Braille

Your fingers trace the landscape of my palm, reading stories written in the ridges of a life lived before you arrived— each crease a chapter, each scar a word I never learned to speak aloud. Love, you tell me, is not found in the poetry of perfect vision, but in the quiet percussion of hearts learning each other’s rhythms through barriers we thought beyond reach. You map my silences like continents, discover the raised dots of my uncertainties, the way my breathing changes pitch when you’re three rooms away and absence becomes its own raised text. In darkness, we become translators of a language that has no alphabet— only the grammar of skin against skin, the calloused edge of your thumb brushing my wrist. The syntax of sighs, the punctuation of fingertips finding their way home. I am learning to love in raised letters, to read the braille of your laughter pressed into the hollow of my throat, to understand that the most profound truths are written in a script only touch can decode. Here, blindfolded by trust, we discover that love is not about seeing but about being seen, not about what eyes reveal but about the territories fingers claim until every surface becomes a page and we are both reader and text, writing ourselves into existence one careful, deliberate touch at a time.
