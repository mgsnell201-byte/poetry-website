Trick or Tree-eat

The fog rolls in on silent feet, And whispers, “Trick or Tree-eat!” The old tree bends with a crooked grin, A hollow mouth where the dark pours in. Its mossy lips are stretched out wide, With shadows waiting deep inside. Its gnarled arm reaches, bent and low, Like a hand that begs, “Come close. Hello.” Behind it, ghostly giants stand, Half-lost in mist, a silent band. They lean together, hushed and still, As if they’ve gathered for the kill. The broken fence posts, thin and gray, Look like old bones where wanderers lay. No candy here, no porch light’s gleam, Just roots that twist like a waking dream. So if you pass this haunted place And glimpse that wide and hungry face, Don’t linger long, don’t stop to greet: Or you’ll be the treat in Trick or Tree-eat!
