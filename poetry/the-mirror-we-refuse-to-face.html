The Mirror We Refuse to Face

We are the judges with dirty hands, Pointing fingers from our pedestals of sand, Demanding perfection from every soul we meet While our own flaws remain conveniently discreet. You should be kinder, we declare with scorn, While our own cruelty cuts like thorns. You should be honest, we righteously demand, As we weave lies with our other hand. We expect heroes from ordinary men, Saints from sinners, again and again. But when we stumble, oh how quick we are To find excuses, near and far. "I was tired," "I was stressed," "I didn't know," "It's complicated," our defenses flow. Yet when others fail in the very same way, We offer no mercy, no understanding today. Perhaps we project our own hidden shame, Making others carry what we won't name. Or maybe it's easier to critique from afar Than to heal our own internal scars. The mother who screams about bad parenting While her own children suffer, silently smarting. The politician who preaches about moral decay While corruption guides his every play. The friend who demands loyalty without question But abandons ship at the first suggestion Of inconvenience, of effort required— Our hypocrisy leaves relationships expired. So why do we hold others to impossible heights While we stumble through our own dark nights? Is it fear of facing our own reflection, Of admitting our need for self-correction? The truth is sharper than we'd like to confess: We expect more because we accept our own mess. We've made peace with our contradictions, But offer others no such exemptions. Until we learn to turn that mirror around, To seek in ourselves what we think should be found, We'll remain prisoners of our own creation— Demanding from others our own transformation.
