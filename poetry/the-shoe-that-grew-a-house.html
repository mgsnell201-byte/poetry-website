The Shoe That Grew a House

An old leather shoe went wandering far, Over hilltops and meadows and streams, With no one to wear it and nowhere to be, Just following the sun and its dreams. It splashed through the marsh and it clomped down the lane, It tiptoed past owls in the trees, It hopped over puddles and dodged through the rain, And it shook off the burrs and the bees. Then one golden evening, as daylight grew thin, It came to a moss-covered glade, Where the forget-me-nots nodded, and buttercups grinned, And it sat down in the softest of shade. "This is it," sighed the shoe, "I have wandered enough, And my toes have stopped wanting to roam. So I'll stay in this spot, where the moss is so soft, And I'll make it my very own home." Then the rain gently fell and the sun shone through, And the moss stitched a roof, soft and green, A door and a window came peeking out too, The cosiest cottage you've seen. Last, a chimney of brick rose up to the sky, And it breathed out a curl of warm air, A sign for all wanderers passing by: "There's a home, and there's room, and there's care." Then along came a mouse in a jacket of gold, Who knocked at the small arched door, And the house said, "Come in, it is warm, and I'm told That I've room for one traveler more." So if ever you wander the woods and you see A shoe with a chimney on top, Remember it wandered as far as could be Till it found the right place to plop.
