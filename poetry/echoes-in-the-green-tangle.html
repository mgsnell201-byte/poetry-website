Echoes in the Green Tangle

A Tribute to Jane Goodall In the tangle of green where the tall trees all lean, Young Jane dreamed of chimps with their whoops and their gleams. But oh, how they hollered and harried her days, Stealing her sketches right out of her gaze. She'd perch on a stump with her pencil held tight, To capture their gambols in soft morning light. Yet Fifi would filch it, that sly silver thief, And scribble wild loops on a termite-leaf wreath. Then Goliath would grumble, his belly a-roar, And raid her poor lunch till there wasn't no more. Bananas and berries, all gulped in a go, Leaving Jane with just echoes of hooting below. The little ones scampered, with paws like small storms, Tugging her braids till they frayed into forms. They'd climb up her shoulders and chatter her ear, With questions that tumbled like raindrops of fear. Oh, the jungle kept calling with whispers so sweet, But each joyful tussle brought aches to her feet. Till one twilight hush, as the shadows grew long, Jane saw in their eyes what she'd known all along: The swings that they shared were as fleeting as breath, A tender tangle twixt living and death. She hugged Fifi close in the firefly's glow, And whispered, "My dears, we must let some things go."
