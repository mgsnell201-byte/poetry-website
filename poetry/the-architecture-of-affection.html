The Architecture of Affection

Is love the ancient code written in our bones, A primal script that evolution owns? Or is it learned, like language, line by line, A craft we perfect with practice and time? Watch the newborn grasp its mother's finger— Is this love, or survival's whisper? The oxytocin flooding neural streams, Biology disguised as romantic dreams? The mother bird who feeds her crying young, Is tenderness truly what moves her tongue? Or chemical commands she can't refuse, Instinct wearing love's elaborate ruse? Yet humans write sonnets to their beloved, Build monuments to hearts that have been moved. No peacock writes poetry to his mate, No wolf composes ballads for his fate. We choose to love the damaged and the strange, The ones who offer no evolutionary exchange. We love the dying, the distant, the opposed— Choices that leave our genes decomposed. The elderly couple, hand in weathered hand, Their bodies beyond biology's demand, Still choosing each other, day after day— Is this instinct or something else at play? Perhaps love is both the seed and the flower, Instinct providing the initial power, But consciousness crafting something more: A choice to keep opening that primal door. The mother's fierce protection—surely innate, But her decision to forgive comes from a different gate. The lover's desire—chemistry's sweet call, But staying through hardship transcends it all. Maybe we are animals learning to be human, Our instincts the clay, our choices illumined By something beyond mere survival's art— The conscious cultivation of the heart. For love requires what no instinct can teach: The courage to be vulnerable, within reach Of pain, of loss, of being truly known— A risk that biology has never shown. So perhaps love is instinct's wild beginning, but grows beyond where evolution's winning— where choice takes the wheel, where we decide to love not because we must, but because we feel something deeper than the need to survive: The need to be beautifully, dangerously alive.
