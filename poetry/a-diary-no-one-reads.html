A Diary No One Reads

I was the one you leaned against that August afternoon, your back settling into my bark as if I were some chair you’d ordered from a catalog and were pleasantly shocked to find already waiting in the yard, fully assembled. For a hundred and twenty years I did what trees do best: stood still, breathed, listened the way only the best listeners can— quiet, uncomplaining, mildly amused. Squirrels checked into my branches like a hotel that never asked for reservations. Moss moved in without so much as a knock. Then came the saw. I won’t bore you with the details, except to say the scent it released was the sweetest thing I had carried all those years without ever noticing. Now here I am: a thick round of oak on someone’s porch, a small table for their morning coffee, still wearing every one of my hundred and twenty rings like a diary no one reads— though everyone who sits down rests their hands right on the cover.
