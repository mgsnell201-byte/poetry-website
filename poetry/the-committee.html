The Committee

I watched them assemble at the glass— a tribunal of ducks, their orange beaks like stamps waiting to approve or deny my request for entrance. I had come bearing stale sourdough in a plastic bag, thinking this would be enough, but they wanted something more. It was the way they pressed forward, white feathers overlapping like tiles on a crooked roof, each neck craned to get a better look at me standing there in my ridiculous human shoes. One blinked. Another shifted weight. The huddle tightened. I thought of all the committees I have faced— the silent panels, the skeptical faces, the forms I forgot to fill out in triplicate— but none quite like this, where the only question seemed to be whether I understood the terms of engagement, whether I grasped that bread is not a right but a negotiation. They were waiting, I realized, for me to make the first move, to tear the bag open and scatter proof across the water. But I stood there, frozen by the intensity of their staring, those dark eyes beneath the feathered brows, and wondered who was really on trial. The video doorbell would show only this: a man holding bread, a wall of white and orange, and the long moment before anyone moved— that pause where power shifts hands. Then my wife's voice from inside: "Honey, those are geese. The ducks are around back." I had been reporting to the wrong committee all along.
