On the Back of a Sparrow

I don't know how I got there exactly, the way you never know in dreams how you came to be standing in your old school wearing only one shoe, but there I was, gripping the warm brown feathers of something no bigger than a fist, and we were rising— past the gutters, past the neighbor's oak, past the particular cloud that always looks like a philosophy professor I once had. And here is the strange thing, the thing worth mentioning over coffee: all of it came loose. The unpaid bill, the unanswered letter, the thing I said in 1987 that still wakes me at 3 a.m., the general shapeless dread that follows me around like a dog who has chosen the wrong owner— all of it simply slipped from my pockets and fell back into the world where it apparently belonged. I remembered then what Emily knew, that hope had been flying around in a small body this whole time, singing without words, asking nothing, keeping us aloft in the most unlikely of ways— not an eagle, not an angel, just a sparrow, doing what sparrows do, which is more than enough.
