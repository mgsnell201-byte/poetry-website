The Correspondence

Every day someone is born and every day someone dies, and somewhere between those two events a star collapses into itself, or flares, or simply goes dark. I don't know if there is a correspondence— if your grandmother's last breath coincided with the dimming of some distant light, or if the baby crying in the hospital arrived as a new sun ignited. But I like to think of it this way: that we are all burning, each of us a small fire fed by the same elements that feed the stars— hydrogen, oxygen, the will to shine. And when we go out, we go out the way stars do—not into nothing, but into something else, our light traveling outward, still moving through the dark. This morning I watched a sparrow land on the feeder, then fly away, and I thought: this is what we do, we arrive, we eat, we leave, and the world goes on without us. But also with us—because the light we made is still traveling, still reaching toward whatever comes next, still saying: I was here, I burned, I belonged to the family of things.
