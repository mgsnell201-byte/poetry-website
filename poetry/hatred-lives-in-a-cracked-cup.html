Hatred Lives in a Cracked Cup

I found it this morning on the kitchen counter, the cup with the hairline fracture running from rim to base, the one I should have thrown out months ago but kept using anyway. You know the kind—it seeps. Not dramatically, not all at once, just a slow dark stain spreading across whatever surface you set it on. That's where hatred lives, in ordinary vessels compromised by the smallest break. It doesn't announce itself with trumpets or manifestos. It pools quietly beneath the morning coffee, beneath the herbal tea you drink for your nerves, beneath the water you offer a guest who doesn't notice the cup is damaged. And you keep filling it, don't you? Even though you know. Even though every time you lift it to your lips, you can feel the rough edge where the porcelain split, the place where the crack wants to widen. Hatred doesn't need much room. Just that initial fissure, that first allowance. Then it moves through the clay like infection, patient as geology, turning the whole thing brittle, making everything it holds bitter. I suppose you could say throw it away, get a new cup, start fresh. But we both know it doesn't work like that. The cabinet is full of cracked cups. We've been drinking from them so long we've learned to ignore the taste, the way our fingers come away damp with what we pretended wasn't leaking. Don't tell me about repair. Don't mention glue. What's broken stays broken, and what seeps through will find the table, then the floor, then the foundation. One day you'll wonder why the whole house smells like poison. I'm telling you now: it started with the cup.
