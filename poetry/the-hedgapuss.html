The Hedgapuss

I was sitting at the kitchen table as I often do in the early morning, with the dictionary open to the H's, a cup of coffee cooling beside me, when I discovered that a hodgepodge is not, as I had long believed, a hedgehog and a platypus. This was disappointing. I had carried the idea for years the way you carry a coin in your pocket without remembering which country it came from— the two animals fused in my mind into a single, implausible creature, quilled on one half, billed on the other, waddling through the underbrush of the English language looking for a place to rest. And it turns out the word is only Middle English for a stew, a corruption of "hochepot", which the French used for whatever you threw in the pot when you had nothing particular in mind. But I prefer my version— the hedgehog contributing its spines and its talent for curling into a ball at the first sign of trouble, the platypus offering that duck bill, those venomous ankles, and the quiet confidence of an animal that looked at evolution and said no thank you, I'll stay. Between them they'd make a word that means exactly what it means: a gathering of things that have no business being together but somehow are, like the contents of a junk drawer, like the crowd at a Wednesday matinee, like this moment at the table where the coffee has gone cold and the dictionary lies open to a page I am no longer reading, and outside the window a sparrow is doing something ordinary with a twig that I will watch until it flies.
