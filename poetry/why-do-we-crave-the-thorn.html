Why Do We Crave the Thorn?

We reach for roses—knowing well how thorns will prick and petals fall. Yet still we cup the bleeding palm, and call the wound divine. What gravity, what hidden law compels the heart to break itself against the cliffs of almost love, yet whisper "once more"? Is pain the tax on ecstasy? The shadow where the light must dance? For even ghosts of love outshine the safest, barren air. We drink the ache like vintage wine— each sip a sweeter suffering. The hands that bruise us feel like home, the chaos, sacred art. Perhaps the heart is not designed for peace, but pilgrimage— each scar a holy testament: "I burned, I wept, I dared." Love’s blade carves hollows in the chest, then fills them up with stars. What’s hollowed out will always hold more light than solid stone. So let the thorns stay in the stem, let every kiss risk ruin. The soul that never splits with grief has never held the sun.
