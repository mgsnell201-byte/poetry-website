The Congregation of the Bar

A rabbi, a priest, and a pastor one night Walked into a bar under dim amber light, And perched on the stools, already at ease, Sat a rat, a chimp, a dolphin, and a parrot — swapping keys To jokes so old the bartender just groaned, But the animals roared like the punchline was owned. The rabbi sat down and said, "Gentlemen, please — Explain to me why beasts are laughing with such ease." The rat merely grinned and adjusted his tie: "We've heard every sermon you've ever preached — twice — and still don't know why." The priest, mildly wounded, turned up his collar, "We speak of forgiveness," he said, "not for scholars—" The chimp interrupted with a bark and a hoot, "Forgiveness is easy, when you're throwing no fruit!" The pastor leaned in with his warmest church-smile, "Faith isn't a laughing matter — not for a while." But the dolphin just clicked, spinning circles with glee, "Everything's funnier under the sea!" Then the parrot, who'd silently soaked in each word, Repeated the punchlines — reversed and absurd: "A bar walks into a rabbi! A priest meets a joke!" And the whole room dissolved into laughter and smoke. The three holy men blinked, then started to chuckle, Their solemn faces starting to buckle, For somewhere between scripture and animal noise, They remembered that laughter's the oldest of joys. So if ever you wonder what heaven finds funny, Ask a rat, a chimp, a dolphin — spare no money — Or a parrot who's mastered the art of the tale: The punchline's not sacred. It's just the wag of a tail.
